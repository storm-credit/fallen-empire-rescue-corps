# CONFIG A — MID-30s MALE / MOTHER / YOUNGER SISTER / FORMER PARTNER

## Protagonist
- male, 35–37
- never married or long-term relationship ended several years ago
- mid-level field transition specialist
- highly competent, emotionally evasive through work

## Parent anchor
- mother, early-to-mid 60s
- rooted in H8 Legacy Habitat
- ordinary civilian profession/community role
- strongly place-attached but not anti-technology

## Younger protected person
- younger sister, 23–27
- adult, fully able to judge protagonist's past intervention
- currently pursuing a career/life that can move her away from family orbit

## Intimate relation
- former long-term partner, similar age
- profession intersects personhood/medicine/community advocacy but does not duplicate protagonist

## BH-1
- woman, early 50s
- partnered, one adult/near-adult seeded child
- community food/fermentation cooperative manager

### Strengths
- very legible family network
- protagonist can have both “son” and “older brother” blind spots
- younger sister autonomy issue is immediately understandable
- BH-1 family is distinct from protagonist family
- former relationship supports mature romance rather than first-love plot
- male protagonist may reduce entry friction for some Korean male-oriented web-novel readers without changing thematic depth

### Risks
- potentially conventional family configuration
- must avoid “older brother knows best” becoming sympathetic by default

# 2. Recommendation

## Preferred: CONFIG A

Working core:

- protagonist: male, 35–37
- mother: early/mid 60s, H8 community anchor
- younger sister: mid-20s, adult autonomy arc
- former serious partner: protagonist-age peer with independent profession
- BH-1: unrelated woman in early 50s with seeded partner/child

### Why A wins

It creates **four distinct relational directions** around protagonist:

1. upward/generational — mother
2. downward/protective — younger sister
3. peer/intimate — former partner
4. peer/professional — colleague/rival/mentor

BH-1 then sits completely outside protagonist's family and romantic destiny.

This helps the novel make its strongest point:

> the sole biological human is not important because the protagonist owns them through blood, love, or fate.

# 3. Protagonist Working Psychological Design under A

## Age
36 is the current strongest working age.

Why:
- enough years to be credible as an experienced specialist
- young enough for long career/future decisions
- believable living parent and adult younger sibling
- mature relationship history possible

## Public strength
Sees invisible dependency chains in communities.

## Private coping style
Turns emotional uncertainty into a solvable system.

## False belief
> If every dependency is correctly modeled, I can make a fair transition.

## Hidden contradiction
He once falsified a classification because he knew the correct record would produce an intolerable human outcome.

## Fear
Being the person who destroys a life while being able to prove he followed procedure.

## Desire
Become trusted with the ship's hardest transitions.

## Need
Learn that:
- systems must recognize people
- people cannot be reduced to systems
- consent can invalidate even benevolent optimization
- physical limits remain real even when categories are unjust

# 4. Character Differentiation Rules

## Mother
Must not be:
- gentle wisdom dispenser
- secret historian
- always correct about home

Give her:
- local power/influence
- selfish attachment
- one decision that burdens others
- humor/social life outside son

## Younger sister
Must not be:
- perpetual victim
- protagonist's morality pet

Give her:
- independent competence
- political/social position protagonist dislikes
- ability to refuse his repair attempt

## Former partner
Must not be:
- exposition lawyer/doctor only
- waiting romantically for protagonist

Give them:
- a life that improved in protagonist's absence
- their own professional/faction conflict
- at least one reason not to resume relationship even after trust returns

## BH-1
Must not be:
- protagonist surrogate mother
- mystical older woman
- perfect ordinary-person moral oracle

Give her:
- stubbornness
- bad judgment in one practical issue
- local self-interest
- professional competence
- a future choice protagonist does not endorse
