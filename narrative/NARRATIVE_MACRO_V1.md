# Narrative Macro Architecture v1

Status: **PROVISIONAL MACRO — NOT EPISODE BLUEPRINT / NOT MANUSCRIPT**
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》
Target core length: **250 episodes**
Optional extension: 251–300 only if post-arrival story earns a new engine; no padding.

---

# 0. Macro Design Thesis

The series must not run on one withheld twist.

The macro therefore changes its dominant question several times:

1. **Why do records fail to describe real lives?**
2. **Why does an obsolete biological category say there is only one?**
3. **Then what are the other 299,999 people?**
4. **How did human descendants become artificial-origin without becoming fake lives?**
5. **Who is the sole baseline human, and who owns that fact?**
6. **Who inherits a mission written for a different kind of humanity?**
7. **What must be preserved, what may end, and who gets to decide?**

Every answer changes law, relationships, resource allocation, and the protagonist's work.

The planned ending is not a factual answer alone. It is a civilization-scale transition:

> truthful provenance without civic hierarchy + rewritten plural settlement.

| Act | Episodes | Dominant Story Mode | Main Question | Permanent Change |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| ACT I — The Life That Doesn't Fit the Record | 1–20 | professional/social mystery | Why do classifications fail? Why is baseline count 1? | protagonist crosses from ordinary transition work into restricted provenance problem |

# 2. ACT I — THE LIFE THAT DOESN'T FIT THE RECORD
## EP001–020

### Act purpose

Prove the story works as a web novel before spending the headline twist.

The protagonist is introduced through competence, not investigation.

They are a **Habitat Transition Officer** responsible for continuity during forced physical change: households, schools, clinics, jobs, transit, caregiving networks, and community dependencies.

The first twenty episodes must provide:

- immediate professional wins/losses
- family conflict independent of ontology
- at least one material-system pressure
- a concrete historical anomaly
- confirmation of the title premise by the end of the Act

### Protagonist opening belief

> If records are accurate and procedure is fair, a transition can preserve what matters.

### Act-ending belief damage

> The records may be internally consistent and still describe the wrong kind of reality.

## SUB-ACT I-A — Equivalent Homes Are Not Equivalent Lives
### EP001–005

Primary engines:
- E1 Habitat Transition
- E4 Relationship

Secondary:
- E3 Habitat Culture

External event:
A seemingly routine relocation/consolidation project is about to split a community dependency that the official household model does not record.

Episode-scale goals:
- EP001 must open with protagonist making a consequential professional decision.
- Show the ship as lived geography, not metal corridors.
- Introduce closest operational colleague.
- Introduce parent/home conflict in miniature.
- Seed protagonist's old falsification through behavior/avoidance, not exposition.

Payoff by EP005:
Protagonist discovers a hidden caregiving/social dependency and redesigns part of the transition.

Cost:
The redesign consumes a scarce scheduling/material concession and creates an institutional/political loser.

Mystery level:
Low. At most one anomaly connected to an obsolete population field appears in otherwise mundane records.

Reader promise:
This protagonist is good at solving human-scale problems inside a giant closed world.

## SUB-ACT I-C — One
### EP013–020

Primary:
- E6 Historical/Infrastructure Archaeology

Secondary:
- E5 Material Constraint
- E1 Transition

External event:
A legacy infrastructure handoff cannot be completed because an obsolete biological-development field remains referenced by a protected safety protocol.

The protagonist does not investigate out of curiosity. The project physically cannot close until the dependency is understood.

Clue families:
1. infrastructure/protocol
2. medical classification terminology
3. archival/legal remnant

False interpretation:
The field may refer to a medical risk class, founder-status token, or corrupted registry residue.

Major factual payoff:
By EP018–020, independent verification establishes:

> Current baseline-development biological-human count aboard the ship = **1**.

This is the **title-premise confirmation**, not the final mystery.

What is NOT revealed yet:
- who the one person is
- whether protagonist is the one
- exactly what the other 299,999 are
- how history reached this state

Act climax consequence:
Access to the category becomes restricted because it touches medical/privacy/infrastructure dependencies.

Relationship consequence:
The protagonist can no longer treat their own family records as emotionally neutral data.

Act-end new question:
> If one person is baseline human, what exactly are the rest of us?

# 3. ACT II — WHAT ARE WE, THEN?
## EP021–055

### Act purpose

Resolve the largest credibility question early enough that the premise deepens rather than stalls:

- births are real
- bodies are real
- childhood is real
- artificial origin does not mean downloaded adult NPC

The Act also proves death/restoration stakes.

### Protagonist belief entering

> Maybe the category is technically strange, but my life is still obviously human.

### Belief leaving

> The mechanism that made me may be different from what I believed; that does not tell me what my life is worth.

### EP021–032

Primary:
- E2 Personhood Claim

Secondary:
- E3 Habitat Culture
- E4 Relationship

External event:
A reproductive/medical continuity case collides with the newly discovered baseline field.

Purpose:
Reveal cognition-seeding technology through an actual family decision rather than textbook exposition.

Factual payoff:
- standard pregnancies are genuine pregnancies
- fetal/neural development is genuine development
- cognition seed is not an adult personality upload
- present citizens know the technology as ordinary developmental medicine

New distinction:
The hidden issue is not whether the technology exists, but **what historical threshold it crossed**.

Relationship movement:
Parent/family responds to protagonist's growing fixation on origin with irritation rather than secret knowledge.

New question:
At what point did developmental support become artificial-origin cognition?

## SUB-ACT II-B — Death Does Not Become Cheap
### EP033–043

Primary:
- E2 Continuity Claim

Secondary:
- E5 Material Constraint
- E4 Relationship

External event:
An emotionally important restoration-continuity case affects housing, inheritance, partnership, or professional status during a transition project.

Purpose:
Destroy the easy reader objection: “If they are AI, restore them.”

Factual payoff:
- developmental seed cannot recreate an adult person
- high-resolution continuity capture is separate, costly, incomplete, and consent-bound
- restored continuity (RC) is a claimant/person, not automatically uncontested legal identity

Material cost:
Restoration capacity competes with other closed-system resources.

Emotional payoff:
A relationship must choose how to treat an RC without metaphysics solving itself.

New question:
If origin does not make lives cheap, why did society erase the distinction?

## SUB-ACT II-C — The Protagonist's Own File
### EP044–055

Primary:
- E4 Relationship Obligation

Secondary:
- E9 Information Cascade
- E6 History

External event:
A leak/rumor from the early baseline discovery begins circulating distorted interpretations.

Protagonist action:
Attempts to establish their own developmental provenance through lawful channels.

Obstacle:
Privacy architecture and post-Settlement category removal mean there is no simple “AI/Human” field to query.

Important rule:
No repeated fake-out of “maybe protagonist is the one.”

Relationship turn:
Closest intimate or family relationship reacts to the protagonist treating shared memories as suspect.

Act-end question:
> If seeded development is ordinary now, when and why did it stop being considered a different origin?

# 4. ACT III — THE HISTORY WE INHERITED
## EP056–095

### Act purpose

Move from identity mystery to civilizational archaeology.

Reveal that:
- artificial-origin people were created by humans, but not as replacement robots
- baseline and seeded people coexisted in real mixed families
- the transition involved care, fear, discrimination, law, and survival
- the Continuity Settlement was both humane and epistemically destructive

By the end of the Act the core artificial-origin fact becomes **shipwide social reality**.

Primary:
- E6 History

- E1 Transition
- E3 Culture

Present-tense event:
A transition project in an old habitat uncovers physical/legal remnants of early mixed-development family infrastructure.

Historical payoff:
Original biological humans and seeded-origin descendants lived together for generations.

Recontextualization:

False interpretation defeated:
“AI killed/replaced humanity in one event.”

## SUB-ACT III-B — The Settlement That Saved People by Erasing a Category
### EP069–082

Primary:
- E7 Faction Move

Secondary:
- E6 History
- E9 Information Cascade

External event:
Provenance activists/archivists release authentic fragments that imply deliberate destruction of old origin registries.

Public assumption:
The state covered up a crime.

Deeper payoff:
The Continuity Settlement dismantled provenance registries because the categories had become tools for caste, custody separation, reproductive control, employment discrimination, and restoration-right stratification.

Moral complexity:
The reform materially saved real families and citizens.

Cost:
It also destroyed technical/historical knowledge later generations needed.

Protagonist relationship:
Mentor makes a defensible decision to delay one release because an ongoing relocation/medical operation could collapse under panic or targeted harassment.

Protagonist does not fully agree.

New question:
Can truth be restored without rebuilding the old hierarchy?

## SUB-ACT III-C — Everybody Learns the Wrong Thing First
### EP083–095

Primary:
- E9 Information Cascade

Secondary:
- E7 Faction Move
- E2 Personhood Claim

External event:
Enough evidence escapes that “almost everyone is artificial-origin” becomes impossible to contain, but public interpretation fragments.

Required public reactions:
- some insist nothing changed
- some experience identity collapse
- some embrace provenance pride
- some demand baseline priority
- some demand abolition of human language
- some focus on privacy
- some exploit fear for resource/political goals

No single Human-vs-AI split.

Major factual payoff:
Artificial-origin developmental history becomes broadly confirmed.

Protagonist payoff:
Protagonist accepts they are overwhelmingly likely seeded-origin and continues acting rather than suspending life for identity certainty.

Act-ending permanent social change:
Ship law, religion, education, medicine, and family policy must now operate under a truth their categories were not designed to discuss.

Act-end question:
> If the one baseline human is a living citizen, who is allowed to know who they are?

# 5. ACT IV — THE ONE PERSON
## EP096–140

### Act purpose

Turn the strongest “who is the human?” guessing-game material into richer post-reveal drama.

The sole baseline human should be identified with substantial series remaining.

Working identity:
**an unrelated ordinary adult citizen**, not protagonist, ruler, scientist, hidden heir, or savior.

## SUB-ACT IV-A — The Search Is Already a Violation
### EP096–108

Primary:
- E2 Personhood/Privacy Claim

Secondary:
- E9 Information Cascade
- E7 Faction Move

External event:
Political, medical, religious, and media actors begin trying to infer the sole person's identity from partial data.

Protagonist role:
Not “find the human first.”

Instead the protagonist is involved because relocation/medical access data becomes a route for deanonymization.

Core dilemma:
Can society answer a historically important question without converting one citizen's body into public property?

Payoff:
A strong privacy safeguard is won temporarily, at real informational/political cost.

New question:
Can the identity remain private once physical needs or old protocol interactions expose it indirectly?

## SUB-ACT IV-B — The Ordinary Person
### EP109–122

Primary:
- E4 Relationship / Personhood pressure around BH-1

Secondary:
- E2 Claim
- E3 Culture

Major reveal:
BH-1 identity is confirmed.

Design requirement:
Before full public revelation, BH-1 must already have been presented as a person with:
- job
- family/relationships
- habits
- dislikes
- obligations
- personal plans

The reveal changes society's view of them, not the reader's proof that they mattered.

Factual payoff:
BH-1 emerged through a rare modern **unseeded-development contingency pathway** preserved as reproductive/medical redundancy.

Important inversion:
No secret program was maintaining “one human.” The current count is a rare demographic outcome.

Conflict:
Research access, reproductive pressure, charter symbolism, security, and privacy converge.

- E8 Arrival Decision

- E6 History
- E7 Faction

External event:
Updated destination observations force activation of old settlement procedures.

Some charter language assumes baseline-human bodies, institutions, or representational categories.

## SUB-ACT V-C — Sol Does Not Answer
### EP169–178

Primary:
- E6 History / E9 Information

- E8 Arrival

The series explains why a nearby-star voyage did not simply receive an authoritative answer from home.

- the centralized mission authority later dissolved/fragmented
- dedicated verified channel continuity ended

# 7. ACT VI — WHAT MUST END
## EP191–225

### Act purpose

Force the series' philosophy through irreversible physical loss.

This is where the protagonist's profession, family conflict, ship geography, material scarcity, restoration debate, and destination all converge.

---

## SUB-ACT VI-A — The Beloved Place
### EP191–202

Primary:
- E1 Habitat Transition

Secondary:
- E4 Parent/Home
- E3 Culture

External event:
A culturally beloved district/habitat element must be dismantled, merged, or transformed because preserving it intact would endanger arrival viability or another essential system.

Do not solve with hidden spare capacity.

The protagonist now understands that functional replacement is insufficient.

Parent's correction:
The parent must also accept that total preservation can impose costs on people elsewhere.

Outcome:
Some physical place genuinely ends.

Continuity is preserved through selected relationships/institutions/rituals chosen with the community, not a perfect replica.

Motif payoff:
Closed-system material from the ended place enters future settlement construction.

## SUB-ACT VI-C — Transition Authority Crisis
### EP214–225

Primary:
- E7 Faction

Secondary:
- E5 Material Constraint
- E8 Arrival

External event:
An arrival-preparation failure, deadline, or cascading infrastructure conflict makes existing governance unable to coordinate incompatible habitat plans.

# 8. ACT VII — MORE THAN ONE WORLD
## EP226–250

### Act purpose

Pay the series in action, institution, relationship, and physical transformation.

The climax is not one speech and not one fight.

It is a coordinated civilization transition under a hard material deadline in which different groups must make binding choices.

---

## SUB-ACT VII-A — No Single Plan Can Save Everything
### EP226–235

Primary:
- E8 Arrival

Secondary:
- E1 Transition
- E7 Faction

External pressure:
Final conversion/orbital/landing preparation window closes.

Competing plans:
- total founder-plan settlement
- maximal ship preservation
- provenance-weighted mission authority
- local autonomy without shared burden
- protagonist's emerging plural-transition framework

The preferred framework must have visible costs and losers.

BH-1 payoff:
Their body/status cannot be used as automatic constitutional authority.
Their medical/privacy/reproductive rights remain intact even under final pressure.

Protagonist secret payoff:
They refuse to repeat their old pattern of “protecting people by deciding for them.”

## SUB-ACT VII-B — The Transition
### EP236–245

Primary:
- E1 Civilization-scale Transition

Secondary:
- E5 Material Constraint
- E4 Relationships

Action form:
Parallel but causally linked operations:
- preserve selected ship habitats
- dismantle/convert others
- allocate settlement industry
- maintain life-support during reconfiguration
- execute chosen community transition paths
- handle last-minute material failures without undoing consent framework

This provides external/action pressure without turning the story into war fiction.

Required payoff:
Material from one ended/preserved-in-memory place becomes literal infrastructure for a new settlement.

Relationship resolutions occur through decisions and presence, not philosophical monologues.

---

## SUB-ACT VII-C — Provenance Without Rank
### EP246–250

Primary:
- E2 Civic Settlement / E4 Emotional payoff

Secondary:
- E8 Arrival

Institutional outcomes:

### Personhood
Universal civic status grounded in agency, rights, obligations, and lived continuity.

### Provenance
Baseline-development, seeded-development, restored continuity, and other medically/history-relevant descriptors may be recorded with strong consent/privacy protections.

They do not rank citizenship.

### Human identity
No single state-enforced hierarchy of “how human” someone is.

### Reproductive capability
Baseline-development capacity remains available as voluntary reproductive/medical capability, not a duty.

### Destination
Some communities settle planetary environments; some remain shipborne/orbital; the ship becomes one founding world among several.

### Sol
No answer arrives in time to authorize the choice.

Potential epilogue-level future:
The new civilization may later attempt renewed contact, but outside approval cannot retroactively legitimize what they chose.

Final protagonist state:
Not ruler.
Not chosen human.
Not savior.

A person who became capable of designing transitions where:
- difference can be truthful
- dignity is not ranked
- communities have agency
- material limits are acknowledged
- continuity can survive some endings

# 11. Social Scale Escalation

The series must not simply become “bigger explosions.”

Scale progression:

`household`
→ `building / neighborhood`
→ `institution`
→ `habitat`
→ `cross-habitat coalition`
→ `shipwide legal order`
→ `civilization-scale settlement transition`

Meaning progression:

`does this record fit this family?`
→ `what makes this person continuous?`
→ `what made this civilization?`
→ `who owns a body's provenance?`
→ `who owes what to an inherited mission?`
→ `what can end without erasing us?`

---

# 12. Web-Novel Reward Rhythm Requirements

This macro is invalid if episode blueprints later turn it into delayed exposition.

Mandatory cadence targets:

### Every 1–3 episodes
At least one:
- competence success/failure with visible result
- relationship movement
- concrete information answer
- physical/system change
- binding choice

### Every 5–15 episodes
At least one:
- Arc resolution
- precedent / political consequence
- completed transition
- relationship state change
- significant factual payoff

### Every Act
Must permanently change at least three of:
- protagonist belief
- relationship state
- law/institution
- public knowledge
- physical ship configuration
- faction map
- destination commitment

---

# 13. 300-Episode Extension Rule

Do **not** automatically expand to 300.

Episodes 251–300 are justified only if, during blueprinting, a genuinely new post-arrival engine appears that cannot be paid inside Act VII without compression.

Acceptable extension candidates:
- first functioning plural settlement creates a new, non-repetitive institutional crisis
- preserved ship habitats and planetary settlements diverge enough to generate a new central decision
- recontact with Sol becomes a separate sequel-scale premise

Unacceptable reasons:
- more mystery withholding
- additional sole-human fake-outs
- extra habitat tourism
- repeating arrival votes
- saving relationship payoffs for later

Current recommendation:

**Build a complete, satisfying 250-episode story first. Treat 300 as optional expansion, not target quota.**
