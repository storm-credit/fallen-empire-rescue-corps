# Habitat Revisit Map v1

Status: PROVISIONAL MACRO COVERAGE MAP
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Purpose

The eight habitats and axial world must feel like recurring parts of one civilization, not episodic planets.

Every major habitat must receive at minimum:

1. **Introduction** — reader learns how people actually live there.
2. **Revisit under changed knowledge** — same place means something different after a reveal/policy shift.
3. **Cross-habitat consequence** — its decisions affect somewhere else.
4. **Late-series fate/choice** — settlement/ship-preservation decision is visible.

No habitat owns only one genre.

# 3. Act I Geography

## Dominant worlds
- H8 Legacy Habitat
- H4 Kinship Belt
- H1 Civic Core
- Axis

### Why
Act I needs:
- lived home/community
- family structure
- protagonist's professional institution
- transit/material reality

### H8 first meaning
Old, layered, inconvenient, emotionally dense place whose residents resist “equivalent” relocation.

### H4 first meaning
Family/care infrastructure shows why legal households cannot capture all dependencies.

### H1 first meaning
Procedure, law, and standardized records appear useful and competent—not sinister.

### Axis first meaning
Protagonist and colleague experience physical ship logistics; travel has cost.

### Cross-habitat consequence
A concession made to preserve H8/H4 continuity consumes fabrication/transit resources elsewhere.

# 4. Act II Geography

## Dominant worlds
- H4 Kinship Belt
- H2 Biosphere Commons
- H6 Memory Commons
- H3 Fabrication Concord

### H4 changed function
Not just family/home. It becomes reproductive medicine and child-development law.

### H2 function
Birth/death/ecological reciprocity provides a culture that sees continuity differently from record-centric H1/H6.

### H6 function
Restoration and memory-authentication conflicts; archive culture proves incomplete and socially biased.

### H3 function
Embodied repair/replacement culture complicates simplistic “original body = real person” thinking.

### Consequence
A restoration/personhood ruling in one habitat affects housing or inheritance allocation in another.

# 5. Act III Geography

## Dominant worlds
- H8 Legacy Habitat
- H6 Memory Commons
- H7 Low-Link Communities
- H5 Exchange Ring
- H1 Civic Core

### H8 changed meaning
Physical traces of early mixed-origin families turn “heritage” into contested evidence.

### H6 changed meaning
Archives are politically meaningful but do not contain a clean master answer.

### H7 function
Oral/community memory preserves facts absent from central records—but also preserves distortions.

### H5 function
Rumor, mobility, temporary housing, labor, and trade make public revelation propagate unevenly.

### H1 function
Public/institutional response to the shipwide artificial-origin truth.

### Cross-habitat consequence
A provenance release from H6 changes family safety in H4 and employment/resource politics in H3/H5.

# 6. Act IV Geography

## Dominant worlds
- H7 Low-Link
- H4 Kinship Belt
- H5 Exchange Ring
- H2 Biosphere Commons
- H1 Civic Core

### Why these worlds
The “sole human” phase is about privacy, medicine, ordinary life, kinship, and public deanonymization—not a hidden laboratory.

### H7 changed function
Privacy infrastructure and refusal culture become practically valuable, while also creating blind spots.

### H5 changed function
Movement/identity-verification data creates deanonymization risk.

### H4 changed function
Reproductive pressure and family claims around BH-1.

### H2 changed function
Biological difference matters medically/ecologically without implying moral supremacy.

### H1 changed function
Law has to protect a person whose category has civilization-scale symbolic value.

### Cross-habitat consequence
Attempts to protect BH-1 in one jurisdiction produce resource/security burdens elsewhere.

# 8. Act VI Geography

## Dominant worlds
- H8 Legacy Habitat
- H2 Biosphere Commons
- H3 Fabrication Concord
- H7 Low-Link
- Axis

### H8 payoff candidate
A beloved physical place associated with protagonist/home history undergoes irreversible transformation.

Do not destroy the entire habitat merely for melodrama. The loss should be specific enough to be emotionally legible and materially necessary.

### H2 changed function
Provides living ecological stock/material and challenges what “preserving life” means when some biosphere must be moved or split.

### H7 changed function
Right-to-refuse recording/restoration becomes politically difficult under rare-skill/arrival pressure.

### H3/Axis changed function
Physical transition work makes abstract votes irreversible.

### Consequence
Material from an ended structure is tracked into future settlement infrastructure.

# 9. Act VII Final Geography

The ending must not homogenize the ship.

## Working plural outcomes

### H1 Civic Core
Part remains orbital/shipborne; central authority becomes one node in a compact, not unquestioned capital.

### H2 Biosphere Commons
Splits function: some ecological capacity supports planetary settlement, some remains orbital as redundancy/cultural world.

### H3 Fabrication Concord
Significant industrial mass transforms into settlement infrastructure; a smaller orbital industrial base remains.

### H4 Kinship Belt
Different neighborhoods/family networks choose different transitions; no single “family habitat” destiny.

### H5 Exchange Ring
Becomes transport/migration connector among orbital and planetary settlements.

### H6 Memory Commons
Preserves provenance history under new consent/access law and distributes archive redundancy among new worlds.

### H7 Low-Link Communities
At least one community chooses a lower-record future and wins legal recognition without exemption from shared physical obligations.

### H8 Legacy Habitat
Some heritage survives, some does not. At least one beloved piece has already become something else.

### Axis
Remains critical orbital backbone and a literal reminder that the old ship became infrastructure for multiple futures.

# 10. Anti-Theme-Park Rules

Forbidden patterns:

- H6 = every secret
- H2 = every ecology crisis
- H3 = every labor/resource conflict
- H4 = every family story
- H7 = every privacy debate
- H8 = every historical clue

For each habitat's second major appearance, change at least two of:

- dominant engine
- conflict family
- emotional temperature
- social scale
- primary relationship
- mystery intensity
