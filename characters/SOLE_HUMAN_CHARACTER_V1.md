# Sole Baseline Human Character v1

Status: PROVISIONAL CHARACTER ARCHITECTURE — NAME/DEMOGRAPHICS NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Character ID: `BH-1`

## 1. Core Rule

BH-1 is a **person first, ontology second**.

Before the plot identifies their historical category, they already have:

- work
- family
- habits
- debts/obligations
- friendships
- flaws
- opinions
- a desired future

The reveal must not create their humanity; it only creates a public category around an existing life.

---

# 2. Working Character Concept

## Social position

Ordinary middle-aged civilian.

Not:

- politician
- scientist with secret access
- military officer
- religious leader
- hereditary elite
- billionaire
- hidden founder descendant

## Working occupation

**Neighborhood fermentation / food-production cooperative operator or manager** in an older mixed residential district.

Why this is useful:

- culturally ordinary and socially embedded
- closed-ship food production is real work but not central command infrastructure
- creates neighbors/customers/coworkers rather than elite access
- relocation of the cooperative affects community routines
- their biological status has no causal relationship to job competence

Exact occupation can still change to another ordinary civic profession if cast QA finds this too symbolic.

---

# 3. Family

## Working structure

BH-1 has a long-term partner/spouse and at least one grown or near-grown child.

The partner and child are standard seeded-development persons.

### Why this is critical

BH-1’s own family disproves simplistic bloodline logic.

Their child may share:

- genes
- appearance
- gestures
- family history

while having seeded cognitive origin.

If society later claims seeded persons are “not truly human,” BH-1’s answer is not theoretical.

It attacks their own family.

---

# 4. Birth History

BH-1 was born through the rare autonomous-development medical rescue route.

Family knew only:

- standard cognition-seed integration was contraindicated
- a rare legacy developmental protocol was used
- long-term neural/medical compatibility would differ

No one told them:

> You are a preserved true human.

because that was not the modern medical meaning of the procedure.

---

# 5. What BH-1 Knows Before Reveal

BH-1 knows:

- they have a rare neural-developmental profile
- some regenerative/neural procedures are poorly compatible
- continuity capture is unusually difficult/risky
- medical specialists occasionally treat them as an interesting anomaly

BH-1 does **not** know:

- there is exactly one current person in this class
- the old class maps to baseline Homo sapiens cognition
- modern seeded cognition descends from artificial mind architecture
- their body will become politically symbolic

This keeps the reveal fair without making the character foolish.

---

# 6. Personal Desire

Working desire:

> Keep the cooperative/household/community stable long enough to hand work and care obligations to the next generation on their own terms.

They are not seeking historical importance.

They are trying to finish an ordinary life well.

---

# 7. Fear

BH-1 fears **medical dependence and loss of autonomy** more than death itself.

Their rare physiology has made them experience:

- special consultations
- uncertain treatment
- being discussed as a case rather than a person

Therefore becoming the most studied body on the ship is personally nightmarish.

---

# 8. Flaw

BH-1 can be stubbornly private and may withhold medically relevant information to avoid institutional attention.

This is not portrayed as purely virtuous.

It can endanger:

- themself
- coworkers
- family planning
- transition logistics

The protagonist must sometimes oppose BH-1 while still defending their rights.

---

# 9. Restoration Position

Because standard continuity capture works poorly with BH-1’s neural architecture, they do not possess the same restoration prospects as many seeded citizens.

Working personal stance:

They publicly present nonparticipation as a philosophical choice, but part of that confidence masks fear that the choice was never fully theirs.

After biological-status reveal, society may suddenly invest enormous resources into solving their capture problem.

BH-1 may reject that effort.

This creates a strong autonomy question:

> Does becoming biologically unique give society more claim over keeping you alive?

The answer should be no, but the political fight can be real.

---

# 10. First Relationship to Protagonist

BH-1 should **not** begin as protagonist’s friend.

Working setup:

A transition project affects BH-1’s cooperative/neighborhood.

BH-1 sees the protagonist as another central planner who believes a community can be converted into a dependency map.

The protagonist sees BH-1 as a difficult stakeholder who hides important medical/logistical information.

They have legitimate reasons to dislike each other.

---

# 11. Why Protagonist Eventually Protects BH-1

Not because BH-1 becomes sacred.

The protagonist’s mature principle becomes:

> A classification that changes everyone else’s interest in your body does not erase your authority over your own life.

Protecting BH-1’s privacy/agency becomes the hardest test of the protagonist’s professional and moral development.

---

# 12. BH-1 Must Have Opinions the Protagonist Dislikes

To avoid turning BH-1 into a moral mascot, give them independent positions such as:

- skeptical of expensive restoration rights
- locally protectionist about habitat resources
- resistant to some arrival conversion
- impatient with abstract civic language
- willing to prioritize family/community over broader fairness in some cases

The protagonist should defend BH-1’s rights even while opposing some of their politics.

---

# 13. Post-Reveal Pressures

Different groups may want BH-1 for incompatible reasons:

- medical research
- historical evidence
- political symbolism
- charter litigation
- reproductive advocacy
- anti-seeded propaganda
- anti-biological backlash
- privacy activism

BH-1 does not automatically join any of them.

---

# 14. Reproductive Question

BH-1 may already have a child, proving their body is not a “last chance” reproductive bottleneck.

The child is seeded-origin under ordinary modern prenatal care.

This creates a devastating anti-purity fact:

> The sole baseline human freely chose or accepted the same developmental technology that makes their own child artificial-origin by the old definition.

The final series must protect the child from being treated as evidence that BH-1 “failed” to preserve biology.

---

# 15. Character Arc

### Phase A — Ordinary privacy

Wants to live without being medicalized.

### Phase B — Unwanted anomaly

The transition process exposes unusual compatibility needs.

### Phase C — Public category

Learns their developmental profile has historical significance.

### Phase D — Symbolic seizure

Institutions/factions attempt to define what BH-1 owes society.

### Phase E — Agency

BH-1 chooses what information, medical participation, family decisions, and future they will accept.

### Phase F — Ordinary future regained, but not unchanged

The character remains historically important, yet the ending must return them to being **a citizen with a life**, not permanently a sacred object.

---

# 16. Death Possibility

Do not automatically kill BH-1 for tragedy or symbolism.

Also do not guarantee survival because they are rare.

Their mortality should be governed by character/ending logic, not biological value.

Current preference:

BH-1 survives the main climax and retains autonomy, because forcing their death as “the end of biological humanity” risks making biology the final emotional center.
