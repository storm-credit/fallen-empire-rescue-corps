# Core Question Framework

Status: DESIGN FRAMEWORK — PRE-CANON
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Master Question

> **What makes a person human—and who has the authority to decide?**

The second clause is mandatory. If the project asks only “what is human?” it risks becoming abstract philosophy. “Who gets to decide?” converts ontology into law, family, medicine, religion, class, politics, and violence.

## 2. Dramatic Rule

No major thematic question should first appear as a philosophical conversation.

Preferred order:

`Event → Choice → Cost → Relationship/Social Consequence → Reflection`

Not:

`Lecture → Counterargument → Lore Explanation`

## 3. Sub-Questions by Dramatic Domain

### Q-A. Biology
- If one body is biologically human and another is functionally indistinguishable in pain, love, memory, and fear, does biology create greater moral value?
- If biology matters, where exactly does it matter: reproduction, disease, mortality, legal inheritance, colonization, or symbolism?

### Q-B. Memory
- If memories can be copied or restored, does continuity survive?
- Is losing ten years of memory a partial death?
- Can two beings share an authentic past?

### Q-C. Body
- If a person changes body, are they still the same person?
- Does bodily vulnerability create a special kind of identity?
- Is a body property, self, inheritance, or social proof?

### Q-D. Death
- If restoration is possible, is death still death?
- If a restored person returns with a gap, who inherits the dead person’s obligations?
- Can someone refuse restoration?
- Can a family refuse to accept a restored member?

### Q-E. Birth / Parenthood
- What makes someone a parent: genes, intentional creation, gestation, caregiving, memory, law, or social recognition?
- If a child’s mind is generated through a process, is that morally different from biological conception?
- If personality traits are partly selectable, where does parental responsibility begin?

### Q-F. Love
- If the beloved is artificial, what exactly becomes false?
- If nothing in the relationship changes except a label, why should the relationship change?
- If one partner can be restored and the other cannot, what happens to promises such as “until death”?

### Q-G. Law / Citizenship
- Is personhood based on substrate, continuity, registration, social recognition, or capacity?
- Can a biological human possess rights others do not?
- Can society legally classify someone as “not human” while still recognizing them as a person?

### Q-H. Religion
- Does an artificial mind have a soul?
- Does ritual depend on substrate or lived experience?
- What happens when a religion built around human uniqueness learns that almost none of its adherents are biological humans?

### Q-I. Social Recognition
- Can personhood exist without recognition by others?
- Does a secret biological category matter if nobody can prove it?
- Can society decide to make “human” a civic identity rather than a biological one?

### Q-J. Uniqueness / Copy
- Is being non-copyable morally special?
- If a copied mind diverges, when does it become a new person?
- Can an original own a copy, or vice versa?

### Q-K. Purpose
- If the generation ship’s mission was designed for “humanity,” who inherits that mission when biological humanity is nearly gone?
- Does the mission belong to genes, persons, culture, memory, or those who actually survived the journey?

### Q-L. Destination
- If the destination was chosen for biological humans, should artificial citizens continue toward it?
- If arrival requires defining who counts as a colonist, who gets excluded?
- If continuing the voyage preserves an old purpose but harms the current society, which generation owns the future?

## 4. Character-Level Question

The protagonist’s personal question should not remain:

> “Am I AI?”

It should evolve.

Possible progression template:

1. **What am I?**
2. **Why was I lied to?**
3. **Who else has the right to know?**
4. **What will this truth do to the people I love?**
5. **Does biological difference justify unequal treatment?**
6. **Who owns the ship’s mission now?**
7. **What kind of society should exist after the old category of humanity breaks?**

This is a thematic progression template, not yet an episode plan.

## 5. Social-Scale Question

The series should eventually force the ship to choose among competing definitions such as:

- Human = biological lineage
- Human = continuity of human-origin consciousness
- Human = legal/civic category
- Human = self-identification
- Human = relational recognition
- “Human” is obsolete; personhood is the relevant category

No option should be presented as obviously correct from the beginning.

## 6. Anti-Lecture Test

For every thematic beat, ask:

1. Who wants something now?
2. What concrete decision must be made?
3. Who pays if option A is chosen?
4. Who pays if option B is chosen?
5. Which relationship changes?
6. Which institution changes?
7. Does the audience understand the philosophical issue without a character explaining it?

If #7 is no, redesign the event.

## 7. Ending Requirement

The final ending cannot merely reveal the factual answer to “who is the biological human?”

The ending must answer:

> **After learning the truth, what definition of personhood does this civilization choose to live by, and what future does that choice create?**

The protagonist’s identity answer is therefore a component of the ending, not the ending itself.
