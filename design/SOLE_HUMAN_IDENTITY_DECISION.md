# Sole-Human Identity Decision

Status: **PROVISIONAL DESIGN DECISION — NOT FROZEN UNTIL MACRO QA**
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Decision Question

Should the protagonist ultimately be the one current baseline-development biological human?

This decision affects:

- chosen-one risk
- reader expectation
- protagonist emotional arc
- mystery fairness
- political stakes
- relationship meaning
- ending independence

---

# Option P-H — Protagonist Is the Baseline Human

## Strengths

- simplest commercial promise
- title hook becomes personally immediate
- every medical/political consequence lands on protagonist
- strong paranoia and privacy stakes
- easy first-person emotional identification if used

## Weaknesses

- gravitationally turns protagonist into the most special person in society
- factions naturally orbit the protagonist for biological status rather than earned role
- harder to prove the story means it when it says artificial-origin lives are equally real
- risks chosen-one narrative even without powers
- protagonist’s professional competence can become secondary to what they *are*

## Long-form danger

After identity reveal, every political problem can collapse into:

> “What should we do with the protagonist?”

This narrows the 300,000-person civilization.

### Score

- commercial clarity: 9.5
- thematic originality: 7
- anti-chosen compliance: 5.5
- social-SF breadth: 7
- relationship force: 9
- long-form flexibility: 7.5

---

# Option P-S — Protagonist Is a Standard Seeded Person

## Strengths

- directly tests the core question:
  > If I am artificial-origin, does everything I lived become invalid?
- protagonist remains important because of competence, choices, relationships, and social position
- strongest rejection of chosen-one structure
- allows protagonist to protect the sole human without owning their status
- title mystery can continue after protagonist identity is resolved
- makes artificial-origin citizens emotionally central rather than supporting cast around the “real” person

## Weaknesses

- some readers may initially expect protagonist to be the unique human and feel misled if reveal is handled as cheap gotcha
- protagonist needs strong agency after identity confirmation
- sole human must be interesting enough to matter but not hijack protagonist role

## Long-form advantage

The protagonist can learn “I am not the one” **well before the finale**, then continue changing the world.

That is evidence the series genuinely has engines beyond identity guessing.

### Score

- commercial clarity: 8
- thematic originality: 10
- anti-chosen compliance: 10
- social-SF breadth: 10
- relationship force: 9.5
- long-form flexibility: 10

# 2. Provisional Decision

## **Choose P-S: the protagonist is a standard seeded/artificial-origin person.**

This is the stronger design for this project.

The protagonist’s centrality comes from:

- discovering the cross-system contradiction
- being professionally positioned where classifications become real consequences
- having relationships that embody the cost of reclassification
- choosing how truth is handled
- helping reshape institutions/destination decisions

not from biological uniqueness.

---

# 3. Reader-Promise Protection

The story must not frame the protagonist as definitely the human and then “cheat” later.

Fairness rules:

- protagonist may hope/fear they are the one
- evidence must remain genuinely ambiguous early
- clues supporting seeded origin must exist
- narration cannot state false biological certainty as objective fact
- title refers to the ship, not protagonist

The reveal should feel like:

> “The clues were always there, and this changes what the protagonist thought mattered.”

not:

> “Surprise, the author lied.”

---

# 4. Timing Recommendation

Do **not** preserve protagonist identity uncertainty until the final act.

Working macro target, subject to later Acts:

- early series: protagonist is a plausible candidate
- early/mid series: evidence increasingly contradicts that hope/fear
- around first major civilizational transition of the narrative: protagonist is confirmed seeded-origin
- substantial series remains afterward

This makes the reveal a **character transformation**, not final twist.

---

# 5. Who Is the Sole Human?

## Provisional preference

An **unrelated ordinary adult citizen** with no special political office, command authority, heroic destiny, or prior connection to the protagonist.

### Why adult rather than child

- reduces `Star Child` overlap
- avoids “protect the sacred child” structure
- preserves bodily autonomy as a full adult decision
- permits the person to reject study, publicity, reproduction, or symbolic status

### Why unrelated

- prevents every central mystery from being secretly inside protagonist’s family
- forces protagonist to defend principles for someone they do not own/love initially
- makes biological uniqueness obviously independent of narrative worth

### Why ordinary

The strongest thematic image is:

> The most biologically unique person aboard was already living an ordinary life that did not require uniqueness to have value.

---

# 6. Sole Human Character Function

The sole BH person must become a real character after identification.

They need:

- job
- family/friends
- existing obligations
- flaws
- opinions unrelated to biology
- a desired future
- the right to dislike the protagonist
- the right to refuse public symbolism

They are **not a MacGuffin in human form**.

---

# 7. Political Consequence

Because protagonist is not BH, the protagonist may face the accusation:

> “You are deciding what should happen to the only biological human even though you are not one.”

That accusation has force.

The series then naturally asks:

> Who has standing to decide personhood policy?

This is stronger than letting the uniquely biological protagonist decide their own civilizational importance.

---

# 8. Relationship Payoff

When protagonist is confirmed seeded-origin:

- parent relationship remains real
- intimate relationship cannot be rescued by “at least one of us was human”
- younger family member sees protagonist confront the same classification vulnerability the protagonist once manipulated for them
- mentor’s institutional categories fail to protect protagonist from reclassification
- rival can defend protagonist’s rights while still opposing their politics

This maximizes the project’s emotional thesis.

---

# 9. Ending Compatibility

P-S passes all four ending families:

- Civic Humanity
- Personhood After Humanity
- Plural Continuity
- Rewrite Mission

No ending requires protagonist to biologically represent humanity.

---

# 10. Freeze Condition

Promote P-S from PROVISIONAL to FROZEN only after:

- mystery clue chain proves fair
- protagonist retains agency after reveal
- sole BH ordinary-adult character is designed
- web-novel retention red-team confirms the audience is not being sold a false premise
- final ending works better with P-S than P-H

Current recommendation strength: **HIGH**.
