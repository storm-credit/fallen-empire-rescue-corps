# 1. Core Physical Principle

The eight ~1.5 km-radius rotating habitat worlds do **not** land on Ross 128 b.

They are too large, too massive, and already optimized as orbital living environments.

Planetary settlement uses:
- detachable pressure-rated modules
- refabricated ship structure
- purpose-built landing/ascent craft
- ecological starter systems
- industrial packages

The old ship's late transformation is therefore:

> **one interstellar vehicle becomes a network of orbital worlds, surface settlements, and industrial infrastructure.**

---

# 2. Ending Time Horizon

EP250 does not need to show a century-old planetary civilization.

Preferred epilogue horizon:

**~6–18 months after stable system insertion / first sustained settlement activation.**

By then:
- multiple surface settlements are inhabited
- orbital habitats are in stable operational configurations
- supply chains work
- the constitutional/personhood settlement is in force
- long-term migration remains ongoing

This gives closure without an artificial 30-year time jump.

---

# 3. Population at End — Working Scale

Current ship population near arrival:
~300,000, allowing ordinary demographic variation.

Preferred early post-arrival distribution:

### Surface settlements
**~20,000–30,000 people**

### Planet-associated orbital habitats / stations
**~130,000–160,000**

### Star-centric retained habitats / industrial worlds
**~110,000–140,000**

These overlap only in broad planning terms; exact numbers remain open.

The important point:

> most people do **not** instantly abandon the habitats in which they were born.

Planetary settlement begins as one legitimate future among several.

---

# 4. Why Surface Population Starts Small

The planet is not breathable open air.

Initial surface population is limited by:
- pressure habitat construction
- closed-loop food/ecology commissioning
- water extraction
- local materials qualification
- higher-gravity adaptation
- medical/support redundancy
- ascent/descent capacity

Therefore first settlement is **industrial/ecological city-building**, not pioneers walking into wilderness with tents.

---

# 5. Planetary Orbit Constraint

Ross 128 b's small Hill sphere means planetary infrastructure must stay relatively close.

Working stable large-habitat orbit region:

**tens of thousands of kilometers from planet center**, with detailed stability/inclination assignment later.

No geostationary ring.

Habitat worlds pass over different surface regions rather than hovering above one city.

This helps prevent any one surface settlement from becoming “the capital underneath H1.”

---

# 6. Four Settlement Classes

## Class P — Planetary-orbit worlds

Purpose:
- population stability
- surface logistics
- medical support
- ecological backup
- communications

## Class S — Star-centric shipborn worlds

Purpose:
- preserve shipborn civilization
- heavy industry / archives / autonomy
- avoid overcrowding the planet's compact Hill sphere

## Class G — Ground settlements

Purpose:
- actual planetary habitation
- resource extraction
- ecological experimentation
- new cultural futures

## Class X — Distributed industrial / transfer infrastructure

Purpose:
- axial remnants
- fuel/propulsion systems
- asteroid/resource handling
- cargo transfer
- fabrication

No class is morally higher.

# 7. Habitat Fates

The following are **preferred functional end states**, not exact orbital elements.

---

## H1 — Civic Core

### End state
**Planet-associated orbit**.

### Why
- institutions need high communication access to all settlement nodes
- H1 remains useful as legal/medical/education service center

### What changes
H1 is no longer the unquestioned capital.

Final constitutional framework distributes authority among:
- surface settlements
- orbital habitats
- star-centric habitats

H1 becomes one important node.

### Material sacrifice
Some central administrative/unused formal space is repurposed for multi-settlement services.

---

## H2 — Biosphere Commons

### End state
**Planet-associated orbit**, with major ecological systems retained.

### Why
H2 acts as:
- biological redundancy
- seed/genetic/crop bank
- quarantine/reference ecology
- surface-settlement life-support backup

### What leaves
- selected soil/substrate
- microbial/ecological starter cultures
- water/nutrient stock
- trained ecology teams

### What remains
Enough biosphere capacity that H2 stays a genuine living world rather than being stripped into a warehouse.

---

## H3 — Fabrication Concord

### End state
**Star-centric industrial habitat / partial mass donor**.

### Why
Its industrial architecture is more valuable near large ship/asteroid resource operations than deep inside a compact planetary orbit network.

### Transformation
H3 gives up the largest fraction of nonessential structural/industrial mass among living habitats.

Some cruise-only fabrication lines are dismantled and rebuilt as:
- surface industrial plants
- transfer stations
- orbital yards

### Social result
H3 residents gain political leverage but also bear visible transition costs.

The habitat remains inhabited.

---

## H4 — Kinship Belt

### End state
**Planet-associated orbit**.

### Why
Large family/care networks benefit from not being forcibly dispersed during first-generation settlement risk.

### Population pattern
Some H4 neighborhoods choose surface migration in clusters.
Others remain.

This makes H4 the clearest proof that family continuity does not require one physical future.

---

## H5 — Exchange Ring

### End state
**Planet-associated lower/medium orbit logistics world**.

### Function
Becomes the primary moving interface among:
- surface
- planetary orbit
- star-centric habitats

### Transformation
More transit/cargo space; some old commercial zones change use.

### Risk
Do not turn H5 into a generic spaceport city. It retains resident neighborhoods and cultural life.

---

## H6 — Memory Commons

### End state
**Star-centric habitat with fully distributed archive redundancy**.

### Why
Archives cannot remain controlled by one physical location after the provenance crisis.

### Final archive model
Copies / authenticated shards exist across:
- H6
- H1
- surface settlement institutions
- selected H7/local trustees

H6 remains a living research/cultural world, not civilization's single memory vault.

---

## H7 — Low-Link Communities

### End state
**Star-centric trailing / separated habitat orbit**.

Exact orbital family remains open.

### Why
H7 deliberately chooses greater physical/network autonomy while accepting shared mission obligations.

### Final political meaning
The new compact recognizes the right to lower-record / lower-link civic life.

But H7 cannot refuse shared navigation/radiation/external-risk coordination when its choices physically endanger others.

---

## H8 — Legacy Habitat

### End state
**Retained star-centric habitat world, materially changed**.

The entire cylinder is not sacrificed.

### Major loss
The Old Transfer District is dismantled because launch-era settlement shells beneath it are needed for planetary/orbital infrastructure.

### Result
H8 survives but cannot claim it stayed unchanged.

This is stronger than either:
- total destruction
- perfect preservation

### Symbolic role
H8 becomes proof that a place can remain itself while containing real endings that cannot be reversed.

---

# 8. Axial Spine / Main Industrial Body

The original central ship spine does not remain a single sacred artifact.

Preferred split:

### Retained portion
Becomes a star-centric heavy transfer / fabrication / propulsion-support complex.

### Distributed portions
- cargo handling structures
- settlement industry
- orbital stations
- communication/navigation infrastructure

### Propulsion plant
Large components may become:
- power/industrial sources
- reserve systems
- mothballed heritage/strategic infrastructure

Do not casually reuse every propulsion component as household power; technology/thermal systems differ.

# 9. Surface Settlement A — Temperate Transition City

Design ID: SR-A.

Working ending population:
**~12,000–18,000**.

Primary functions:
- first stable family-capable surface settlement
- administration local to surface residents
- ecological/agricultural commissioning
- mixed profession base

Environmental form:
- pressurized districts
- partial use of external atmospheric pressure to reduce structural differential
- water imported/mined from colder regions during early years

Do not make this “New Earth capital.”

---

# 10. Surface Settlement B — Night-Ice Industrial Corridor

Design ID: SR-B.

Working ending population:
**~4,000–7,000**.

Primary functions:
- water/volatile extraction
- cold-environment industry
- storage
- scientific/industrial work

Cultural opportunity:
Permanent darkness creates a genuinely different human settlement aesthetic.

It is not a punishment colony.

---

# 11. Surface Settlement C — Day-side Energy / Materials Site

Design ID: SR-C.

Working ending population:
**~1,000–3,000**, often shift/rotation heavy.

Primary functions:
- solar-energy collection
- selected high-temperature/material processes
- atmospheric/environmental monitoring

Not yet ideal for large family population.

This makes surface settlement internally diverse from the beginning.

---

# 12. Scientific Reserve / Field Node

Design ID: SR-D.

Working permanent population:
small, hundreds to low thousands.

Purpose:
- planetary science
- protected geochemical/prebiotic sites
- climate monitoring

Because no extant native life is confirmed, this is not an alien quarantine plot.

It represents a future where some places are preserved for knowledge rather than extraction.

---

# 13. Settlement Pressure Architecture

The planet's substantial nonbreathable atmosphere helps engineering.

If future external pressure is ~0.7–0.9 bar, human habitats can use lower pressure differential than Mars-style bases.

Working internal life-support options may use:
- reduced total pressure with Earthlike oxygen partial pressure
- multiple sealed zones
- emergency refuge segments

Exact gas mixture remains engineering-open.

Important:
Air toxicity/asphyxiation risk remains severe because external air is not breathable even if pressure is high.

---

# 14. H8 Material Lineage Payoff

Track one explicit mass lineage:

`Old Transfer District settlement shell`
→ `removed from H8`
→ `processed/refitted through H3`
→ `transported through Axis/settlement chain`
→ `installed in SR-A or a planetary-orbit transfer structure`

Later characters recognize the material/serial history.

But narration must not claim:

> “The old neighborhood lives on unchanged.”

The old neighborhood ended.

Material continuity is not identity equivalence.

# 15. Population Choice, Not Sorting by Ontology

Final distribution must visibly cross developmental origin.

Seeded-development people:
- surface
- planetary orbit
- star-centric habitats

BH-1:
chooses her own path.

Restored persons:
also distributed across settlement classes.

No geography becomes:
- “human planet”
- “AI orbital ring”

That would undo the theme institutionally.

# 16. Governance End State

Preferred constitutional shape:

**Multi-world compact / federation-like network**, exact title open.

Shared functions:
- navigation / collision / system safety
- radiation hazard coordination
- baseline personhood guarantees
- resource agreements
- inter-settlement mobility rights

Local powers:
- family law within common rights floor
- education/culture
- local economic organization
- data/privacy architecture
- settlement-specific environmental policy

No protagonist presidency.

---

# 17. Economic End State

The closed-ship economy begins opening into a system economy but scarcity does not vanish.

New major flows:
- planetary water/minerals
- asteroid/raw material
- orbital fabrication
- biological/food stock
- transit services

The old rule changes from:

> every atom is already inside the ship

to:

> extraction is possible, but processing/transport/ecology/energy still limit use.

This prevents arrival from becoming instant post-scarcity.
