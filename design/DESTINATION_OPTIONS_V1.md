# Destination Architecture Options v1

Status: COMPARATIVE DESIGN — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Destination Requirements

The destination must:

- be physically real
- matter before the final act
- force present-day resource and political choices
- connect to the biological/artificial transition without making one human a magic key
- remain dramatically relevant after the sole-human identity is known
- create more than one defensible future

Reject:

- alien invasion waiting at destination
- paradise that solves every problem
- planet was fake all along
- sole human is required to activate/land/control colony
- arbitrary final boss

---

# D1 — THE SHIP MUST BECOME THE COLONY
## Cannibalization destination

### Core idea

The generation ship was never designed to remain intact after arrival.

Major habitat structures, industrial modules, water reserves, shielding mass, ecological stock, and manufacturing systems are intended to be **dismantled and converted into settlement infrastructure**.

### Present consequence

Arrival preparation begins years before actual arrival.

Habitat transition is no longer routine maintenance only.

Some communities will eventually lose the physical worlds in which their cultures developed.

### Central question

> Do the dead founders have the right to demand that the living dismantle their home to complete the mission?

### Connection to protagonist

Excellent.

A Habitat Transition Officer becomes professionally central without gaining magical authority.

### Connection to sole human

Old settlement plans use baseline-human biological assumptions for some environmental/medical models, but the sole person is a **reference problem**, not a key.

### Strengths

- makes protagonist profession essential
- turns ship geography into emotional stakes
- arrival pressure is active early
- creates material scarcity
- naturally drives Shipborn Future vs Voyage Mandate politics
- visually powerful adaptation potential

### Risks

- if dismantling is inevitable, story may become too deterministic
- need plausible reason the ship cannot simply remain fully intact while building colony

### Solution direction

Settlement requires a large portion of the ship’s finite industrial/ecological mass; keeping everything intact is technically possible only by slowing or shrinking colonization, not impossible by fiat.

**Current score: 9.5/10**

# D2 — THE TARGET WORLD IS HABITABLE, BUT NOT FOR THE CIVILIZATION AS DESIGNED
## Compatibility destination

### Core idea

Launch-era surveys correctly identified a viable target world, but closer modern observation reveals that “habitable” never meant Earth duplicate.

The atmosphere, gravity, chemistry, microbiology, radiation, or day/night cycle requires substantial adaptation.

### Important inversion

Current artificial-origin citizens may be **easier**, not harder, to adapt medically than the baseline human.

The sole human can be the least suitable person for the original “human colony.”

### Central question

> If humanity changed to survive the journey, must the destination be changed for them—or must they change again for the destination?

### Strengths

- attacks biological-superiority assumptions
- strong SF sense of arrival
- connects medicine/ecology to politics
- gives Biosphere Commons major role

### Risks

- can drift into technical terraforming novel
- easy to overuse “planet is more dangerous than expected” trope

**Current score: 8.5/10**

---

# D3 — ORBITAL FUTURE IS BETTER THAN THE PLANET
## Mission reinterpretation destination

### Core idea

The target system contains abundant resources for orbital habitats. By arrival-era technology, expanding ship-derived habitats may be safer and more prosperous than settling the planet directly.

### Central question

> Is landing on a planet still the mission, or was the true mission simply to ensure civilization survives beyond the Solar System?

### Strengths

- strongest critique of inherited goals
- makes Shipborn Future philosophy materially credible
- avoids planet-as-destiny cliché
- permits future IP expansion across one star system

### Risks

- can weaken emotional promise of a generation ship “arriving” somewhere
- potentially less intuitive to mainstream readers
- old biological-human question may feel less materially urgent

**Current score: 8.0/10**

---

# D4 — THERE IS NO SINGLE DESTINATION COMMUNITY
## Distributed settlement destination

### Core idea

The target system offers several imperfect settlement strategies:

- one difficult planetary surface
- orbital habitats
- industrial moons/asteroids
- continued ship habitation

No single option can support all current lifestyles equally well.

### Central question

> Must a civilization remain one society after arrival?

### Strengths

- excellent late-series faction politics
- makes identity, family, and community choices concrete
- avoids one final “correct” answer
- creates expansion potential after main series

### Risks

- can become too sprawling
- weakens focused finale if introduced too early
- requires clear emotional center

**Current score: 8.5/10**

# 2. Recommended Synthesis

## D1 as structural core + D2 as physical complication + D3/D4 as genuine alternatives

Working destination architecture:

1. The target star/system is real.
2. A planetary settlement was the original mission.
3. The ship was designed so a significant fraction of habitat/industrial mass would eventually be repurposed into colony infrastructure.
4. Arrival preparation therefore requires habitat transitions **well before arrival**.
5. Modern observations show the target world is viable but requires more adaptation than launch planners expected.
6. Current artificial-origin citizens are not inherently less suited than baseline humans; some may be better suited.
7. Building orbital habitats or retaining part of the ship is now technically plausible.
8. The civilization therefore has a real choice, not a fake one:
   - fulfill founder plan
   - revise the plan
   - split into multiple settlement modes
   - preserve more of the ship

## Why this is strong

The destination becomes the project’s **future-facing mirror** of the historical mystery.

Past question:

> How did humans change themselves to continue the voyage?

Future question:

> How much should their descendants change themselves—or their inherited mission—to create the next civilization?

# 3. Arrival Pressure Without Immediate Arrival

The series does not need to begin months from planetary landing.

A stronger structure is:

- actual arrival remains years away
- a near-term **irreversible preparation threshold** is approaching

Examples of irreversible preparation decisions:

- habitat conversion contracts
- seed/ecology allocation
- industrial line conversion
- population skill-training cohorts
- long-lead landing/orbital infrastructure
- reserve mass assignment
- route/deceleration commitments

Thus the destination matters now without forcing the whole series to occur during one countdown.

---

# 4. Biological-Human Relevance Rule

The sole baseline human may matter because:

- some old biomedical/ecological models used baseline physiology
- old legal documents use narrow biological terminology
- some factions treat the person as proof of founder continuity
- scientists want data to compare developmental pathways

But the person must **not** be:

- sole legal owner of the ship
- required reproductive parent
- only person who can survive the planet
- biometric activation key
- command override
- hereditary monarch

If any later design creates these, reject it.

# 5. Remaining Destination Decisions

Before freeze:

- real vs fictional star system
- travel distance
- voyage duration
- current remaining years
- propulsion/deceleration model
- target world gravity/atmosphere/biosphere status
- required colony mass
- percentage of ship that must be repurposed under founder plan
- viability of orbital alternative
- long-term viability of preserving ship habitats
- communication history with Solar System / Earth status

These should be fixed only after SF engineering research.
