# Major Relationship Architecture v1

Status: PROVISIONAL — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Relationship Design Principle

Relationships are not clue-delivery devices.

Every major relationship must:

- matter before the biological/artificial truth is known
- possess its own conflict independent of the central mystery
- change because of choices, not only revelations
- remain emotionally meaningful if one or both people are revealed to be artificial-origin
- continue generating story after the protagonist’s own identity is resolved

No major cast should be built so that “everyone secretly knew everything.”

Recommended information distribution:

- most loved ones know **nothing** about the true ontology
- some professionals know narrow anomalies
- one or two people may conceal related personal facts
- almost nobody possesses the whole truth

# 2. Core Relationship Network

Use roles before names. Names, ages, genders, and exact biographies remain open until character differentiation QA.

## R1 — PARENT / PRIMARY FAMILY ANCHOR

### Dramatic function

The relationship that proves the story’s emotional thesis **before** the ontology debate begins.

### Working model

One living parent or parent-equivalent from the protagonist’s ordinary upbringing.

This person is neither a scientist nor hidden mastermind.

They have a recognizable civilian life, local obligations, friends, habits, and flaws.

### Independent goal

Keep the family’s long-standing home/community intact during a major habitat transition, even if the official relocation plan is objectively efficient.

### Conflict with protagonist

The protagonist’s profession values continuity through systems and planned relocation.

The parent values continuity through **place, neighbors, accumulated routines, and lived history**.

This creates an immediate non-mystery conflict:

> Can a home remain “the same life” after everything physical around it is replaced?

### Emotional vulnerability

The protagonist fears becoming professionally responsible for dismantling the world that raised them.

### Reveal transformation

When artificial origin becomes plausible/public, the protagonist may suddenly reinterpret childhood memories as “manufactured.”

The parent does not need a hidden answer.

The real conflict becomes:

> Does learning the mechanism of your parent’s birth alter who fed you, waited for you, fought with you, and raised you?

### Long-term value

The parent can later become politically inconvenient because they refuse both:

- biological-human worship
- the claim that origin never matters at all

Their position can be practical:

> Origin matters when it changes what someone needs, not when it is used to erase what they lived.

### Failure mode

Do not make the parent a pure moral oracle.

They should be capable of selfishness, nostalgia, manipulation, and fear.

## R2 — YOUNGER FAMILY MEMBER / PROTECTED PERSON

### Dramatic function

Makes the protagonist’s old classification falsification personally consequential.

### Working model

A younger sibling, cousin, niece/nephew, foster-relative, or household dependent.

Exact relation remains open.

### Independent goal

Wants autonomy from being treated as “the person the protagonist saved.”

They resent having their life continuously interpreted through vulnerability.

### Link to protagonist secret

Years earlier, during an emergency or relocation, the protagonist altered a household classification so this person could remain with the only caregiver/community capable of supporting them.

The choice was compassionate but unauthorized.

### Why this secret is useful

It prevents the protagonist from beginning as a clean defender of procedure.

They already know:

> The system can be wrong, and I once lied to it.

### Conflict

The protected person later discovers or suspects the falsification and may view it differently:

- gratitude
- humiliation
- anger at loss of agency
- fear that their entire legal identity is compromised

### Ontology transformation

When society begins questioning whether records determine personhood, this old family secret becomes a miniature version of the civilization-scale problem.

### Important rule

This younger person should **not automatically be the sole biological human** merely because they are emotionally important.

That possibility can be tested later but is currently disfavored because it risks turning protection into destiny.

## R3 — CLOSEST COLLEAGUE / OPERATIONAL PARTNER

### Dramatic function

Provides everyday competence chemistry, professional argument, humor, and an alternative model of responsibility.

### Working role

A technical/logistics counterpart who travels with the protagonist on many transition projects but lacks the protagonist’s civic authority.

Potential background:

- axial logistics
- systems integration
- maintenance scheduling
- transport coordination

### Independent goal

Wants transition decisions to stop promising more social continuity than the ship can physically afford.

### Core belief

> A bad number can kill people faster than a bad law.

### Conflict with protagonist

Protagonist sees hidden social dependencies.

Colleague sees hidden material dependencies.

Both are often right.

### Story utility

This pair converts abstract scarcity into concrete professional tradeoffs without requiring lectures.

### Relationship arc engine

Trust grows not through confession but through repeatedly accepting responsibility for each other’s blind spots.

### Ontology transformation

If baseline-human physiology requires resources or medical exceptions, this colleague becomes one of the first people forced to ask:

> Does equal dignity require unequal resource allocation?

### Failure mode

Do not make this person a “science exposition sidekick.” They need family, politics, embarrassment, loyalties, and mistakes outside work.

---

## R4 — INTIMATE / LOVE-LEVEL RELATIONSHIP

### Dramatic function

Tests whether relational authenticity survives ontological reclassification.

### Recommended structure

Do not begin with a perfect destined romance.

A stronger working model is a **past or interrupted intimate relationship** that remains unresolved because of conflicting values and careers.

### Possible role

A continuity-rights lawyer, physician, educator, archive professional, or local community advocate whose work repeatedly intersects transition decisions.

Do not finalize profession until cast-role overlap is tested.

### Independent goal

Wants the protagonist to stop using duty as a way to avoid personal commitments.

### Core relational wound

The protagonist can preserve a whole community’s continuity on paper yet failed to preserve this relationship in practice.

### Why this is useful

It ties the series theme to a personal contradiction without making romance the plot.

### Ontology transformation

The relationship must survive at least three conceptual phases:

1. both believe themselves human
2. one/both learn artificial-origin possibility
3. one person’s biological status may become known

The emotional question changes each time.

### Strong rule

If one is baseline and one artificial-origin, the story must **not** imply the relationship is tragic merely because one is “real” and one “fake.”

Actual conflict should arise from consequences such as:

- mortality asymmetry
- restoration rights
- medical incompatibility
- political exposure
- consent and privacy
- future family choices

### Failure mode

Do not use romance solely to reveal “your lover was an AI.” That is too shallow for this premise.

## R5 — MENTOR / SUPERIOR

### Dramatic function

Embodies the best version of the current institutional order before the protagonist outgrows it.

### Working role

Senior transition administrator or cross-habitat civic planner.

### Independent goal

Keep relocation and infrastructure reform politically legitimate enough that the ship can survive necessary century-scale maintenance.

### Core belief

> Institutions are imperfect, but without trusted procedure every strong local group simply protects its own people.

This belief should be persuasive.

### Relationship

The mentor values the protagonist because the protagonist can see human consequences without abandoning feasibility.

### Hidden issue

The mentor may know **one narrow institutional anomaly**—for example that certain old population categories are restricted—without knowing the true biological/artificial history.

This is preferable to making them a full conspirator.

### Future conflict

The mentor may suppress or delay disclosure not because “truth is evil” but because a badly timed revelation could collapse relocation, resource, or arrival planning.

### Emotional function

Protagonist eventually has to decide whether institutional loyalty means obeying the mentor, exposing them, replacing them, or preserving parts of their work while rejecting their secrecy.

---

## R6 — RIVAL / IDEOLOGICAL ANTAGONIST

### Dramatic function

A recurring person who reaches different decisions from the protagonist using defensible reasoning.

### Recommended role

A habitat-level community organizer / representative / legal advocate who repeatedly challenges transition projects.

Not a villain.

### Independent goal

Protect local self-determination against central planners who claim to understand communities better than residents do.

### Core belief

> No model of dependency is legitimate if the people inside it cannot refuse the plan.

### Why this works

The protagonist is good at systemic continuity but still exercises institutional power.

The rival exposes the paternalism hidden inside “I know what will preserve your life.”

### Relationship development

Possible progression:

- professional obstruction
- mutual respect
- strategic alliance
- severe rupture over truth disclosure
- later cooperation under changed terms

No permanent enemy label is necessary.

### Ontology transformation

The rival may argue that the sole biological human deserves privacy and self-determination precisely because everyone wants to turn them into public property.

Or may argue the opposite in a later phase if public consequences become too large.

The point is not fixed ideology; it is coherent value pressure.

---

## R7 — OLDER CIVILIAN / COMMUNITY MEMORY ANCHOR

### Dramatic function

Provides lived history without becoming an exposition elder.

### Working model

An older figure connected to Legacy Habitat or another repeatedly revisited community.

Could be:

- retired repair worker
- former teacher
- community registrar
- hospice worker
- local shop owner with long social memory

### Independent goal

Preserve a specific community institution, not “tell the protagonist the secret.”

### Memory value

This person remembers things official archives flatten:

- who actually cared for whom
- why a local ritual changed
- which relocation broke families
- which old term people stopped using

Their memories may be incomplete or wrong.

### Ontology transformation

Later factual revelations can make their ordinary stories newly significant.

### Failure mode

Do not turn them into a conveniently omniscient archive.

# 3. Relationship Matrix

| Relation | What protagonist wants from them | What they want from protagonist | Main friction | Long-term thematic pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parent | reassurance/home continuity | presence, not professional solution | place vs planned continuity | lived relation vs origin label |
| Younger family | safety / absolution | autonomy | protection vs agency | records vs lived identity |
| Colleague | material certainty | social realism | people vs resources | equal dignity vs unequal needs |
| Intimate relation | acceptance without surrender | commitment / honesty | duty vs intimacy | love across ontology |
| Mentor | legitimacy / guidance | institutional loyalty | procedure vs truth | order vs recognition |
| Rival | cooperation / compliance | consent / local power | expertise vs self-determination | who decides personhood |
| Memory anchor | context / continuity | preservation of community | archive vs lived memory | history vs official category |

---

# 4. Relationship Anti-Mystery Rule

No more than a minority of these relationships should be directly tied to the central hidden-history conspiracy.

Recommended distribution:

- Parent: no secret knowledge
- Younger family: no central secret knowledge
- Colleague: no central secret knowledge
- Intimate relation: profession may expose partial anomalies, not full truth
- Mentor: narrow restricted anomaly only
- Rival: discovers public evidence independently later
- Memory anchor: lived fragments, no technical answer

This preserves the feeling that ordinary people are **affected by the truth**, not secretly staging it.

---

# 5. Relationship Payoff Requirement

Each major relationship needs at least four distinct payoff families across the series:

1. **Competence payoff** — they solve or survive something together
2. **Choice payoff** — one chooses the other at real cost
3. **Reinterpretation payoff** — old behavior acquires new meaning
4. **Post-truth payoff** — relationship is redefined after ontology becomes known

The same relationship cannot survive 200 episodes by repeating “do you still trust me?”

# 6. Sole-Human Identity Stress Test

Before identity is frozen, test at least these distributions:

### H-A — protagonist is baseline human
Strength: immediate personal stakes.
Risk: chosen-one gravity.

### H-B — intimate relation is baseline human
Strength: love/privacy/political objectification become direct.
Risk: romance can consume the plot.

### H-C — parent is baseline human
Strength: biological parenthood and inheritance become explosive.
Risk: hidden lineage implications.

### H-D — unrelated minor/ordinary citizen is baseline human
Strength: strongest rejection of chosen-one logic; protagonist must act without personal ownership of the truth.
Risk: protagonist’s identity mystery needs another emotional resolution.

### H-E — rival or politically inconvenient ordinary person is baseline human
Strength: forces protagonist to defend someone they oppose.
Risk: can feel constructed for irony.

Current design preference should remain open between **H-A and H-D**, with H-D receiving serious priority because it best protects the project from chosen-one drift.
