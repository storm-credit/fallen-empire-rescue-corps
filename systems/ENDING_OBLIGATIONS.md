# Ending Obligations Ledger v1

Status: ACTIVE PRE-MACRO LEDGER
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Principle

An ending obligation is a promise created by:

- premise
- character wound
- relationship setup
- world rule
- mystery
- recurring motif
- institutional conflict

The finale does not need to answer every minor question, but every **load-bearing promise** must receive one of:

- explicit payoff
- deliberate irreversible loss
- transformed meaning
- consciously unresolved philosophical plurality

Unintentional disappearance is failure.

---

## EO-001 — The title premise

Promise:
There is exactly one current baseline-development biological human aboard the ship.

Must pay:
- why count is one now
- what the category technically means
- who the person is
- why the person is not morally/legally supreme
- what happens to the category afterward

Final state:
The one-person fact becomes historically important but ceases to determine civic worth.

---

## EO-002 — Protagonist identity

Promise:
Protagonist cannot initially prove whether they are the one.

Working payoff:
Protagonist is confirmed seeded-origin with substantial story remaining.

Must pay emotionally:
- fear/hope of being baseline
- relationship responses
- no invalidation of prior life
- renewed agency after identity resolution

Failure condition:
Protagonist becomes secondary after learning they are not BH.

---

## EO-003 — BH-1 autonomy

Promise:
A living ordinary person becomes the most biologically unusual citizen on the ship.

Must pay:
- medical privacy
- political symbolism
- research pressure
- reproductive pressure
- charter/legal pressure
- personal future choice

Final state:
BH-1 remains a citizen, not a sacred asset.

---

## EO-004 — Protagonist’s past falsification

Promise:
Protagonist once altered a household classification to protect a vulnerable younger family member.

Must pay:
- discovery/exposure
- career/institutional consequence
- younger person’s own judgment
- protagonist realizes protection can violate agency
- lesson affects final civilization-scale transition

No quiet forgiveness with no consequence.

---

## EO-005 — Parent / home conflict

Promise:
Protagonist’s own family resists the idea that functional replacement equals continuity.

Must pay:
- concrete early disagreement
- later loss or transformation of meaningful place
- protagonist learns place/consent matter
- parent also learns total preservation is impossible

Final echo:
Ship-to-colony transition uses the matured version of this lesson.

---

## EO-006 — Younger-family autonomy

Promise:
The person protagonist once protected wants ownership of their own identity and life.

Must pay:
- independent choice protagonist dislikes
- response to falsification
- post-reveal self-definition
- no permanent role as “person needing protection”

---

## EO-007 — Intimate relationship

Promise:
A meaningful intimate bond is tested by duty, continuity, and ontology.

Must pay:
- relationship value before central reveal
- real rupture/choice unrelated to “AI shock”
- response to protagonist seeded identity
- post-truth decision about continuation/separation

Either continuation or separation can work, but it must be a chosen resolution, not plot neglect.

---

## EO-008 — Mentor / institution

Promise:
Current procedure has real value even though its categories are incomplete.

Must pay:
- mentor makes at least one defensible secrecy/order decision
- protagonist eventually opposes mentor on meaningful grounds
- institution is reformed, not simply destroyed
- mentor’s strongest argument survives into final system

---

## EO-009 — Rival / self-determination

Promise:
Expert planning can become paternalistic.

Must pay:
- rival defeats protagonist at least once on legitimate grounds
- later alliance does not erase ideological difference
- final transition grants real community agency
- physical limits still constrain local choice

---

## EO-010 — Colleague / material truth

Promise:
People-centered solutions still obey mass, heat, energy, time, and maintenance.

Must pay:
- protagonist’s social solution fails once because material constraints were underestimated
- colleague’s numerical approach fails once because social dependency was underestimated
- final civilization transition integrates both

---

## EO-011 — Restoration

Promise:
Artificial-origin minds can sometimes be reconstructed, but death remains contested.

Must pay:
- at least one emotionally important RC relationship
- legal consequence
- inequality/resource consequence
- right-to-refuse issue
- final law protects RC as person without pretending metaphysics is solved

---

## EO-012 — Continuity Settlement

Promise:
The historical reform was both humane and destructive of knowledge.

Must pay:
- reveal why caste data was dismantled
- reveal someone materially saved by that reform
- reveal knowledge lost because of it
- present society designs a third path rather than simply restoring old registry

---

## EO-013 — Provenance Circle

Promise:
Truth can protect civilization and also become a weapon.

Must pay:
- faction uncovers real evidence
- at least one release helps
- at least one release harms or is exploited
- internal split over public-right-to-know vs privacy
- final archival regime reflects this conflict

---

## EO-014 — Mortality Covenant

Promise:
A person can have the right not to be captured/restored.

Must pay:
- concrete consent conflict
- politically difficult case
- final right-to-refuse survives even when society fears losing rare people/skills

BH-1 is likely a major test but cannot be the only one.

---

## EO-015 — Restoration Rights League

Promise:
If continuity reconstruction exists, access cannot remain purely privileged.

Must pay:
- class/access injustice
- resource constraint that prevents unlimited entitlement
- a durable compromise that recognizes restoration rights without treating them as infinite

---

## EO-016 — Voyage Mandate

Promise:
Founders sacrificed for a mission current people inherited.

Must pay:
- strongest legitimate founder argument
- old charter conflict
- real material benefit of original settlement plan
- final rewrite acknowledges rather than mocks founder intent

---

## EO-017 — Shipborn Future

Promise:
The ship is already a civilization, not merely transportation.

Must pay:
- proof that indefinite unchanged ship life is physically costly/not fully viable
- proof that current habitats have real cultural claims
- final settlement preserves some shipborne future without freezing everything

---

## EO-018 — Stewardship Account

Promise:
Moral ideals cannot ignore closed-system physics.

Must pay:
- major scarcity decision
- faction not always villain
- final plural settlement has explicit material cost
- no everyone-wins resource miracle

---

## EO-019 — Kinship Commons

Promise:
Lived care can matter more than origin labels.

Must pay:
- family law conflict after artificial-origin reveal
- BH-1’s seeded child
- at least one case where biology matters but does not monopolize parenthood
- final law protects relational obligations

---

## EO-020 — Habitat identities

Promise:
The ship is a continent/archipelago, not disposable settings.

Must pay:
- every major habitat reappears after initial introduction
- cross-habitat consequences
- at least several habitat cultures visibly change after truth
- finale involves different settlement choices rather than homogenizing all habitats

---

## EO-021 — Material recycling / death materiality

Promise:
Nothing material truly leaves a closed ship.

Must pay:
- daily-life normalization
- emotionally difficult use in death/heritage/transition
- colony construction literally uses inherited ship matter

The motif must never imply people are “only material.”

---

## EO-022 — Cognition seed

Promise:
Everyone knows the technology but not its historical ontological meaning.

Must pay:
- public normality early
- historical threshold evidence
- scientific explanation precise enough to distinguish enhanced human development from artificial-origin cognition
- no late magical definition

---

## EO-023 — Reversibility safeguard

Promise:
Unseeded development remains possible as redundancy and reproductive autonomy.

Must pay:
- historical reason it was preserved
- modern medical use
- BH-1’s birth path
- late debate over whether society should intentionally create more BH children

Final state:
Capability retained; no individual reproductive duty.

---

## EO-024 — Destination physical reality

Promise:
The destination is real and genuinely usable but not a simple Earth duplicate.

Must pay:
- updated observations
- adaptation requirements
- current citizens’ compatibility
- actual settlement tradeoffs

No late “planet fake” escape.

---

## EO-025 — Ship cannibalization

Promise:
Founder plan uses major ship mass for settlement.

Must pay:
- early abstract awareness
- mid-series conversion projects
- visible community loss
- final decision about what gets dismantled/preserved

At least one beloved place must genuinely end/change.

---

## EO-026 — Solar silence

Promise:
At 10–15 ly, home communication cannot simply be ignored.

Must pay:
- historical communication era
- reason verified channel ended
- last authenticated message/archive relevance
- modern uncertainty
- no outside answer before final choice

---

## EO-027 — Human word

Promise:
Characters sincerely believe they are human before learning artificial cognitive provenance.

Must pay:
- multiple plausible reactions
- no authorial declaration that 299,999 were “fake”
- final law separates personhood/provenance/human identity

---

## EO-028 — Protagonist profession

Promise:
The protagonist specializes in preserving continuity during forced physical change.

Must pay progression:

1. household/building
2. neighborhood
3. habitat
4. cross-habitat policy
5. civilization/settlement transition

Final competence should be an earned scale-up, not sudden promotion to omnipotent leader.

---

# 2. Ending Obligation Audit Rule

Every major Act later must:

- create no more ending obligations than can plausibly be paid,
- advance at least several existing obligations,
- close some obligations before finale,
- reserve only truly series-scale obligations for final Act.

The finale is not a dumping ground for 28 unresolved threads.
