# Dialogue & Voice Matrix v1

Status: **PROVISIONAL PREWRITING VOICE BIBLE — NO MANUSCRIPT DIALOGUE**
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

This document defines speech behavior, not finished dialogue.

Goals:
- identify speakers by rhythm/priority even in audio
- prevent every smart character from sounding like the author
- prevent philosophy from becoming lecture
- make professional background audible without jargon dumping

---

# 1. Global Dialogue Rules

Every major voice is distinguished along six axes:

1. **Sentence shape** — short/long, declarative/questioning, interrupted/complete
2. **First attention** — what the character notices first
3. **Conflict move** — how they push back
4. **Evasion move** — how they avoid vulnerable material
5. **Humor** — if/how they joke
6. **Vocabulary source** — profession/community metaphors they naturally use

Hard rule:
No one gets a gimmick catchphrase.

Hard rule:
At least two major characters must be equally articulate on opposite sides of an issue.

# 2. HAREN SAVIN — Protagonist

Status: preferred provisional name.

## Sentence shape
- medium-short
- tends to sequence problems in causal order
- under stress, becomes shorter and more procedural
- rarely gives ornate speeches

## First attention
- who depends on whom
- sequence / handoff
- what fails if one person or service moves

## Conflict move
Reframes an argument from stated preference to hidden dependency.

He often asks for:
- the actual consequence
- the next affected party
- what happens if a step is removed

## Evasion move
Turns emotional uncertainty into something operational:
- schedule
- eligibility
- options
- next action

This should be recognizable as avoidance before he recognizes it himself.

## Humor
Dry situational humor; most effective with Tessa and family.
Never constant snark.

## Vocabulary source
- route
- dependency
- handoff
- transition
- burden
- fallback

Avoid making him say `system` every paragraph.

## Growth in voice
Early:
more likely to present an optimized answer.

Late:
more likely to state constraints, identify who must choose, and leave space for another person's answer.

The growth is visible in **conversation behavior**, not more philosophical vocabulary.

# 3. MARA SAVIN — Mother / H8 Repairer

## Sentence shape
- medium length
- associative rather than linear
- uses concrete people/objects/places to challenge abstractions
- comfortable interrupting family, less so officials she distrusts

## First attention
- who actually uses a place/object
- how long something has been repaired/adapted
- whether the replacement survives everyday use

## Conflict move
Produces a concrete counterexample from lived practice.

She does not say the model is philosophically wrong; she points to the person/job/routine it forgot.

## Evasion move
Turns fear of loss into irritation or practical criticism.

## Humor
Familiar teasing, especially about Haren's professional habits.
Can be sharp without becoming sitcom mother.

## Vocabulary source
- fit
- seam
- patch
- wear
- material memory
- customer/apprentice routines

Do not overuse textile metaphors until they become thematic neon.

## Blind-spot voice marker
When defending H8, she talks vividly about local costs and vaguely about costs imposed elsewhere.

Late voice change:
Begins naming outside communities/costs with the same concreteness she once reserved for home.

# 4. YUNA SAVIN — Younger Sister / Settlement Ecology Trainee

## Sentence shape
- faster / relatively short
- direct corrections
- dislikes euphemistic professional wording
- asks sharp factual questions when she suspects someone is deciding for her

## First attention
- autonomy
- actual risk rather than someone else's comfort with risk
- ecological time scale
- what an intervention changes downstream

## Conflict move
Forces the speaker to name who gets authority.

## Evasion move
Treats accepting help as potential surrender of control; may become overly self-sufficient.

## Humor
Quick sibling reversals; more playful away from Haren.

## Vocabulary source
- threshold
- feedback
- containment
- adaptation
- release
- recovery

Important:
She should not sound like a climate textbook. Professional terms appear when a decision depends on them.

## Growth
Learns that autonomy includes responsibility for effects on others; does not return to accepting Haren's protection.

# 5. TESSA NOR — Colleague / Logistics Engineer

## Sentence shape
- compact declaratives
- numbers only when they change the choice
- can compress several assumptions into one sentence
- comfortable saying a plan cannot work

## First attention
- mass
- heat
- throughput
- time window
- failure margin

## Conflict move
Asks what budget / margin / physical path pays for the promise.

## Evasion move
Uses technical certainty to avoid discussing social harm she cannot quantify cleanly.

## Humor
Driest core-cast voice.
Often understated and work-specific.

## Vocabulary source
- margin
- load
- queue
- bottleneck
- thermal budget
- reserve

## Growth
Learns to treat unrecorded care/labor dependency as an input that must be discovered socially, not dismissed because it is hard to quantify.

She does not become less quantitative.

# 6. ELIN VESS — Former Partner / Neural Rehab & Continuity Medicine

## Sentence shape
- patient, complete clauses
- uses questions more than declarations in intimate/clinical settings
- becomes surprisingly blunt when someone tries to turn consent into paperwork

## First attention
- what the patient/person currently understands
- what choice is actually being offered
- what harm is reversible
- whether the person is being treated as their diagnosis/status

## Conflict move
Separates:
- medical need
- institutional convenience
- consent

## Evasion move
Can hide personal wants behind respect for the other person's autonomy, thereby avoiding asking for commitment herself.

## Humor
Warm, low-key, usually observational; not sarcastic by default.

## Vocabulary source
- recovery
- adaptation
- consent
- function
- pain
- expectation

## Growth
Accepts that honoring one person's choice does not erase burdens on caregivers/community; learns to ask explicitly rather than always defer.

# 7. TOMA VENN — Mentor / Senior Transition Director

Status: **preferred replacement candidate for Toma Das**; exact name still pre-freeze.

## Sentence shape
- calm, structured, longer than Haren
- rarely interrupts
- often states the institution's reason before his own position
- can summarize opposing positions fairly, which makes disagreement harder

## First attention
- precedent
- weak communities not present in the room
- system-wide consistency
- whether today's exception becomes tomorrow's power tool

## Conflict move
Asks whether a rule still works when the sympathetic protagonist is not the one using it.

## Evasion move
Calls information control `sequencing`, `stability`, or `responsible release` when he is afraid of losing institutional control.

## Humor
Rare, restrained; more visible in private long-history scenes with Haren.

## Vocabulary source
- minimum guarantee
- precedent
- authority
- review
- distribution
- common rule

## Growth
Learns that a legitimate common floor does not require central authority to own every decision or every piece of information.

He never becomes anti-institutional.

# 8. KIAN REL — Rival / Local Self-Determination Advocate

## Sentence shape
- public speech: clean, memorable contrasts and concrete examples
- private speech: shorter, more skeptical, less polished
- tends to expose hidden assumptions in words like `consultation`, `equivalent`, `necessary`

## First attention
- who has veto/choice
- who defined the available options
- whose cost is being made invisible

## Conflict move
Moves from outcome to legitimacy:
A good result is not enough if affected people had no meaningful choice.

## Evasion move
Can define external constraints as political claims longer than he should, because acknowledging hard limits weakens his bargaining position.

## Humor
Sharper than Haren; can use public irony, but should not become a quip machine.

## Vocabulary source
- consent
- mandate
- local burden
- option
- representation
- refusal

## Growth
Accepts that no community has the right to export physical risk invisibly to another community.

He does not abandon local autonomy.

# 9. BRAM OR — Older H8 Transit Anchor

## Sentence shape
- story fragments / route-based recollection
- often gives an answer through a remembered trip/person rather than abstract claim
- may self-correct or be corrected

## First attention
- how people moved
- where access used to be
- which change older residents adapted to before

## Conflict move
Uses lived precedent.

## Evasion move
Turns uncertainty of memory into confident continuity claims.

This must sometimes be wrong.

## Humor
Most naturally anecdotal voice in core cast; can be self-deprecating.

## Vocabulary source
- stop
- line
- transfer
- shift
- route
- detour

## Growth
Becomes more comfortable distinguishing:
- what he remembers
- what records show
- what people now choose to preserve

His value does not depend on perfect memory.

# 10. NADIA VELO — Sole Baseline Human / Fermentation Cooperative Manager

Status: **preferred replacement candidate for Nadia Pell**; exact name still pre-freeze.

## Sentence shape
- concrete and sensory
- dislikes being led into abstract symbolic discussion
- tends to answer broad questions with the immediate practical implication
- when angry, becomes more precise rather than louder

## First attention
- process contamination
- timing
- smell/taste/temperature cues
- who must work extra if a change happens
- whether medical/research access will disrupt ordinary life

## Conflict move
Refuses the premise of questions that treat her as a category before a person.

## Evasion move
Withholds information she thinks institutions will misuse; this can become irresponsible when others bear the risk.

## Humor
Blunt workplace humor, often aimed at grand language that ignores actual labor.

## Vocabulary source
- culture (microbial/community double meaning, but do not overplay)
- batch
- contamination
- starter
- timing
- spoilage
- handover

## Growth
Learns that privacy/autonomy still require honest disclosure when hidden information transfers material risk to others.

She never accepts symbolic ownership by society.

# 11. Core Voice Collision Matrix

|---|---|---|
| Haren / Tessa | both concise/problem-solvers | Haren = people/dependency sequence; Tessa = physical budget/throughput |
| Haren / Toma | both procedural | Haren = case-level adaptive; Toma = precedent/system floor |
| Yuna / Kian | both autonomy-sensitive | Yuna = personal/ecological thresholds; Kian = collective legitimacy/power |

If future prose makes these pairs interchangeable, voice QA fails.

# 13. Philosophy Dialogue Guardrail

When two characters argue a thematic issue, their voice must remain tied to domain.

Bad architecture:
Everyone speaks in generic lines about `what makes us human`.

Preferred architecture:
- Tessa argues the heat/mass trade
- Kian argues who selected the options
- Elin argues what consent was actually obtained
- Mara argues what use/network disappears
- Haren maps the combined dependencies

The philosophical question emerges from incompatible concrete claims.

---

# 14. Audio QA Rules

In any future 3+ person dialogue scene:

- no more than 3 consecutive untagged turns unless voices are unmistakable
- every core speaker should have a different default conflict move
- jargon must be attributable to role
- speaker identity should survive TTS playback without typographic cues

Before manuscript Freeze testing, create short **non-canon voice calibration snippets** only after the Full Prewriting Gate allows prose tests.

Until then, no dialogue samples are needed.
