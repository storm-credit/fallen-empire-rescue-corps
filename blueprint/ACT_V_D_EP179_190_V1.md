**Immediate Goal**
Begin H5's first cargo-hub conversion stage without losing the narrow fabrication/thermal window supporting the larger 3/5 insertion schedule.

**Primary Engine**
E7 Governance / Local Autonomy.

**Secondary**
E1 Transition / E8 Arrival.

**External Event**
Local H5 councils, vendors, workers, and residents were extensively `consulted`.

- relocation sequence,
- compensation,
- mitigation,

# EP184 — Consent Includes the People Who Pay for Your No

**Immediate Goal**
Create an operational consent procedure that can be used for the next irreversible common-asset conversion.

**Primary Engine**
E7 Governance.

**Secondary**
E5/E8.

**External State**
The H5 case proved:
- consultation without refusal is insufficient,
- local refusal can be legitimate,
- refusal can impose external costs,
- those cost-bearers need voice without turning every local choice into a shipwide veto.

**Decision / New Procedure**
For irreversible conversion decisions, the process now requires:
- clear scope of what can actually be refused/changed,
- a bounded decision window,
- published material/externality ledger,
- representation for directly affected external cost-bearers,
- independent rights review,
- no requirement for unanimity across the entire ship.

**Result**
A legitimate procedure exists for the first common-asset dismantling decision.

**Cost**
The procedure is slower and more complex than executive authorization.

**Theme**
TH-015 + TH-007.

**Continuity**
Process becomes precursor to Act VI shared authority and final compact.

**Reward / Hook**
RWD-SOCIAL + RWD-CLOSURE.
HK-PROMISE.
Forward pull: the first asset placed under the new procedure is a centuries-old axial construction cradle that cannot be rebuilt in time once dismantled.

**4-point** 4/4.

# ARC 30 — THE FIRST THING WE CANNOT PUT BACK
## EP185–190

Working asset:
**Axial Construction Cradle Three** (`AC-3`, exact in-world nickname flexible).

Canon function:
- one of the ship's remaining giant zero/low-g axial fabrication/assembly structures,
- used over centuries to assemble/repair large habitat modules, truss sections, radiator structures, and mission hardware,
- non-residential,
- historically important to the growth of the eight-habitat civilization,
- now partly redundant because final-deceleration heavy construction demand is changing and new Ross 128 system yards will eventually replace its role.

Why dismantle:
Its high-grade truss, thermal structures, handling rails, and fabrication interfaces can become:
- planet-linked cargo-transfer frames,
- orbital construction infrastructure,
- surface-descent/arrival support structures.

Why irreversible:
Dismantling AC-3 removes near-term redundancy/capacity for simultaneous km-class ship construction until new destination yards are established.

It is **not** the forward dust shield or another system still required for safe braking.

# V-D Rollup

## Consent payoff
H5 proves:
- consultation without refusal is not consent,
- real refusal can be legitimate,
- legitimate refusal can impose real external costs,
- external cost-bearers need representation.

Kian owns the cost without surrendering autonomy.

## Irreversible payoff
AC-3 dismantling:
- is nonresidential but culturally/industrially meaningful,
- is safe to remove but reduces real construction resilience,
- provides material for arrival infrastructure,
- cannot be restored within current horizon,
- proves mission rewrite is physically real.

## Anti-H8 preservation
AC-3 is not the beloved residential-place loss reserved for Act VI.
It prepares the emotional/mechanical vocabulary without paying H8's final loss early.

## Act-V exit
- lawful mission rewrite process exists
- Sol cannot answer in time
- 3/5 + staged surface architecture is working physical basis
- consent includes externality representation
- first irreversible conversion completed
- Haren remains under shared authority
- arrival is no longer hypothetical
