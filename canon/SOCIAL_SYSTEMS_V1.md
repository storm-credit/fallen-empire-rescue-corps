# Ship Civilization Social Systems v1

Status: PROVISIONAL — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Core Social Principle

The ship is neither:

- a military dictatorship
- a post-scarcity utopia
- a capitalist city transplanted unchanged into space
- an omniscient AI-managed hive

It is a **resource-constrained civic civilization** that has had centuries to build law, markets, families, institutions, religions, and regional identities around life inside a closed machine.

---

# 2. Population

## Working scale

- total population: approximately 300,000
- population intentionally kept within a safe operating band rather than one exact number
- eight major habitats hold most residents
- axial/service population smaller but significant

## Demographic order-of-magnitude

If average effective lifespan is around 90–120 years, stable population implies a few thousand births and deaths per year, not tens of thousands.

Working noncanon range:

- births: ~2,500–3,500/year
- deaths: similar over long average periods
- actual restoration: minority of deaths

These numbers will later be simulated.

---

# 3. Reproductive Governance

A closed ship cannot pretend reproduction has zero carrying-capacity consequence.

## Bad options rejected

- children assigned by authoritarian breeding ministry
- tradable birth licenses sold to wealthy people
- eugenic ranking determining who deserves children
- unrestricted exponential growth somehow ignored

## Working model — Demographic Windows

The civilization sets multi-year **population capacity windows** based on:

- habitat space
- food/ecology capacity
- age structure
- school/medical capacity
- long-term arrival needs

Within the window, reproductive access is managed through waiting periods and scheduling rather than trait scoring.

### Rights principle

No institution may rank reproductive eligibility by:

- intelligence
- developmental provenance
- faction
- occupation prestige
- “genetic worth”

Medical risk can affect recommended treatment but not personhood.

### Social conflict

Reproduction timing is still politically sensitive.

Questions include:

- Does a couple have a right to a child now or only eventually?
- Do habitats control their own demographic quota?
- Do arrival planners get to increase/decrease births for mission reasons?
- Should parents be able to choose seeded vs rare autonomous-development pathways?

This creates politics without dystopian breeding control.

# 4. Household / Family Law

## Family is not defined only by genes

Recognized household relationships can include:

- biological parentage
- gestational parentage
- caregiving parentage
- adoption
- long-term guardianship
- partnership/marriage
- multigenerational households
- chosen household compacts

This legal flexibility is historically rooted in the transition era, when families contained mixed developmental origins and restoration complicated continuity.

## Household Registry

A household registry records:

- care obligations
- residency rights
- dependent relationships
- emergency authority
- schooling/medical guardianship

It deliberately records **relationships**, not only genealogy.

This is central to the protagonist’s profession.

---

# 5. Housing / Place Rights

Private rooms/homes exist, but habitat land cannot operate like unlimited terrestrial real estate.

## Working model

Residents hold long-duration **occupancy/use rights** with strong protections.

They can:

- inherit qualifying household rights
- exchange residences
- renovate within standards
- form cooperatives
- operate neighborhood businesses

But the ship retains eminent transition authority when structural/ecological needs require major reconfiguration.

## Why protagonist matters

A forced transition must determine:

- relocation equivalence
- household continuity
- school/clinic access
- business loss
- cultural-site value
- community dependency

Compensation cannot be just money because equivalent space may not preserve equivalent life.

# 6. Economy

## Dual-layer economy

### Layer A — Guaranteed civilization floor

Every recognized person receives access to:

- breathable environment
- basic water
- standard nutrition
- essential healthcare
- basic housing
- education
- emergency services
- basic network access

These are treated as civic survival rights.

### Layer B — Market / cooperative discretionary economy

Currency/credits operate for:

- specialty food
- entertainment
- larger/desirable housing access
- personal goods
- travel convenience
- professional services
- art/craft
- business investment

## Critical-resource exception

Some things cannot be bought freely regardless of wealth:

- habitat structural mass
- reactor allocation
- radiator capacity
- rare medical bioreactor time
- restoration slots
- major water/ecological reserves

These use public/regulated allocation.

### Political result

Money matters, but material accounting can override money.

This supports both ordinary economic motivation and closed-system realism.

---

# 7. Property / Inheritance

Citizens can own:

- personal goods
- savings
- business shares
- intellectual work
- some equipment
- contractual interests

They do not own:

- atmosphere
- core life-support systems
- structural hull
- ecological reserves

Housing occupancy rights can be inheritable under household-continuity rules but remain subject to major civic transition powers.

## Restoration problem

If a dead person’s property is distributed and an RC returns months later, law cannot simply rewind all transactions.

This creates staged continuity rights rather than total automatic restoration.

---

# 8. Governance

## Federal civic structure

Avoid one all-powerful captain.

Working institutions:

### Shipwide Assembly

Represents population and habitats; controls general law, major budgets, arrival policy.

### Habitat Councils

Control local services, cultural rules, zoning/use, education variations, and some identity/family implementation.

### Civic Executive / Coordination Directorate

Implements shipwide policy and cross-habitat projects.

The protagonist’s transition office likely sits here or in an independent public authority.

### Systems Stewardship Authority

Responsible for non-negotiable physical safety envelopes:

- atmosphere
- power
- heat
- structural integrity
- material reserves
- ecological thresholds

It can issue emergency constraints but cannot permanently legislate personhood.

### Continuity / Civic Court

Resolves high-level conflicts involving:

- identity
- restoration
- household rights
- habitat jurisdiction
- privacy
- constitutional personhood

### Arrival Commission

Temporary/expanding institution coordinating settlement preparation.

Its increasing power becomes a major political issue.

---

# 9. Why There Is No Captain-King

Centuries of normal civilian life make permanent naval command culture implausible.

The ship may preserve emergency command roles for:

- collision
- major hull loss
- reactor crisis
- navigation emergency

but ordinary society uses civilian governance.

This also prevents the plot from being solved by controlling one bridge.

---

# 10. Law Enforcement / Security

A 300,000-person society has crime and violence.

Working system:

- civilian public-safety services
- habitat-level investigation
- shipwide jurisdiction for cross-habitat crime
- specialist cyber/identity/medical-record security
- armed capability limited but real

No giant standing military is necessary during interstellar cruise.

External-defense functions focus on:

- debris
- radiation events
- infrastructure sabotage
- emergency response

Violence can happen without turning the novel into military SF.

---

# 11. Medicine

## Universal essential care

Basic medicine is civic infrastructure because untreated disease threatens closed-system society.

## Scarce advanced care

Some therapies compete for scarce capacity:

- radical regeneration
- restoration
- rare neural reconstruction
- specialized body fabrication

Allocation uses medical need, prognosis, waitlists, and public rules rather than simple ability to pay.

Corruption/favoritism can exist but should not define the whole system.

---

# 12. Education

## Common curriculum

All children learn:

- ship ecology/resource literacy
- emergency behavior
- civic law basics
- voyage history
- physical geography of the ship
- common language(s)

## Local differentiation

Habitats add emphasis:

- ecology
- fabrication
- archives
- logistics
- law
- care

## Apprenticeship

Essential technical fields use long apprenticeship/certification pipelines.

This matters because centuries-old systems cannot be maintained by generic education alone.

## Historical vulnerability

The standard history curriculum teaches the Continuity Settlement as a civil-rights achievement but simplifies the technical origin categories that caused it.

The education system is therefore **not teaching a fabricated history wholesale**; it is teaching a morally coherent but incomplete one.

---

# 13. Work / Labor

Most adults work, but labor is not solely tied to survival rations.

### Essential fields

- ecology
- fabrication
- maintenance
- power/thermal
- medicine
- education
- logistics
- administration
- data/archives

### Automation

Automation handles dangerous/repetitive work but does not eliminate human/person labor because:

- adaptive repair requires judgment
- local trust matters
- institutions need accountable decision-makers
- cultural/economic life generates nonessential work

Artificial-origin citizens are not redundant simply because machinery exists.

---

# 14. Mobility

People can move between habitats, but residency is more consequential than visiting.

Constraints:

- available housing
- work/school placement
- household obligations
- demographic planning
- local residency rules

Migration disputes naturally intersect with transition projects.

---

# 15. Information / Privacy

The Registry Crisis leaves deep institutional memory.

## Modern norm

Personal provenance, medical, and continuity-capture data are strongly compartmentalized.

No universal official can casually query every citizen’s full identity history.

### Consequence for mystery

Even a legitimate protagonist cannot search:

> “Show me the one unseeded citizen.”

without extraordinary legal authority and privacy conflict.

This makes the identity mystery fair without hiding data behind an arbitrary password.

---

# 16. Media

The ship has:

- shipwide public broadcasters
- habitat media
- professional publications
- independent journalists
- personal/community channels

Information scarcity is not the problem.

**Interpretation and trust** are the problem.

A leak can spread quickly while still being impossible to verify because underlying medical/provenance records are protected.

---

# 17. Political Time

Elections/appointments should occur on ordinary civic cycles rather than every crisis creating a revolution.

Arrival preparation introduces a new long-term political cleavage:

- how much present life can be reorganized for future settlement?

The biological/artificial reveal overlays this existing conflict rather than replacing it.

---

# 18. Social Class

Class should be multidimensional:

- desirable location
- credential access
- professional prestige
- network influence
- restoration access
- mobility
- family/community capital

But nobody can legally buy unlimited oxygen or private reactor power.

This keeps inequality plausible within a closed survival system.

---

# 19. Everyday Life Requirement

Worldbuilding must include non-plot life:

- food cultures
- school routines
- sports
- festivals
- local slang
- courtship
- commuting
- elderly care
- funerals
- neighborhood disputes
- hobbies
- vacations between habitats

Without this, the claim that 300,000 people have a real civilization will not emotionally land.

These details are designed later by habitat, not dumped all at once.

This social design naturally produces:

- transition vs home rights
- reproduction vs carrying capacity
- restoration access vs resource limits
- privacy vs historical truth
- habitat autonomy vs shipwide survival
- market freedom vs material stewardship
- founder mission vs living-generation consent
- family continuity vs formal identity

All exist before the central ontology reveal.
