# SF / World Red-Team v1

Status: ACTIVE QA — PROVISIONAL FIXES
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》
Date: 2026-08-17

## 1. Verdict Summary

The current architecture is **salvageable and strong**, but three issues require correction before canon freeze:

1. Modern scientists cannot plausibly be ignorant that their bodies/brains contain engineered structures.
2. A centuries-long closed ship cannot treat recycling losses as negligible.
3. “Artificial consciousness” needs a sharper internal definition than “organically embodied but somehow artificial.”

The correct fix is not to abandon the embodied model. It is to distinguish:

> **Known engineering** from **forgotten ontological/historical meaning**.

Modern citizens can know they are heavily engineered descendants of humans while sincerely believing the engineered prenatal system preserves ordinary human consciousness. The hidden historical fact is that the system crossed a threshold and became the architecture that **constructs/bootstraps the conscious mind itself**.

# 2. Rotating Habitat Check

## Current concept

Eight major rotating habitats around a service/transit spine.

### Reference physics

Artificial gravity follows:

`a = ω²r`

NASA/NTRS research and NASA NIAC concepts explicitly treat rotation as a plausible artificial-gravity approach. NASA’s kilometer-scale habitat concept notes that humans can experience discomfort at rotation rates of a few RPM and that kilometer-scale structures allow near-1g at roughly 1–2 RPM.

### Working numerical sanity case

If one habitat has:

- radius: ~1.5 km
- cylindrical length: ~4 km

then:

- inner cylindrical surface ≈ 37.7 km²
- 1g rotation ≈ 0.77 RPM
- eight such primary surfaces ≈ 302 km²

For 300,000 residents, that is roughly 1,000 residents/km² averaged over primary habitat surfaces before counting stacked structures or separate industrial/agricultural volumes.

This is not cramped by city standards.

### Red-team verdict

**PASS as a scale family**, not final dimensions.

### Required correction

Do not make all eight habitats perfectly identical cylinders socially or structurally. Centuries of rebuilds should produce different usable lengths, interior stacking, damage history, and local density.

# 3. Gravity / Low-G Transition

### Risk

If most citizens live at ~1g, moving through the axial zero/low-g spine creates vestibular and operational issues.

### Fix

- ordinary inter-habitat travelers use enclosed transfer systems
- frequent axial workers train for low-g
- radial elevators create gradual perceived-gravity change
- axial culture treats nausea, orientation, cargo restraint, and movement as everyday occupational knowledge

### Narrative benefit

Physical geography matters without requiring technobabble.

**PASS with operational detail later.**

# 4. Radiation

NASA identifies deep-space ionizing radiation as a major human health risk, including DNA damage, cancer, central-nervous-system effects, and degenerative disease.

### Current historical use

We proposed accumulated radiation as one contributor to biological maintenance debt.

### Red-team objection

A 300,000-person generation ship designed for centuries should have substantial shielding. It is weak to imply everyone simply receives astronaut-like exposure for generations.

### Fix

The ship must use civilization-scale shielding:

- water tanks
- food/organic reserves
- waste/recycling mass
- structural feedstock
- dedicated shielding mass

positioned around major habitats and storm-safe zones.

Radiation becomes a **residual and maintenance problem**, not an unmitigated fatal design flaw.

Historical biological pressure should therefore come from a combination of:

- residual radiation
- rare shielding failures/events
- reproductive bottlenecks
- medical industrial dependency
- long-term genetic/epigenetic burden
- closed-ecosystem pathogen/immune pressures

not radiation alone.

**PASS after correction.**

# 5. Air / Water / Nutrient Closure

NASA’s current ISS ECLSS recovers about 90% of water and uses active air revitalization and oxygen-generation systems. Current technology is nowhere near sufficient by itself for a centuries-long 300,000-person voyage.

### Red-team objection

Even tiny continuous losses become enormous over centuries.

### Fix

Future ship architecture requires **multiple nested loops**, not one magical recycler:

- municipal water recovery
- biological wastewater processing
- industrial chemical recovery
- atmospheric capture
- nutrient/mineral recovery
- sealed agriculture
- large reserve inventories
- leak localization and section isolation
- material accounting down to elements important to ecology

The design rule remains:

> Closed-loop does not mean 100% efficient; it means losses are tracked, minimized, buffered, and eventually become political.

### Strong story implication

An oxygen leak matters, but chronic loss of nitrogen, phosphorus, coolant, catalysts, or trace nutrients can be more strategically dangerous.

**PASS only if reserves/loss budgets are quantified later.**

# 6. Food System

### Red-team objection

Picturesque fields alone cannot efficiently feed 300,000 people in a closed ship.

### Fix already aligned

Mixed production:

- stacked high-efficiency crops
- fermentation/microbial protein
- algae/fungal systems
- cultured products
- visible soil/ecological agriculture for diversity, culture, and resilience

### Additional correction

Agriculture is also:

- air processing
- water cycling
- psychological landscape
- seed/genetic preservation

but should not be treated as the sole oxygen-control system.

**PASS as future-tech closed ecology, needs later quantitative budget.**

# 7. Energy

### Current assumption

Fusion primary power.

### Red-team objection

Fusion is future technology and interstellar propulsion requires vastly more energy than ordinary habitat power.

### Fix

Keep two accounting domains separate:

1. **civilization power** — habitat, industry, agriculture, computation, thermal systems
2. **propulsion energy / reaction mass** — voyage acceleration/deceleration infrastructure

Cruise phase should not imply engines burn at full interstellar thrust continuously.

### Hard rule

Do not use “solar-power habitat” as a major cruise-era energy source between stars.

Starlight is not a practical primary city-power source in interstellar cruise.

Solar becomes important only near origin/destination stars.

**OPEN pending propulsion study.**

# 8. Heat Rejection

NASA spacecraft thermal-control systems rely on radiators/heat exchangers, and NASA identifies high-capacity heat rejection as a key spacecraft design capability.

### Red-team conclusion

For a city-sized ship, radiators are not background decoration.

They are civilization-scale organs.

### Required design

- multiple radiator fields
- segmentation/redundancy
- coolant loops
- repair access
- micrometeoroid protection/isolation
- emergency power-down rules

### Narrative value

Heat rejection can generate crises different from “power shortage.”

**PASS as major infrastructure category.**

# 9. Maintenance for Centuries

### Red-team objection

A ship cannot carry spare parts for every failure over centuries.

### Required model

Civilization must maintain a **manufacturing ecology**:

- raw-material inventory
- metal refining/recovery
- polymers/ceramics
- electronics fabrication at several technology tiers
- machine-tool production
- sensor manufacture
- software preservation
- biotechnology production
- calibration standards

Some launch-era technologies may become impossible to reproduce exactly.

This creates the desired technological archaeology:

> The ship can be advanced overall while still depending on old components nobody can reproduce perfectly.

**PASS conceptually; later manufacturing stack required.**

# 10. 300,000 Artificial Minds: Compute Problem

### Old-risk model

If 299,999 people are server-hosted AIs operating bodies remotely, compute, network latency, central-server vulnerability, and easy copying become enormous holes.

### Embodied model advantage

Their primary cognition occurs in their own living neuro-computational brains.

They are not streaming personalities from a central data center.

Therefore:

- no civilization-scale mind-server is required for ordinary consciousness
- network outages do not switch citizens off
- body death can destroy unique live state
- “backup” requires active state capture

**PASS and strongly preferred.**

# 11. Artificial-Consciousness Definition

### Current weakness

If current citizens have organic brains, grow from fetuses, and form selves through experience, readers may reasonably ask:

> Why are they AI instead of genetically engineered humans?

### Required correction

Define artificial origin at the **cognitive architecture** level.

Working model:

A developmental cognition seed is not a chip containing personality.

It is a designed, self-organizing cognitive kernel encoded into a bio-computational neural scaffold. During fetal/child development it:

- establishes core learning architecture
- stabilizes self-model formation
- bootstraps general cognition
- integrates sensory/body development
- then grows irreversibly through experience

The mature mind is not a copy of the seed, but the seed is a **designed artificial cognitive ancestor** in the same way an operating architecture can generate an unrepeatable long-lived system state.

### Modern-knowledge correction

Modern doctors/scientists **know** this engineered developmental system exists.

They do **not** think it makes people nonhuman.

They understand it as ancient human neurodevelopmental technology.

What is lost is:

- when it replaced baseline neurodevelopment rather than merely assisting it
- the original technical classification that treated the seed as artificial general cognition
- the historical social conflict over that threshold

This is much more plausible than scientists overlooking visible engineering.

**PASS if terminology/rules are sharpened in Canon v2.**

# 12. Medical Detection of the Baseline Human

### Red-team objection

A genuinely unseeded brain/body should show differences to specialists.

### Correct answer

**Yes. It can.**

The project should not require perfect invisibility.

A modern specialist might identify the person as having an extremely rare:

- legacy neurodevelopmental profile
- incompatible neural architecture
- absent standard developmental markers
- unusual regenerative-interface response

But the specialist lacks the historical conclusion:

> This means baseline Homo sapiens / the sole biological human.

The anomaly can be medically known while its **ontological meaning** is forgotten.

### Narrative benefit

Fair clues exist before the reveal.

**PASS after explicit canon update.**

# 13. Why Independent Researchers Have Not Revealed Everything

### Old weak answer

Records are hidden.

Not enough.

### Stronger answer

Researchers know many pieces:

- citizens are engineered
- prenatal cognition scaffolds exist
- body standards changed across centuries
- old classifications existed

What they lack is the **historical mapping between technical developmental categories and the original concept of artificial consciousness**, because:

- transitional categories were messy
- civil provenance data was deliberately severed from ordinary identity
- key technical standards are obsolete
- terminology changed
- old political definitions were contested even at the time
- no modern scientific question requires labeling current people “AI” for routine medicine

Some researchers can and should have published partial anomalies.

The protagonist is not the first intelligent person to notice anything.

**PASS with partial-discovery history.**

# 14. Backup / Restoration

### Red-team objection

If the mind is artificial, why not copy it perfectly?

### Fix

The artificial part is the developmental architecture, not a continuously digital adult state.

Mature identity is distributed through a living, plastic, embodied neural system.

Restoration requires a high-resolution **state capture** performed while the person is alive or medically recoverable.

It cannot infer everything from the original cognition seed.

### Proposed constraints

- captures are periodic, not continuous
- some state is inaccessible/unmeasurable at practical resolution
- memory after last capture is lost
- reconstruction occurs into a new growing/conditioned neural body
- emotional/sensorimotor reintegration is imperfect
- multiple reconstructions create legal and identity conflicts
- repeated copying is restricted by both resource cost and divergence

### Important framing

The story does not need to declare scientifically whether restoration is metaphysically “the same consciousness.”

That uncertainty is part of society.

**PASS as speculative technology with strict limits.**

# 17. Destination / Ship Cannibalization

### Red-team objection

Why would founders design a ship that must be destroyed if orbital habitats are possible?

### Stronger framing

The ship is not physically forced to self-destruct.

The **original settlement plan** budgets large portions of finite mass, ecology, factories, shielding, and stored resources for colony construction because doing so dramatically accelerates settlement.

Modern civilization can preserve more of the ship only by accepting tradeoffs:

- slower surface settlement
- smaller initial colony
- more orbital life
- less reserve margin
- different resource allocation

Thus it is a political choice under physics, not an arbitrary lock.

**PASS.**
