# Solar System / Origin Humanity Status Options

Status: COMPARATIVE — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Why This Must Be Solved

At a target distance of roughly 10–15 light-years, communication delay is long but not impossible.

A generation ship cannot simply behave as if the Solar System vanished from physics.

The design must answer:

- whether the ship transmitted home
- whether Sol transmitted to the ship
- when regular communication ended
- why no outside society corrected the ship’s historical understanding
- whether biological humans still exist outside the ship

---

# S1 — SOLAR CIVILIZATION COLLAPSED

### Mechanism

A major Solar System collapse destroyed or permanently reduced interstellar communication capability.

### Strengths

- simple
- explains silence
- makes ship independent

### Risks

- familiar apocalypse cliché
- creates another giant mystery that can overshadow the ship
- risks turning sole shipboard human into “last human in universe” mythology
- invites external-catastrophe sequel bait

### Verdict

**Not preferred.**

---

# S2 — CENTRAL SENDER DISAPPEARED, CIVILIZATION DID NOT

### Mechanism

The ship originally communicated through dedicated, highly directional interstellar laser/particle communication infrastructure operated by a launch-era Solar authority.

Over centuries:

- that authority fragmented/dissolved
- successor polities no longer shared one colonization program
- the enormous tracking/beam infrastructure lost budget/political ownership
- message cadence declined
- eventually the dedicated beam ended

Ordinary Solar electromagnetic leakage is too weak/unstructured for the ship to reconstruct civilization from 10+ light-years with certainty.

### Designer truth

Solar civilization **survives** in multiple forms.

It is no longer one polity and has itself undergone extensive biological, synthetic, and digital diversification.

Baseline biological humans still exist there, but they are not the uncontested definition of civilization.

### Strengths

- avoids last-human-of-universe cliché
- explains why no external authority fixes ship identity politics
- mirrors the series theme: humanity changed elsewhere too
- no alien/apocalypse required
- sequel/IP expansion possible without changing main ending

### Risks

- why did *nobody* keep sending?
- high-tech civilizations might cheaply maintain some contact

### Required supporting facts

- the old beam required specialized long-baseline infrastructure and precise ephemeris/protocols
- successors had no shared legal obligation
- ship protocols drifted
- occasional ambiguous detections do not equal verified messages
- political fragmentation plus technological divergence compounds compatibility loss

### Score

**9/10 — preferred base option.**

---

# S3 — SHIP-SIDE COMMUNICATION CAPABILITY DEGRADED

### Mechanism

A major historical failure destroyed the main origin-facing high-gain receiver/transmitter array.

Replacement capability never fully recovered.

### Strengths

- physical explanation
- can connect to technological archaeology

### Risks

- advanced civilization should eventually rebuild
- conveniently isolates plot
- can feel like author shutting off the phone

### Verdict

Useful only as a **secondary contributor**, not sole explanation.

---

# S4 — THE SHIP CHOSE CULTURAL SECESSION

### Mechanism

During transition-era political conflict, ship leadership deliberately ended external communication to protect autonomy or suppress destabilizing influence.

### Strengths

- connects directly to history
- creates moral responsibility

### Risks

- conspiracy burden too high
- makes “why don’t people know?” overly dependent on censorship
- one leadership decision implausibly controls centuries

### Verdict

Reject as sole explanation.

---

# S5 — SOL MOVED BEYOND LEGACY COMMUNICATION

### Mechanism

Solar societies shifted toward new communication, computation, or posthuman forms and stopped maintaining backward-compatible interstellar channels.

### Strengths

- thematic mirror
- no collapse

### Risks

- can become handwave: “technology too advanced to talk to us”
- risks deus-ex-machina posthuman reveal

### Verdict

Use only as part of S2’s long technological divergence.

---

# 2. Recommended Synthesis

## S2 + limited S3/S5

Working history:

1. Launch-era Sol operates a dedicated interstellar communication authority.
2. For the first major portion of the voyage, delayed but regular archive/message exchange continues.
3. Solar political order gradually fragments into successor polities.
4. Dedicated support becomes less frequent.
5. A ship-side communications degradation reduces sensitivity/compatibility but does not make physics impossible.
6. The final authenticated high-bandwidth Solar archive arrives **before** the ship’s deepest seeded-development transition becomes socially invisible.
7. The dedicated Solar beam eventually stops.
8. Modern ship astronomers occasionally detect ambiguous artificial-looking signals or energy events, but nothing sufficient to reconstruct current Sol civilization.

### Designer truth

The Solar System remains inhabited.

Its current civilization is plural and heavily post-biological/synthetic; baseline humans are one living lineage among several.

The shipboard `one biological human` fact is therefore **local to the ship**, exactly as the premise states.

---

# 3. Why External Humanity Must Not Solve the Story

Even if Sol survives, the ship should not receive a final authoritative answer like:

> “Hello, we solved personhood centuries ago. Here is the correct constitution.”

That would destroy the current civilization’s agency.

### Recommended timing

No authenticated modern-Sol answer arrives before the civilization makes its key destination/personhood choices.

If the ship re-establishes a powerful target-system transmitter after arrival, a message exchange still takes decades round-trip.

Their future must be chosen **before** outside validation can arrive.

---

# 4. Narrative Use

Solar silence can generate secondary questions without becoming the central mystery:

- old messages show that launch culture itself changed over the first centuries
- some transition-era medical technology originated from Sol updates
- final communication records preserve now-obsolete definitions
- factions mythologize Sol differently
- Voyage Mandate treats launch-era instructions as authority
- Shipborn Future argues that a society which stopped answering cannot own their future

The final status of Sol can remain partially beyond the main narrative horizon while being known to the designer.

---

# 5. Current Recommendation

Promote the following to **PROVISIONAL PREFERRED**, not FROZEN:

- Sol civilization did not simply go extinct.
- dedicated verified communication ended through political/institutional/technical divergence.
- ship society has lacked authoritative current-Sol information for centuries.
- biological humans still exist elsewhere; the ship’s lone baseline person is not the species’ last hope.
- external Sol civilization does not rescue or answer the ship’s moral problem.

This strongly supports the anti-chosen-one design.
