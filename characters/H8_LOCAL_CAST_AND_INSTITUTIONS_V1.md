# 1. Purpose

The H8 district cannot become emotionally important only when Act VI announces that it will be dismantled.

The reader must know it as a functioning place from Act I onward.

Minimum requirement:
- 5 recurring local people besides protagonist
- 4 institutions/businesses with causal roles
- internal disagreement over preservation
- at least 3 different eventual futures

The district is **not a unified nostalgic community**.

---

# 2. Physical Identity

Working reader-name remains OPEN.

The district grew over centuries around launch-era **settlement transfer shells** embedded beneath the inner habitat deck.

Modern residents experience them as:
- foundations
- basement storage
- service tunnels
- utility walls
- market infrastructure

Most residents do not think of them as detachable colony hardware.

Over generations, additional construction turned the launch modules into an ordinary neighborhood.

Visual/social identity:
- narrow layered streets rather than H1's planned grids
- repair shops and small fabricators using reclaimed materials
- exposed old service routes that residents know by habit
- mixed residential/market floors
- high density of informal care and exchange

The district feels old because it has **accumulated adaptations**, not because it is dirty or technologically backward.

# 4. LC-H8-02 — Older Transit Anchor (R7)

Working demographics:
- man, early/mid 70s at series start
- retired local transit dispatcher/operator

Current activity:
runs or helps run a **commuter/repair club** that assists older residents with route changes, mobility devices, and unofficial wayfinding.

## Independent desire
Keep the old local transfer route functioning because several older/disabled residents depend on its familiar topology.

## Strength
Knows physical route history, old service workarounds, and who actually uses which paths.

## Flaw
Confuses remembered continuity with factual permanence.

He repeatedly says some routes have “always” existed; later records show parts were created during earlier major relocations.

This does not invalidate his attachment.

## Act VI future
He chooses to move the **route-help institution** rather than preserve the obsolete transit segment itself.

His final contribution is practical:
help design accessible routes in a new orbital/surface settlement network.

No sage-death-for-symbolism requirement.

# 5. LC-H8-03 — Young Repair Apprentice

Working demographics:
- woman or man, 19–22 at series start
- no blood relation to protagonist's family
- trainee in mother's workshop

## Independent desire
Wants certification in **pressure-shell / settlement fabrication**, a higher-status future field than neighborhood repair.

## Conflict with mother
Mother wants the apprentice to inherit/localize the workshop tradition.

Apprentice admires her but does not want to spend life preserving H8.

## Series function
Mirrors BH-1/child and mother/sister succession questions without duplicating them.

## Act V
Gets accepted into an H3 settlement-module program that will literally dismantle/refabricate some H8 structural material.

This is emotionally uncomfortable but not betrayal.

## Act VI/VII payoff
Helps convert old district material into new infrastructure.

The craft survives through **transformation**, not identical continuation.

# 6. LC-H8-04 — Community Meal Hall Coordinator

Working demographics:
- woman, 40s
- seeded-development
- single household or multi-adult care household; exact family open

Institution:
coordinates a low-cost community meal hall / bulk kitchen that uses surplus from H2/H5 and provides:
- meals
- social contact
- emergency feeding
- informal job information
- care check-ins

## Independent desire
Keep the meal network intact, but she is **less attached to the original building** than the protagonist's mother.

## Strength
Understands people who are invisible to household records.

## Flaw
Can pressure socially isolated residents into participation “for their own good.”

## Internal preservation conflict
She becomes one of the strongest local voices saying:

> Spend political capital on keeping the people/network together, not on saving every wall.

This gives H8 internal disagreement.

## Final future
The meal institution becomes modular/mobile and splits between at least two new settlement nodes.

Continuity persists without one physical center.

# 7. LC-H8-05 — Small Workshop Owner Opposed to Preservation

Working demographics:
- man, 50s
- owns a compact precision-parts / appliance-repair business

## Independent desire
Leave H8 before the district's aging layout ruins another decade of business.

He has wanted redevelopment for years.

## Strength
Provides a credible resident argument that old-place preservation also traps people.

## Flaw
Underestimates neighbors who cannot move business/customer/care networks as easily as he can.

## Act I
May support central transition plan against mother's position.

## Act VI
When full preservation becomes impossible, he is not smugly proven right.

His preferred early relocation also creates costs he failed to predict.

## Final future
Moves early to an orbital industrial district and does reasonably well.

Important:
Not every H8 resident ends sad.

# 8. LC-H8-06 — Local Teen / Young Adult Care Recipient

Working demographics:
- 15–18 at series start
- lives in an irregular caregiving network spanning multiple households

No chosen-child mystery.

## Function
Makes invisible care infrastructure concrete in Act I.

They receive:
- meals from LC-H8-04 network
- occasional supervision from several neighbors
- school transit help from R7's club
- repair/apprenticeship exposure from mother's shop

No single legal household record captures this network.

## Long arc
By Act V–VI this character is an adult and must choose whether they even want the community obligations others built around them.

This prevents “care network” from being automatically benevolent.

## Final future
Open among:
- surface training
- independent H5 life
- retained orbital habitat

Choice must be their own.

# 10. Institution I-H8-B — Community Meal Hall

Not charity-only.

Functions:
- bulk cooking / fermentation use
- food surplus balancing
- social support
- emergency meals during transitions
- informal work/care exchange

It later becomes important during a real transition failure because official logistics assume household-level food provision that does not exist for everyone.

# 11. Institution I-H8-C — Old Local Transfer Route

A short local transit/service path built around/through launch-era settlement modules.

Functions:
- passenger movement
- freight/service carts
- accessibility route

Problem:
Its old geometry is inefficient for current standards but perfectly adapted to local routines.

Act III:
historical archaeology reveals why the route follows apparently irrational geometry.

Act VI:
route physically disappears when modules are removed.

Its **navigation knowledge / accessibility service** survives, not the track.

---

# 12. Institution I-H8-D — Basement Utility / Market Cold Loop

A shared old cooling / process-water / cold-storage network serving:
- food shops
- repair materials
- small medical storage
- community kitchen

This is a good early concrete hidden dependency.

It also explains why moving several businesses independently destroys their economics.

Act I:
protagonist realizes relocation plan omitted shared thermal/service dependency.

Act III:
old dual-development medical/utility interface lies in adjacent launch-era infrastructure.

No single “secret room” contains the whole truth.

# 13. BH-1 Early Appearance Integration

# 14. Internal H8 Political Split

By Act VI, residents roughly divide across more than two positions:

### Position A — Preserve physical district
Mother and some merchants/residents.

### Position B — Preserve networks, release structures
Meal-hall coordinator and some care organizers.

### Position C — Take compensation/opportunity and leave early
Workshop owner + younger workers.

### Position D — Mixed/conditional
Keep selected institutions/modules but accept most conversion.

No authorial “correct residents” faction.

# 16. Emotional Anti-Cheat Rules

Do not:
- kill several locals to force reader sadness
- make every local lovable
- make the pro-preservation side morally pure
- make the leave-early side selfish villains
- reveal a priceless secret under the market that “saves” it
- preserve every business in one new replica district

The loss is meaningful because ordinary useful life accumulated there.
