# Social Factions v1

Status: PROVISIONAL — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Design Rule

Factions are **not habitat teams**.

Every major faction should exist across multiple habitats and contain internal disagreement.

They must have meaningful agendas **before** the biological-human truth becomes public, so the reveal transforms existing politics rather than magically creating politics from nothing.

No final structure may collapse into:

`Human side vs AI side`

---

# F1 — THE CIVIC CONTINUITY SCHOOL

Working label only.

## Core philosophy

> Personhood is produced by continuous civic life: responsibility, rights, memory, relationships, and recognition. Substrate should not determine citizenship.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- standardize personhood law across habitats
- strengthen continuity rights for restored claimants
- reduce local discrimination based on medical/cognitive history
- centralize identity records

## Resources

- legal scholars
- civil administrators
- universities
- professional associations
- media credibility

## Fear

That fragmented local definitions of personhood will make shipwide governance impossible.

## Red line

Creation of a legal caste with fewer civil rights based on body/origin.

## Internal fracture

### Institutional wing
Believes central law should decide identity.

### Radical wing
Believes self-identification and lived continuity should override old records entirely.

## Hidden vulnerability

Its confidence in universal civic law depends on historical definitions it has never fully examined.

If ancient mission law uses a biological category, the faction faces its foundational contradiction.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

Publicly insists biological status is irrelevant; privately fears the revelation could invalidate old constitutional assumptions.

# F2 — THE MORTALITY COVENANT

## Core philosophy

> A finite life has moral shape because it ends. A copy, reconstruction, or restoration must never be allowed to erase the reality of death.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- right to refuse neural state capture
- right to permanent death
- limits on restoration claims
- strong privacy over cognitive records
- protection of unrecorded life

## Resources

- religious communities
- privacy advocates
- hospice workers
- low-link communities
- some medical ethicists

## Fear

That society will redefine human life as an endlessly recoverable information asset.

## Red line

Mandatory backup or compulsory restoration.

## Internal fracture

### Spiritual wing
Believes death has sacred meaning.

### Secular dignity wing
Rejects soul language but opposes informational ownership of persons.

### Hardliners
Consider a restored claimant a new person under all circumstances.

## Hidden vulnerability

Some of its oldest rituals may be distorted remnants of the biological/artificial transition era.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

Not automatically pro-human. Some may argue the baseline person’s irreversible mortality gives them special vulnerability, **not special authority**.

# F3 — THE RESTORATION RIGHTS LEAGUE

## Core philosophy

> If a mind can return with meaningful continuity, society has a duty not to treat technical interruption as death.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- broader access to state capture
- public funding for restoration
- inheritance/marriage continuity rights
- anti-discrimination protections for restored persons

## Resources

- families of restored people
- cognitive medicine sector
- memory/archive professionals
- civil-rights lawyers

## Fear

That restoration will remain a privilege of wealthy or well-connected citizens.

## Red line

Legal doctrine that automatically treats every restored claimant as a stranger.

## Internal fracture

### Continuity maximalists
Restored self is legally the same person.

### Fork-rights wing
Restored self is a new person who inherits some but not all continuity rights.

### Access wing
Cares more about equality of access than metaphysical doctrine.

## Hidden vulnerability

The technical infrastructure enabling restoration may descend directly from artificial-consciousness architecture.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

Could become the strongest opponent of biological privilege: if almost everyone is artificial-origin, their movement becomes majority civil-rights politics overnight.

# F4 — THE VOYAGE MANDATE

## Core philosophy

> No generation owns the ship completely. The living owe a duty to the launch generation and the future settlement for which the voyage exists.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- preserve destination timetable
- maintain mission reserves
- oppose resource consumption that jeopardizes arrival
- centralize critical infrastructure authority
- preserve old charter institutions

## Resources

- navigation professionals
- some senior administrators
- mission historians
- engineering leadership
- destination-planning institutions

## Fear

That the ship will become comfortable enough to abandon its purpose before arrival.

## Red line

Permanent abandonment of the settlement mission without civilization-wide mandate.

## Internal fracture

### Literalists
Original charter language is binding.

### Functionalists
Mission purpose matters, but definitions may evolve.

### Survivalists
Arrival is valuable only if current civilization can survive it.

## Hidden vulnerability

The oldest mission documents may define “humanity” in biological terms that no longer match the population.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

This faction could split catastrophically:

- some argue the one baseline human is the legal mission beneficiary
- others argue that interpretation betrays the mission’s real purpose
- others seek to amend the charter before the truth destabilizes arrival planning

# F5 — THE SHIPBORN FUTURE

## Core philosophy

> The ship is not a waiting room. A civilization has already been born here, and the dead cannot own the future forever.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- habitat autonomy
- redirect arrival reserves toward current quality of life
- reconsider destination assumptions
- treat the ship as a permanent civilization rather than temporary transport

## Resources

- younger political movements
- local habitat councils
- artists/media
- labor groups that bear maintenance costs
- citizens skeptical of inherited mission sacrifice

## Fear

That current generations are being consumed to fulfill an ancient promise they never made.

## Red line

Sacrificing major present populations solely to preserve launch-era mission doctrine.

## Internal fracture

### Reformists
Want renegotiated mission and greater present investment.

### Permanence wing
Wants to remain shipborne if technically possible.

### Destination revisionists
Still want a planet, but not necessarily the original destination.

## Hidden vulnerability

The ship may not be capable of indefinite survival. Their freedom-first politics may depend on optimistic maintenance assumptions.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

May argue the revelation proves biological launch-era identity no longer has any right to dictate the future.

---

# F6 — THE STEWARDSHIP ACCOUNT

## Core philosophy

> Every life depends on a closed material system. Rights cannot ignore mass, energy, heat, time, and repair capacity.

This is not a cartoon technocracy.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- strict material accounting
- limits on resource-intensive restoration
- repair priority based on system risk
- population/resource balancing
- transparent maintenance debt

## Resources

- fabrication guilds
- ecological managers
- infrastructure planners
- resource economists
- maintenance workers

## Fear

That moral promises will exceed the ship’s physical ability to honor them.

## Red line

Policies that commit civilization-scale resources without material accounting.

## Internal fracture

### Egalitarian stewards
Every person receives an equal claim under scarcity.

### Utilitarian stewards
Resources should maximize total survival.

### Heritage stewards
Launch-era reserves must be protected even from current needs.

## Hidden vulnerability

Historical resource models may contain body-type assumptions that indirectly expose the biological/artificial transition.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

Could reject both biological supremacy and unlimited equality if baseline-human medical needs differ substantially. Their question is practical: what does sustaining this person cost, and why?

# F7 — THE KINSHIP COMMONS

## Core philosophy

> No theory of identity should be allowed to erase lived obligations between people who have actually cared for one another.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- family continuity protections
- guardianship rights
- caregiving credits
- protection against forced family separation during relocation/restoration disputes
- legal recognition of nontraditional households

## Resources

- family networks
- teachers
- community organizations
- pediatric/elder care workers
- local mediators

## Fear

That abstract legal or technical definitions will destroy real families.

## Red line

A classification that automatically nullifies parenthood, marriage, or caregiving history.

## Internal fracture

### Relationship-first wing
Care defines family.

### Parentage wing
Creation/gestation matters more.

### Community wing
Household and neighborhood obligation matter more than individual continuity.

## Hidden vulnerability

Some family institutions may have evolved specifically to stabilize artificial-development childhood, meaning their “ancient human tradition” is much newer than believed.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

Likely the faction most resistant to the idea that AI status can retroactively invalidate love—but internal conflict erupts over the status of biological parentage.

# F8 — THE PROVENANCE CIRCLE

## Core philosophy

Publicly:

> Civilization cannot make good decisions if it destroys its own evidence.

This begins as an archival/transparency movement, not a secret villain society.

## Pre-reveal agenda

- restore damaged historical records
- prevent deletion of provenance data
- decentralize archives
- challenge politically convenient history

## Resources

- archivists
- historians
- forensic data specialists
- journalists
- old-family record collections

## Fear

That another generation will permanently lose the ability to distinguish history from policy myth.

## Red line

Deliberate destruction of primary historical evidence.

## Internal fracture

### Public historians
Want open archives.

### Harm-minimization wing
Believes some records can restart origin-based caste violence.

### Obsessive provenance wing
Begins to treat origin as inherently valuable, creating potential drift toward biological essentialism.

## Hidden vulnerability

This faction is the most likely to contain people who have already found partial evidence of the artificial-successor transition.

They do **not** necessarily know the full one-human truth.

## Likely reaction to one-human revelation

Explodes internally over whether truth must be public regardless of social cost.

# 2. Why These Factions Are Not a Binary

The reveal can create unusual coalitions.

Examples:

- Civic Continuity + Restoration Rights oppose biological caste.
- Mortality Covenant + Kinship Commons cooperate on protecting consent, despite disagreeing about restoration.
- Voyage Mandate + Stewardship Account cooperate on arrival resources but fight over charter interpretation.
- Shipborn Future + Provenance Circle may ally to expose history, then split over whether history should constrain the present.
- A biological-preservation argument could attract members from several factions for completely different reasons.

No faction should have a monopoly on compassion, rationality, or selfishness.

# 3. Public-Truth Escalation Model

## Stage 0 — Before one-human fact

Politics concern:

- restoration
- privacy
- habitat autonomy
- resource allocation
- mission duty
- family rights
- historical transparency

## Stage 1 — Rumor of an obsolete category

Factions reinterpret old debates.

## Stage 2 — Evidence that artificial origin is population-wide

Identity politics explodes, but alliances remain cross-cutting.

## Stage 3 — Evidence of exactly one baseline human

The question becomes:

- rights?
- protection?
- privacy?
- symbolic status?
- mission relevance?
- scientific study?

## Stage 4 — Identity of the person becomes discoverable/public

The political question becomes painfully personal.

## Stage 5 — Destination forces institutional choice

The civilization must decide which definition will govern its future rather than merely debate the past.

# 4. Faction QA Rules

Before freeze, each faction must pass:

- can generate sympathetic characters
- can generate harmful actions without becoming cartoon evil
- has internal disagreement
- exists before main reveal
- has material interests, not only philosophy
- can win or lose concrete policy battles
- changes over time
- can ally with ideological opponents
- can survive after protagonist identity reveal

If a faction exists only to state a philosophical position in dialogue, redesign it.
