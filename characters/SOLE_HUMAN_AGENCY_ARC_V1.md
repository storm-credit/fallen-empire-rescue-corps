# 1. Core Test

BH-1 must pass this test:

> If the reader removed the fact that this person is the sole baseline human, would they still have a recognizable life and character arc?

Required answer: **yes**.

Her ontology creates external pressure.
It does not create her personality.

# 3. Occupation

## Fermentation / food-production cooperative manager

This is not quaint decorative work.

The cooperative manages:
- starter cultures
- microbial protein / fermentation lines
- preserved foods
- flavor/cultural food production
- local nutrient-stream contracts
- cold/warm process timing
- neighborhood employment

It is a small but real part of closed-loop food resilience.

### Professional competence

BH-1 understands living microbial systems, process timing, contamination, taste, and community demand.

Her expertise is practical and learned.

It has nothing to do with being baseline human.

---

# 4. Independent Goal A — Succession

Before any ontology reveal, BH-1 wants to stop being the person every cooperative decision depends on.

She wants to transfer real operational responsibility to a younger generation within a few years.

Problem:
Her adult child does **not** want to inherit the cooperative.

This creates an ordinary parent/child conflict before the world turns her into a biological symbol.

BH-1's blind spot:
She talks about autonomy politically later while initially assuming her own child will continue the family/community work.

---

# 5. Independent Goal B — Keep the Cooperative Viable Through Transition

A habitat/market transition threatens:
- access to process water
- temperature-stable space
- local customer foot traffic
- nutrient contracts
- storage

The official replacement space is technically adequate but destroys the cooperative's supply/community network.

BH-1 becomes a difficult stakeholder in the protagonist's work **before** he knows her ontology.

This gives them a real relationship foundation.

---

# 6. Independent Goal C — Medical Privacy

BH-1 has lived for decades with a rare developmental profile.

She knows:
- some neuro-rehabilitation protocols do not fit her well
- continuity capture has poor reliability/compatibility
- specialists sometimes want extra tests

She dislikes being converted into a case.

Her privacy instinct predates public baseline status.

This is why later refusal of research is character-consistent rather than convenient plot behavior.

# 8. Pre-Reveal Mistake

BH-1 must make a consequential mistake that cannot be blamed on society objectifying her.

## Preferred mistake

She omits part of her rare neuro/vestibular compatibility history from a **work-transition fitness disclosure** because she fears being automatically removed from hands-on responsibility during the cooperative relocation.

She rationalizes:
- she has worked safely for decades
- the form is overbroad
- central medicine does not understand her profile
- losing certification would hand the cooperative to people who do not know the process

During a gravity-transfer / axial logistics phase, the omitted condition contributes to:
- a medical episode or impaired response
- an operational delay
- spoilage / lost culture inventory / equipment damage
- another worker taking avoidable risk

Do not make it fatal.

### Why this matters

BH-1 is right that institutions medicalize her.

She is also wrong to transfer risk to coworkers without their knowledge.

Autonomy does not mean freedom from consequences to others.

This makes her philosophically symmetrical with protagonist rather than morally pure.

# 9. Professional Competence Payoff

Before or near the identity reveal, a food-system contamination / culture-collapse threatens a larger supply chain.

BH-1 recognizes a pattern that automated monitoring and younger technicians misinterpret.

She:
- isolates the correct process stage
- preserves a core starter lineage
- reroutes production
- prevents a local shortage or much larger waste

The payoff establishes:

> society does not need her because she is baseline; it needs her because she knows her work.

This should happen before factions fully mythologize her.

# 10. Reveal Reaction

When told her old developmental profile maps to “baseline biological human,” BH-1's first concern should not be:

> “Am I the chosen one?”

More credible concerns:
- Who accessed my medical file?
- Does my family know?
- Does this affect my child?
- Does this invalidate my work certification?
- Are people going to come to my home/cooperative?

She may initially reject the historical label because it has no place in her lived identity.

---

# 11. Three Mandatory Refusals

## Refusal 1 — Scientific beneficence

A sympathetic medical/research team requests extensive voluntary study that could genuinely improve knowledge and future baseline care.

BH-1 refuses at least part of it.

The researchers are not villains.

Her refusal has a real knowledge cost.

## Refusal 2 — Political protection

A civil-rights/privacy coalition offers to make her a protected public symbol/spokesperson.

She refuses the role even though their goals may help her.

Reason:
She does not owe ideological labor as the price of protection.

## Refusal 3 — Reproductive preservation

A well-intentioned movement argues that preserving baseline reproductive capability is too important to leave to chance.

BH-1 explicitly rejects any personal reproductive duty and rejects treating her adult child as evidence of “failed preservation.”

---

# 12. One Decision That Frustrates the Protagonist

Late-series preferred choice:

The protagonist can secure BH-1 a relatively protected **orbital/shipborne** future with excellent medical continuity.

BH-1 instead chooses a higher-uncertainty transition path tied to:
- the cooperative's successor project
- her partner/child's chosen future
- or a new settlement food operation

The exact destination can be planetary or another orbital node after engineering is fixed.

Important:
She is not choosing danger to prove she is human.

She is choosing an ordinary future for reasons that matter to her.

The protagonist must defend her right to take a reasonable risk he would not choose for her.

This is a direct payoff of his sister arc.

# 13. Relationship With Protagonist

## Phase 1 — Difficult stakeholder

She thinks he converts communities into diagrams.
He thinks she withholds information and confuses tradition with necessity.

## Phase 2 — Mutual usefulness

He preserves a dependency she thought central planners would miss.
She solves a food/process issue his plan underestimated.

## Phase 3 — Unwanted protection

After identity reveal, he becomes involved in protecting her privacy because his own transition data is being used to infer her status.

She resents being “protected” by another professional who now knows too much.

## Phase 4 — Open disagreement

They disagree about:
- medical disclosure
- resource allocation
- her future risk
- community preservation

## Phase 5 — Rights without ownership

He eventually protects her authority to choose even when she chooses against his recommendation.

They need not become intimate friends.

Mutual respect is enough.

Different public narratives may describe BH-1 as:

- last real human
- medical relic
- proof biology is irrelevant
- mission heir
- endangered minority of one

The narrative must repeatedly contrast these with ordinary facts:
- she is late to something

- she makes bad staffing decisions

Not through cute slice-of-life filler, but through causal daily life.
