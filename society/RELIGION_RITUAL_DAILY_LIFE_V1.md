# Religion, Ritual, and Daily Life v1

Status: PROVISIONAL — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Design Principle

The ship must feel inhabited before it feels mysterious.

Daily life is not filler. Ordinary practices create the emotional baseline that later revelations recontextualize.

Religion and ritual must arise from centuries of:

- closed-system mortality
- engineered birth
- restoration disputes
- habitat geography
- voyage purpose
- family continuity
- material recycling

No religion exists merely to state the author’s philosophy.

---

# 2. Religious Landscape

There is no single ship religion.

Modern faith exists in three broad families plus secular ritual culture. Each contains many congregations and local traditions.

## RF-1 — Continuance Traditions

### Core intuition

A person exists inside chains of care extending backward and forward through generations.

### Practices

- naming ceremonies involving family/community witnesses
- keeping limited memory objects rather than exhaustive recordings
- annual remembrance of household dead
- obligations to descendants and future settlers

### View of restoration

Varies.

Some accept RC as the returning continuation of a relationship.
Others recognize RC as a new bearer of inherited obligations.

### Historical root

Likely descends partly from mixed-origin family movements during the transition era.

### Reveal pressure

Seeded origin does not necessarily threaten this faith; it may strengthen its claim that lineage is relational rather than substrate-based.

---

## RF-2 — Finality Traditions

### Core intuition

A life gains moral shape because some moments cannot be repeated.

### Practices

- explicit capture/restoration directives
- funerals that mark irreversible absence
- destruction or sealing of selected private recordings
- hospice rites centered on consent

### View of restoration

Ranges from cautious acceptance to strong rejection.

### Historical root

Descends from resistance to compulsory cognitive capture and provenance surveillance.

### Reveal pressure

Artificial-origin status creates an internal crisis: if minds are designed, does that make copying natural—or make embodied uniqueness more sacred?

---

## RF-3 — Voyage Covenant Traditions

### Core intuition

The living participate in a promise crossing generations toward a future world.

### Practices

- launch/founding commemorations
- children ceremonially learning the destination’s sky position
- public service vows
- memorials for generations who maintained systems they would never see completed

### View of humanity

Often historically expansive: “humanity” is the chain carrying the mission.

Some conservative congregations later interpret old founder language biologically, creating schism after the reveal.

### Reveal pressure

The central question becomes whether the mission belongs to founder biology, inherited culture, or current persons.

---

## RF-4 — Secular Civic Ritualism

Many citizens are nonreligious but still practice communal rites because closed civilization needs shared markers.

Examples:

- birth registration gatherings
- adulthood certification
- first independent inter-habitat trip
- public memorial days
- retirement/apprenticeship completion
- habitat founding anniversaries

These rituals matter even to people who reject metaphysical claims.

# 3. Birth Rituals

## Public medical reality

Prenatal developmental care, including cognition-seed integration for standard pregnancies, is ordinary and known.

## Social ritual

The key ceremony is **not** seed installation.

The meaningful social moment occurs when the child receives a name and is formally entered into a household/community relationship network.

Working ritual concept:

- parents/guardians name primary caregivers and witnesses
- witnesses accept limited emergency/care obligations
- household registry records the relationship network

### Why useful

Later, when ontology becomes disputed, the ritual visibly demonstrates:

> Society always made a child someone through relationships, not through a hidden certificate saying “human.”

---

# 4. Coming of Age

A useful ship-specific adulthood marker:

**first independent emergency responsibility certification**.

Every citizen learns enough habitat safety to:

- respond to pressure/fire alarms
- manage emergency masks/shelter
- understand evacuation routes
- recognize air/water contamination basics

Adulthood is partly cultural, but competence in shared survival gives it civic meaning.

Habitat variations can add:

- ecological stewardship shift
- fabrication apprenticeship milestone
- archive/privacy declaration
- first axial transit qualification

---

# 5. Death / Funeral Culture

Funerals differ strongly by restoration philosophy.

## Universal physical issue

Bodies contain valuable matter, but treating remains as raw waste would be culturally brutal.

### Working compromise

After medically and ritually appropriate periods, most bodies are eventually returned to controlled biological/material cycles according to consent and local custom.

Forms can include:

- ecological compost/mineral recovery
- memorial plantings
- sealed memorial portions
- cremation where energy/material law permits

### Cultural phrase concept

Do not finalize prose, but the civilization may possess a broad notion equivalent to:

> nothing leaves the ship, but nobody is only material.

This is a design motif, not final dialogue.

## Restoration cases

When RC is planned, funeral may occur before return.

Families therefore distinguish:

- mourning the interrupted embodied life
- welcoming/refusing continuity claimant later

This can create emotionally complex customs.

---

# 6. Memorial Space

Physical storage is finite, so the ship cannot preserve unlimited tombs.

Memory practices use layered systems:

- small household objects
- public name walls/rotating memorial records
- community trees/gardens
- digital archives under privacy rules
- temporary physical shrines

Legacy Habitat may have rare old permanent memorial structures, making redevelopment politically sensitive.

# 7. Food Culture

Industrial food production does not mean culturally bland food.

## Base production

- fermentation protein
- fungi
- algae
- cultured fats/meats
- high-yield grains/tubers
- hydroponic vegetables

## Prestige/cultural foods

Scarce items derive value from ecological/time cost:

- tree fruit
- long-aged fermentation
- high-water crops
- true animal products if any limited livestock line survives
- habitat-specific herbs

## Habitat identities

- Biosphere Commons: seasonal fresh produce prestige
- Fabrication Concord: portable high-calorie shift foods, fermentation culture
- Kinship Belt: family batch cooking and school foods
- Exchange Ring: mixed fusion cuisine from all habitats
- Low-Link Communities: locally witnessed recipes/traditional preparation without full automation logs
- Legacy Habitat: recipes using obsolete crop strains or archived founder dishes

Food becomes an easy way to show culture without exposition.

---

# 8. Housing / Domestic Life

Homes are compact but not barracks.

Common features:

- modular walls/furniture
- strict fire/air safety
- local water-use norms
- shared neighborhood rooms
- balcony/garden privileges vary by habitat

Long occupancy creates emotionally dense personalization despite standardized infrastructure.

This supports the protagonist’s transition problem:

Two apartments with identical square meters are not socially equivalent homes.

---

# 9. School Life

Children encounter the ship as a world, not a spacecraft emergency.

Ordinary schooling includes:

- common academics
- ecology/resource literacy
- civic history
- emergency training
- cross-habitat cultural modules

Common childhood experiences can include:

- class trips to other gravity environments
- first axis/low-g visit
- habitat exchange programs
- maintenance-community service
- destination astronomy nights

The main mystery should be seeded into school culture only through ordinary incomplete history, never through absurdly censored textbooks full of obvious holes.

---

# 10. Sports and Recreation

Use ship physics for culture.

Possible families:

## Rotational sports

Curvature/Coriolis effects become part of elite athletics in large habitats.

## Low-g sports

Axial arenas support games impossible at 1g.

## Endurance/ecology sports

Long interior routes, climbing/maintenance-inspired events, water-based competitions where habitat allows.

## Inter-habitat leagues

Creates rivalry and shared identity without military conflict.

Sports can later become political stages when origin debates erupt.

---

# 11. Leisure / Travel

Citizens can vacation between habitats.

Travel has recognizable cultural stereotypes:

- Civic Core residents visit Biosphere for open green space
- family trips to low-g recreation
- Exchange Ring for food/markets
- Legacy Habitat for heritage festivals

However ordinary people may still spend most of life in one or two habitats.

---

# 12. Dating / Partnership

Partnership law is flexible because continuity/restoration and mixed household history forced it to become so.

Cultural tensions remain around:

- habitat background
- restoration directives
- reproductive plans/wait timing
- willingness to move habitats
- privacy/capture preferences
- family obligations

A serious partnership may require discussing one’s **continuity directive** much as contemporary couples discuss children/finances.

This turns speculative technology into lived intimacy.

---

# 13. Work Identity

Because essential work literally keeps civilization alive, occupations carry social identity.

But the society must not worship engineers alone.

Care work, teaching, ecology, logistics, law, sanitation, food, archives, and mediation all visibly sustain the ship.

This supports the protagonist’s professional respect without making them elite.

---

# 14. Festivals

Working festival categories:

## Launch Day

Shipwide founding remembrance.

Can later become politically contested after truth about founder biology emerges.

## Turn / Cycle Festivals

Habitat-specific seasonal/cycle celebrations based on artificial ecology.

## Crossing Day

Marks historical midpoint or major voyage milestone; exact event later.

## Continuity Day

Modern civic holiday commemorating the end of provenance caste/Registry Crisis.

This is potentially one of the most powerful later recontextualizations:

A holiday citizens celebrate as equality becomes the anniversary of the reform that also buried technical truth.

Do not make it sinister; both meanings are valid.

## Arrival Preparation Milestones

Increasingly prominent modern events as settlement conversion approaches.

# 15. Language and Naming

Do not invent excessive futuristic slang.

Working principle:

- common language descended from launch lingua franca(s)
- habitat accents/registers exist
- professional vocabulary diverges
- archaic technical words survive in rituals without understood origin

Final choice of Korean-rendered naming conventions should prioritize reader readability and read-aloud clarity.

This will be frozen with the Style/Read-Aloud Bible.

---

# 16. Human-Belief Distribution in Daily Life

Most people do not spend breakfast debating whether they are human.

“Human” is an ordinary self-category until the series destabilizes it.

Different philosophies become visible through practice:

- who counts as family
- how death is handled
- what gets recorded
- what can be restored
- whether place matters
- what is owed to future generations

This prevents the world from feeling like a 300,000-person philosophy seminar.

---

# 17. Post-Reveal Cultural Consequences

After artificial-origin truth becomes public, different daily practices are reinterpreted:

- birth naming ceremony: proof of relational personhood or denial of biological truth?
- funerals: stronger finality movement vs broader restoration movement
- Launch Day: founder-identity controversy
- Continuity Day: celebrated, protested, or reframed
- sports: public symbols/athlete disclosure controversies
- dating: new provenance privacy boundaries
- family: questions about whether parents should choose autonomous development
- food/ecology: baseline-human biological difference becomes politicized in absurd and serious ways

The reveal enters daily life, not only parliament.
