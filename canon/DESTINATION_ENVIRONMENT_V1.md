# 0. Critical Accuracy Correction

Ross 128 b does **not** currently have a directly measured radius.

It was discovered by radial velocity and does not transit.

Present-day robust values are primarily:

- distance to Ross 128: ~3.37 pc / ~11 ly
- planet minimum mass (`M sin i`): ~1.40 Earth masses
- orbital period: ~9.8658 days
- semimajor axis: ~0.0496 AU
- received stellar flux: ~1.38 Earth
- equilibrium-temperature estimate depends strongly on albedo assumptions
- host star is an old, slowly rotating, relatively inactive M4 dwarf

Therefore any precise radius, surface gravity, atmosphere, ocean, or surface map below is **future-fiction canon**, justified by centuries of better observation and late-voyage probes.

Do not present those fictional values as 2026 astronomy.

# 1. Why Ross 128 b Remains the Preferred Destination

Ross 128 b is useful because it is neither:

- an obvious death world
- nor a convenient Earth copy.

The real system already provides:

- ~11 ly mission distance
- a confirmed roughly terrestrial-mass world
- a quiet red-dwarf host compared with highly active nearby M dwarfs
- a ~9.9-day close orbit where tidal locking / spin-orbit resonance is physically plausible
- stellar irradiation near the difficult inner edge of M-dwarf habitability

The latest climate literature reinforces that such worlds can occupy **multiple climate regimes depending on atmosphere, water inventory, and rotation state**.

That uncertainty is useful: the launch civilization and late-voyage probes can legitimately discover the specific state used by the novel.

# 3. Climate Design Principle

Do not create the cliché:

> tiny razor-thin habitable ring between a lava day side and frozen night side.

A substantial atmosphere transports heat.

3D climate work on synchronously rotating M-dwarf planets shows that:
- atmospheric circulation can move substantial heat from day to night
- substellar clouds and equatorial/superrotating circulation can strongly change climate
- near ~10-day rotation, circulation regimes can differ from very slowly rotating cases
- inner-edge M-dwarf planets can enter moist-greenhouse or other unstable states depending on water inventory and atmospheric assumptions

Therefore the chosen future climate should be **broadly zoned and dynamic**, not a geometric cartoon.

# 4. Preferred Atmospheric State — Fictional Future Canon

The planet survived early stellar evolution with an atmosphere, but it is **not an open-air human world**.

Working surface pressure:

**~0.7–0.9 bar**

Working bulk composition:

- mostly N2
- modest CO2
- water vapor strongly region-dependent
- trace Ar / other volcanic gases
- essentially no breathable free O2

Reader-facing rule:

> Humans can work outside only with breathing and thermal protection; ordinary settlements are enclosed/pressurized.

## Why not a 1-bar Earth atmosphere?

Ross 128 b receives high stellar flux for an M-dwarf temperate candidate. A warm, water-rich Earth analog would sit uncomfortably close to moist-greenhouse behavior in some climate models.

## Why not Mars-thin atmosphere?

A moderately substantial atmosphere gives:
- meaningful day-night heat transport
- weather
- lower pressure-habitat differential than Mars
- a world that is difficult rather than barren

# 5. Water Inventory

Preferred:

**water-rich enough for civilization, water-poor enough to avoid an Earth-ocean clone.**

Most accessible water exists as:

- nightside ice sheets / cold-trap reservoirs
- buried/subsurface ice
- high-latitude or topographic glacial reservoirs
- groundwater / brines in some transition regions

Limited stable surface liquid-water environments can exist in the broad temperate transition zone and selected basins, but there is no global ocean.

Why this is useful:
- water is abundant enough to make settlement rational
- mining/transport still matters
- climate remains relatively dry compared with Earth
- the planet avoids becoming a waterworld or simple Earth replacement

# 6. Temperature Geography

Exact numerical map remains open until a dedicated climate model is chosen.

Use qualitative bands instead of pretending precision.

## Substellar / Deep-Day Zone

- brightest permanent red daylight
- dry or seasonless in the ordinary sense
- warm-to-hot lowlands
- cloud/convection patterns concentrated in favored regions
- intense solar-energy access

Not a lava desert.

## Broad Day–Night Transition Zone

- most comfortable natural thermal conditions
- strong winds / weather gradients in some regions
- mixed rock, ice-fed watersheds, cloud belts
- prime early surface-settlement candidates

This zone is **broad**, not a narrow line.

## Deep-Night Zone

- permanently dark
- extensive ice / cold traps
- extremely valuable water resource
- colder but not automatically atmospheric-collapse cold because N2-dominated air and circulation transport heat
- artificial illumination and thermal infrastructure required

## High Terrain

Topography can create local thermal / condensation microclimates on both sides.

This gives multiple settlement cultures without inventing multiple planets.

# 7. Weather

Because the planet is synchronously rotating, weather is organized around persistent geography more than Earth-like day/night cycles.

Working features:

- strong large-scale circulation from illuminated to dark regions
- equatorial high-altitude / superrotating flow
- recurrent cloud structures near preferred convective regions
- persistent wind corridors rather than Earth-like moving seasonal fronts
- local storms where moist air from ice-fed/temperate regions meets warmer day-side air

Avoid saying weather is static just because the star is fixed in the sky.

---

# 8. Light / Sky Identity

Ross 128 is a cool M dwarf.

Surface daylight is visually redder/dimmer in visible wavelengths than Earth sunlight even while total energy flux can be high.

Design direction:
- small red star fixed in the sky on the day hemisphere
- no normal sunrise/sunset under synchronous rotation
- twilight color/brightness changes by geography, clouds, terrain, and atmospheric scattering
- night hemisphere has permanent astronomical darkness except artificial light / aurora / reflected sources

Do not make every outdoor scene crimson monochrome. Human vision, atmospheric scattering, cloud cover, and artificial lighting still create varied color.

# 9. Native Life Decision

## Preferred v1 decision

**No confirmed extant native life.**

The planet contains:
- complex abiotic organic chemistry
- mineral / atmospheric disequilibria worth scientific study
- possible ambiguous prebiotic signatures

But no proven microbial biosphere by the time settlement begins.

### Why this is preferred

If native life exists, the series immediately inherits a second enormous thematic engine:
- planetary protection
- xenobiology
- contamination ethics
- whether humans may colonize a living world

That could be excellent in another novel but competes with this project's human/AI/personhood core.

### Future flexibility

Ancient/ambiguous biosignatures may remain a scientific subplot, but cannot become a hidden-alien twist.

## Day-side strength
- stellar energy collection
- high-temperature processing opportunity
- direct observation / communications

## Transition-zone strength
- best early surface habitation
- mixed water/temperature access
- agriculture under controlled structures

## Night-side strength
- water ice
- cryogenic storage opportunities
- low-temperature astronomy / some industrial processes

- higher gravity than ship habitats
- nonbreathable air

- closed-loop agriculture still necessary at first

This prevents a single “best zone” from monopolizing all value.

# 12. Orbital Architecture

Ross 128 b's close orbit around a low-mass star implies a relatively small Hill sphere compared with Earth.

Working future measurements yield a planetary Hill radius on the order of **~150,000 km**.

This means:
- stable prograde planetary orbits are plausibly limited to well inside that scale
- giant distant moons / Earth-like lunar architecture are unlikely
- the planet cannot support an Earth-style geostationary orbit if the synchronous-orbit radius lies beyond the stable Hill region

## Story consequence

There is no magic “station permanently over one settlement.”

Use:
- lower / medium planetary orbit relay constellations
- multiple orbital habitats/stations
- star-centric co-orbital infrastructure
- L4/L5-like system-scale industrial/reserve nodes where useful

Exact orbital mechanics require later engineering pass.

# 13. Fate of the Generation Ship

Do not force the entire billion-ton-class vessel into one planetary orbit intact.

Preferred arrival architecture:

### Some rotating habitats
Detached/preserved as orbital or star-centric habitat worlds.

### Axial / industrial spine
Partly retained as a major orbital/transfer/fabrication complex.

### Settlement modules / sacrificial structures
Converted into planetary and orbital infrastructure.

### Some habitat mass
Cannibalized because the original mission deliberately designed components for reuse.

This physically supports the ending:

> the old ship becomes **several places**, not one ship disappearing into one colony.

# 14. Seeded vs Unseeded Physiology

Do not create a biological winner.

Both groups have human-derived bodies.

Potential difference lies in treatment compatibility:

Seeded-development citizens may respond more predictably to:
- standardized neurovestibular adaptation protocols
- certain neural rehabilitation tools
- state-capture monitoring

BH-1 / unseeded development may have:
- higher response variance
- different diagnostic baselines

But ordinary strength, intelligence, fertility, creativity, and survival are not ranked by origin.

The ~1.1–1.2 g environment affects **everyone**.

# 15. Settlement Timeline

The final novel does not need a fully mature planetwide civilization.

By EP246–250, it is enough to prove:

- stable orbital settlement infrastructure operates
- at least one sustained surface settlement is genuinely inhabited
- multiple habitat populations have chosen different futures
- supply chains among shipborne/orbital/surface communities function
- no single place monopolizes the definition of “the new home”

Long-term terraforming is outside the main-series payoff.

# 16. Why the Planet Does Not Solve the Theme

Ross 128 b must not become:

> “A natural planet proves the biological human was right.”

Nor:

> “Artificial people belong in space while the human belongs on the planet.”

Surface/orbital choices cross developmental categories.

Some seeded people remain shipborne.
Some seeded people settle the surface.
BH-1 chooses according to her own life.

Geography is a future preference, not an ontology caste.
