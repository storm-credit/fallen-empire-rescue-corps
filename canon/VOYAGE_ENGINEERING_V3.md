# 1. Core Mission Envelope

Preferred mission shape:

- target: Ross 128 system / Ross 128 b (provisional)
- distance: ~11 light-years
- peak cruise velocity: ~0.02c (~6,000 km/s)
- total voyage: ~600 years class
- story begins: **~6 years before final system arrival / insertion**
- primary settlement-conversion deadline: begins much earlier than physical landing

This is not a constant-thrust mission.

# 3. Story Start at T-6 Years

If the final braking phase removes roughly the last ~0.02c over ~60 years, the mean braking acceleration is order:

**~0.003 m/s²**

= roughly **3×10^-4 g**.

This is negligible compared with the ~1 g habitat rotation.

Under a broadly smooth final-braking profile, at T-6 years the ship can still be moving relative to the target star at roughly:

**several hundred km/s**

working center:

**~600–700 km/s**.

Remaining distance is then on the order of:

**a few hundred AU**.

This places the ship in the outer reaches / approach volume of the Ross 128 system while still requiring years of hard braking.

# 5. Propulsion Energy Separation

The civilization electrical grid (~30–50 GW normal) is **not the propulsion power system**.

Final interstellar braking handles petawatt-class kinetic-power flows at some stages.

Therefore the ship contains two conceptually distinct energy architectures:

## Civil power
- habitats
- agriculture
- industry
- computing
- medical systems

## Interstellar propulsion plant
- high-energy fusion direct exhaust
- enormous magnetic/plasma systems
- dedicated radiators / pulse structures
- mission propellant inventory

The propulsion plant is one of the largest structures on the vessel and is not casually repurposed during ordinary city politics.

---

# 6. Effective Exhaust Velocity Requirement

A billion-ton-class ship cannot carry ordinary rocket propellant for a 0.02c maneuver.

To keep propellant fraction within civilization-scale but finite limits, effective exhaust velocity must be an appreciable fraction of light speed.

Preferred fictional propulsion family:

**advanced direct fusion / charged-fusion-product exhaust**

working effective exhaust speed order:

**~0.05–0.1c**.

This is a large future extrapolation.

Why this range:
- at exhaust velocity much below this, rocket-equation propellant becomes dominant
- magnetic/plasma braking can reduce required onboard delta-v

Do not claim current fusion concepts can do this at this scale.

---

# 7. Braking Split

Preferred qualitative split:

### Early braking
Very large magnetic/plasma sail systems interact with interstellar plasma / stellar environment and reduce part of the required momentum.

### Main controllable braking
Advanced onboard fusion direct exhaust provides the largest reliable vector-controlled braking contribution.

### Final insertion
Fusion propulsion + smaller high-control systems match the destination star/planetary orbital geometry.

Magnetic sail is **not** a magic brake that stops the ship by itself.

# 8. Why Braking Is a Social Background Fact

For most citizens, the century-long braking phase is normal history.

It affects:
- propulsion maintenance careers
- power/thermal reserve politics
- axial access restrictions
- settlement calendar
- external-shield orientation
- public rituals around distance/time-to-arrival

But citizens do not feel the tiny linear deceleration strongly because habitat rotation dominates bodily gravity.

The propulsion system should feel like a distant but civilization-defining infrastructure, analogous to a star-scale public works project.

# 9. Forward Scout Architecture

The main ship is too massive to do all detailed reconnaissance itself.

During late cruise/braking it launches much smaller high-acceleration scouts.

## Scout classes

### Long-lead astronomical scouts
Launched decades before main arrival.
They decelerate more aggressively and enter the system years ahead.

### Planetary mapping probes
Deploy after first scouts establish safe approach corridors.

### Sample-return / atmospheric probes
Return data, not necessarily physical samples, to main ship via tight-beam communication.

## They provide before Act V
- actual planet radius
- actual mass/inclination
- atmosphere composition/pressure
- weather maps
- water distribution
- orbital hazards
- surface geology
- possible biosignatures

This is why the future canon can know what 2026 astronomy cannot.

---

# 10. Scout Narrative Limitation

Scouts do not remove human decisions.

They can tell citizens:
- what the planet is
- what materials exist
- what risks exist

They cannot tell them:
- whether to dismantle H8
- whether communities must settle
- whether old charter definitions bind them
- whether BH-1 has more authority

Information reduces uncertainty; it does not solve politics.

# 11. Arrival Is Distributed, Not One Docking Event

A ship with multiple rotating habitats and enormous mass should not remain one rigid vehicle through every final maneuver.

Preferred arrival sequence:

## Stage 1 — Mission package separation
Before deepest system entry, selected settlement modules, shield mass, and industrial packages separate into planned trajectories.

## Stage 2 — Habitat destiny separation
Some rotating habitats are preserved intact and receive their own insertion trajectory.

Others are partially dismantled or converted.

## Stage 3 — Planetary/orbital capture
Smaller modules enter planetary orbit or surface-transfer chains.

## Stage 4 — Main retained infrastructure
Axial/industrial elements settle into a stable star-centric or planet-associated infrastructure orbit after large-scale mass reduction.

The old “ship” stops being one object before the final episode.

This physical fragmentation mirrors the social transition into several worlds.

# 12. Ross 128 b Orbital Constraint

Because Ross 128 b orbits very close to a low-mass star, its Hill sphere is relatively compact.

Working future-planet values imply Hill radius order:

**~150,000 km**.

Therefore:
- stable prograde satellite infrastructure lives well inside that scale
- there is no Earth-like giant distant moon system
- a synchronous/geostationary orbit tied to the ~9.9-day planet rotation would likely sit outside the useful stable planetary region

Story consequence:

No permanent city-sized station “hovering over the same surface settlement.”

Communications use constellations and moving orbital habitats.

# 16. Remaining Propulsion Weakness

Even this v3 does not make the interstellar propulsion “real technology.”

The largest speculative burden remains:

- effective fusion exhaust velocity
- petawatt-class propulsion plant
- huge magnetic/plasma braking structures
- century-scale reliability

This is acceptable only because:
- the story does not depend on discovering a magic engine
- the technology is ancient, expensive, specialized infrastructure
- constraints remain visible
- it grants no protagonist power fantasy
