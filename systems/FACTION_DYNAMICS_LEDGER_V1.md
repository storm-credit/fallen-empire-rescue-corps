# Faction Dynamics Ledger v1

Status: PROVISIONAL MACRO QA
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Core Rule

Factions must act **without waiting for protagonist discovery**.

Each Act should include at least:
- one autonomous faction move
- one unexpected coalition
- one internal faction fracture or dissent
- one material consequence of ideology

No Act may reduce politics to `pro-human vs pro-AI`.

# 3. ACT I — BEFORE THE ONTOLOGY CRISIS

## F1 Civic Continuity
Move:
Pushes for standardized cross-habitat household/continuity records to make large transition projects easier.

Benefit:
Reduces arbitrary local discrimination.

Harm/risk:
Flattens local caregiving relationships not captured in the standard schema.

## F6 Stewardship Account
Move:
Demands transition project cuts because fabrication/maintenance reserve is below comfort threshold.

## F7 Kinship Commons
Move:
Challenges relocation rules that split caregiving networks.

## F5 Shipborn Future
Move:
Uses the relocation controversy to demand more habitat-level control over central transition planning.

### Unexpected coalition
F7 + F5 oppose a central plan for different reasons.

### Political result
The protagonist's early professional case is already part of a living political ecosystem before any “one human” reveal.

# 4. ACT II — PERSONHOOD / RESTORATION PRESSURE

## F3 Restoration Rights
Move:
Launches a funding/access campaign after a restoration-continuity case exposes inequality.

## F2 Mortality Covenant
Move:
Pushes stronger right-to-refuse capture/recording protections.

## F1 Civic Continuity
Move:
Attempts a harmonized legal category for restored claimants.

## F6 Stewardship
Move:
Publishes resource-cost accounting showing universal high-resolution restoration cannot currently be offered without tradeoffs.

### Unexpected coalition
F2 + privacy wing of F8 support limits on centralized identity capture.

### Internal fracture
F3 splits between “same legal person” maximalists and “new person with continuity claims” advocates.

### Result
The audience sees that artificial-person politics already existed before artificial-origin history becomes public.

# 5. ACT III — PROVENANCE TRUTH EMERGES

## F8 Provenance Circle
Move:
Releases authentic transition-era evidence.

Positive consequence:
Breaks a false official simplification and enables historical understanding.

Negative consequence:
Partial records are used to target families/communities or build false bloodline narratives.

## F1 Civic Continuity
Move:
Defends equal civic status while discovering its own legal framework contains inherited biological assumptions.

## F7 Kinship Commons
Move:
Mobilizes against any attempt to retroactively invalidate parentage/marriage/care obligations.

## F5 Shipborn Future
Move:
Argues the reveal proves launch-era biological identity cannot automatically govern present citizens.

## F4 Voyage Mandate
Internal fracture:
- literalists worry mission beneficiary language is biological
- functionalists insist current descendants are the mission population
- survivalists prioritize operational stability over semantic purity

### Unexpected coalition
F1 + F7 + F3 defend current families/personhood against provenance-based rights loss.

### Another coalition
F5 + F8 demand broader truth disclosure, then immediately disagree over whether old provenance categories should be restored in records.

# 6. ACT IV — THE SOLE HUMAN BECOMES PERSONAL

## F2 Mortality Covenant
Move:
Defends BH-1 bodily/privacy autonomy and opposes compulsory capture/research.

## F8 Provenance Circle
Fracture:
- public-history wing argues identity is of civilization-wide importance
- harm-minimization wing argues publishing identifiable medical data recreates old caste machinery
- provenance essentialists drift toward biological fetishization

## F4 Voyage Mandate
Fracture deepens:
Some literalists try to use BH-1 as old-charter authority/beneficiary; functionalists reject this.

## F6 Stewardship
Move:
Insists that any special medical support for BH-1 be openly budgeted rather than disguised.

This should be politically uncomfortable but not villainous.

## F7 Kinship Commons
Move:
Opposes reproductive pressure and protects BH-1's existing family relations from being reframed as “species assets.”

## F1 Civic Continuity
Move:
Attempts emergency anti-discrimination/personhood protection.

### Unexpected coalition
F2 + F7 + F1 institutional wing protect BH-1 privacy for different reasons.

### Critical rule
No faction owns BH-1. BH-1 must refuse requests from allies as well as opponents.

# 7. ACT V — THE MISSION CRISIS

## F4 Voyage Mandate
Move:
Pushes activation of old settlement preparation and protected reserves.

## F5 Shipborn Future
Move:
Demands present-generation ratification before irreversible ship cannibalization.

## F6 Stewardship
Move:
Supports some conversion because indefinite ship maintenance has rising costs, but rejects politically inflated settlement promises.

## F3 Restoration Rights
Move:
Demands that settlement design include RC citizens and continuity infrastructure instead of assuming one body/identity model.

## F2 Mortality Covenant
Move:
Opposes mandatory pre-arrival cognitive capture justified as “mission risk management.”

## F8 Provenance Circle
Move:
Publishes/contests founder charter language and historical communications with Sol.

### Unexpected coalition
F4 functionalists + F6 support partial conversion on material grounds.

### Counter-coalition
F5 reformists + F7 + parts of F1 demand representative consent before community disassembly.

Local refusal forces redesign of transition authority.

# 8. ACT VI — SCARCITY MAKES PRINCIPLES COSTLY

## F6 Stewardship

Forces explicit ranking of physical project priorities.

They must be right about at least one ugly constraint.

## F5 Shipborn Future
Fracture:
- permanence wing wants maximal habitat preservation
- reformists accept some settlement transformation
- some younger members choose planetary future, complicating identity politics

## F2 Mortality Covenant
Move:
Defends a rare/valuable person's refusal of restoration capture under intense public pressure.

## F3 Restoration Rights
Move:
Accepts that infinite access is impossible but demands fair allocation and anti-wealth bias.

## F1 Civic Continuity
Move:
Begins drafting personhood/provenance separation principles.

## F7 Kinship Commons
Move:
Insists transition plans preserve care networks, but encounters cases where keeping one network intact harms another.

### Unexpected coalition
F2 + F3 can cooperate on consent and personhood protections despite metaphysical disagreement.

### Internal fractures
Every major faction should have at least one member/wing choosing differently from stereotype.

# 9. ACT VII — NO ONE FACTION WINS

The final system must visibly contain surviving principles from multiple factions.

## What survives from F1
Universal civic personhood and interoperable institutions.

## What survives from F2
Right to refuse capture/restoration and strong privacy/bodily autonomy.

## What survives from F3
Restored claimants recognized as persons with continuity rights and fairer access.

## What survives from F4
Mission continuity, destination preparation, and intergenerational obligation remain meaningful.

## What survives from F5
The living can rewrite inherited mission; shipborne civilization is legitimate in its own right.

## What survives from F6
Every moral commitment must disclose material cost; no resource miracle.

## What survives from F7
Lived care and relational obligations receive legal weight.

## What survives from F8
Provenance/history are preserved rather than erased again, but under privacy/anti-caste protections.

### Final political principle
The ending should feel like **a new constitutional ecology**, not the protagonist's ideology defeating eight opponents.

- occur before protagonist knows about them
- create a material/social consequence
- would still happen if protagonist were absent

- strike/boycott
- court challenge
- archive release
- privacy refusal
- budget veto
- local referendum
- professional standard change
- restoration-access campaign
- mission reserve activation
- community migration choice
- charter amendment attempt
- internal faction schism
