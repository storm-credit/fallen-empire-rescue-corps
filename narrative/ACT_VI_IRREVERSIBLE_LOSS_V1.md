# ACT VI Irreversible Loss v1

Status: **PROVISIONAL PREFERRED PAYOFF DESIGN**
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

Purpose: choose a specific physical loss so the theme “continuity can survive change but not everything can be preserved” is earned materially rather than stated.

---

# 1. The Place

Working design ID: **H8-OLD TRANSFER DISTRICT**
Reader-facing name: OPEN.

Location:
H8 Legacy Habitat.

Scale:
A several-block / long-market-corridor district, not the entire habitat.

It contains:
- protagonist's mother's repair/reuse workshop
- long-standing market stalls
- apartments
- a community meal space
- local club/institution tied to R7 older memory anchor
- school/transit routes
- small craft/service businesses

The place has existed in recognizable form for generations.

---

# 2. Why This District Exists Physically

At launch, this part of H8 was built around a bank of **modular pressure-rated settlement shells / structural transfer modules**.

Original mission logic:

- the modules served useful ship functions during cruise
- near arrival they were intended to be detached / reworked into settlement pressure vessels, storage shells, and utility structures

Launch designers regarded the overlying cruise-era occupation as temporary on a centuries-long mission timescale.

History did not cooperate.

Over six centuries:
- shops attached to them
- utilities crossed them
- housing grew around them
- transit routes depended on them
- cultural memory accumulated

A mission component became a neighborhood.

---

# 3. Why It Cannot Simply Be Preserved

By late approach, updated destination/settlement engineering requires a large quantity of:

- certified pressure-rated shell material
- high-integrity structural composite
- shielding/volatile interface hardware
- standardized attachment geometry

The old transfer modules remain one of the few large stocks already qualified for rapid conversion.

Alternative:
Fabricate replacements from H3 / other ship stock.

But that requires enough scarce material/industrial time to force unacceptable costs elsewhere:
- defer another habitat's critical repair
- reduce settlement redundancy below agreed safety margin
- consume a major reserve needed for heat/radiator/pressure systems

There is no hidden spare warehouse.

The decision is genuinely distributive.

---

# 4. Why This Is Better Than “The Habitat Is Falling Apart”

If H8 must be abandoned because it is simply broken, the moral choice disappears.

Preferred situation:

**The district can technically remain alive if civilization spends enough to preserve it.**

The conflict is that preservation would make other people's futures less safe.

This forces:
- mother to confront externalized preservation cost
- protagonist to confront non-equivalence of place
- rival/factions to confront shared burden
- colleague to prove the material constraint is real

---

# 5. Early Setup Requirements

The district must appear long before Act VI.

### Act I
Reader sees:
- mother's workshop
- ordinary routes
- local arguments
- one useful old transfer-module feature treated as mundane

No ominous “this place will die” narration.

### Act II
A family/medical or supply event uses the district's social network.

### Act III
Historical archaeology reveals its launch-era structural origin.
This fact changes meaning but does not immediately doom it.

### Act IV
Provenance/public-truth pressure changes local politics and relationships.

### Act V
Arrival plans reveal that the old modules are still listed as recoverable settlement inventory.

Only then does the preservation fight become immediate.

---

# 6. Stakeholder Positions

## Mother
Initial position:
Preserve the district intact.

Best argument:
A technically equivalent replacement cannot reproduce:
- customers
- informal care
- apprenticeships
- memory
- walking routes
- neighbor trust

Blind spot:
She treats the cost imposed on unknown other habitats as abstract.

## Protagonist
Initial mature-but-incomplete instinct:
Create an extremely detailed relocation/replication plan.

Error:
He still assumes continuity can be reproduced if enough dependencies are mapped.

## Colleague
Position:
The material budget makes full preservation extremely expensive.

Blind spot:
Underestimates the number of non-recorded dependencies that will die if the district is simply dispersed.

## Rival
Position:
Residents must possess meaningful authority over what form transition takes.

Blind spot:
Cannot simply grant them unilateral power to spend civilization-wide reserve mass.

---

# 7. The Decision Process

Do not resolve by one public hearing.

Use multiple action containers:

1. structural/material audit
2. neighborhood dependency mapping
3. alternative fabrication trial
4. cross-habitat cost comparison
5. resident organization / refusal
6. negotiation over what can move as a unit
7. time-limited physical conversion schedule

The community is not asked merely:

> “Do you consent to losing your home?”

That would be false choice.

They receive meaningful choices over:
- timing
- cluster relocation
- which shared institutions remain together
- which modules / objects are preserved
- compensation / future access
- whether some residents choose planetary vs orbital futures

But they cannot choose an impossible option where no one else pays.

---

# 8. What Is Preserved

Not a perfect replica.

Potential preserved continuity elements:
- mother's tools / apprenticeships / customer network partly relocates
- market cooperative retains organizational identity
- community meal institution moves as a functioning group
- selected plants/material/object with genuine ongoing use
- R7 local institution survives in a changed site/form

Avoid museum-only preservation as the entire answer.

The things preserved should still **do something**.

---

# 9. What Is Lost

At least these must genuinely end:

- original spatial layout
- some businesses that cannot survive transition
- spontaneous proximity among certain neighbors
- one familiar transit path
- mother's original workshop space
- physical surfaces accumulated across generations

No last-minute miracle saves the district after emotional goodbye.

---

# 10. Mother Payoff

Mother should not simply admit protagonist was right.

She learns:

> “Keeping my place” is not morally free if another community loses its own safety to pay for it.

Protagonist learns:

> “Moving every function” does not preserve a place.

Their shared mature answer:

> Decide together which continuities people actually need, then admit honestly what will still be lost.

---

# 11. Protagonist Secret Echo

This is the civilization-scale version of his sister incident.

Temptation:
He can manipulate classifications/priority formulas to produce the humane outcome he prefers.

He does not.

Instead:
- displaced costs are shown
- affected communities outside H8 get representation
- H8 residents get genuine option design
- no one is told the “correct” choice is painless

This is a behavioral payoff, not a speech.

---

# 12. Material Payoff

The old pressure/transfer modules are dismantled and converted.

Track a specific material lineage into Act VII:

`old district module`
→ `H3 refabrication`
→ `settlement/orbital infrastructure`

At least one later functioning public structure incorporates material from the old district.

Important thematic boundary:

This does **not** mean the new building “is the same place.”

It means material continuity can coexist with real ending.

---

# 13. R7 Memory Anchor Payoff

The older civilian remembers previous relocations and initially overstates how much the old district has “always” been unchanged.

Historical evidence reveals:
- the district itself was created from earlier changes
- some beloved traditions are younger than he thinks

This does not invalidate attachment.

It prevents nostalgia from becoming a false claim of timelessness.

His final role can be helping decide what living institution moves forward rather than becoming museum curator of a dead past.

---

# 14. Wonder / Visual Payoff

Without writing the scene, the structural visual sequence can include:

- familiar neighborhood utilities shut down in deliberate sequence
- large settlement modules exposed beneath/behind structures residents treated as ordinary walls/floors
- zero/low-g transfer of immense structural pieces through the axis
- H3 refabrication of material at scale
- later reappearance of that material in a new habitat/settlement function

This gives Act VI–VII large-scale SF action without war.

# 15. Anti-Manipulation Rules

Do not:
- kill a beloved resident solely to make the demolition sadder
- reveal the district secretly contains the central AI truth
- save everything via sudden new material reserve
- have one villain order destruction
- make residents irrational NIMBY caricatures
- make engineers emotionless destroyers
- imply moving culture is easy
