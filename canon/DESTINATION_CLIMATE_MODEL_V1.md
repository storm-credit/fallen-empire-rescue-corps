# 1. Scientific Inspiration, Not Claim

A 2026 3D climate-model study focused on Ross 128 b explores:
- multiple atmospheric pressures
- water inventories
- synchronous and spin-orbit states
- the planet's high incident flux

For low-water cases under 1:1 synchronous rotation, the study finds the possibility of **moist bistability**: distinct relatively dry and wetter/moister climate states can exist under similar external forcing because water distribution, clouds, and circulation feed back on climate.

The novel does not copy one simulation output as truth.

It uses this as a scientifically grounded reason to choose a **dry, water-limited stable branch** for the future-observed planet.

These are fictional 600-years-future measurements.

## Mass
**1.48 Earth masses**

## Radius
**1.16 Earth radii**

## Surface gravity
Approximately:

`1.48 / 1.16² ≈ 1.10 g`

Preferred canon value:

**~1.10 g**

Why choose 1.10 rather than 1.2:
- enough to make long-term adaptation and labor different
- mild enough for multigenerational surface settlement
- does not dominate the novel as a high-gravity survival story

## Rotation
**1:1 synchronous** with the ~9.8658-day orbit.

The star remains fixed in local sky longitude over long periods.

# 3. Preferred Atmospheric Inventory

Working surface pressure:

**~0.78 bar**

Composition by bulk pressure, approximate:

- N2: ~0.75 bar class
- CO2: ~0.01 bar class
- Ar / minor gases: remainder
- H2O: highly variable, generally low globally
- O2: trace / nonbreathable

Exact percentages remain adjustable within this family after radiative-climate consistency review.

Important:
The atmosphere is **pressure-useful but chemically unbreathable**.

A habitat leak does not necessarily cause explosive decompression; it can cause rapid asphyxiation / CO2 exposure / contamination instead.

That produces different emergency design than Mars.

# 4. Water Inventory

Preferred total accessible planetary water inventory:

**~5–15 m global-equivalent layer class**

Working center:

**~10 m GEL**.

This is vastly more water than the settlers initially need but far less than an Earth ocean.

Most exists as:
- permanent-night ice
- subsurface ice
- buried glacial reservoirs
- cold-trapped deposits
- localized brines / groundwater

Only a small fraction participates in warm-surface hydrology at one time.

---

# 5. Why the Planet Is Dry Despite Having Plenty of Water for Settlement

The key distinction:

> `water available to a civilization` ≠ `ocean-covered climate`.

Ten meters global-equivalent water is a planetary resource of enormous mass, but still tiny compared with Earth's ~kilometers of average ocean depth.

Synchronous circulation and permanent cold regions sequester much of it away from the hotter day side.

The planet can therefore support:
- ice mining
- local watersheds
- engineered lakes/reservoirs

without becoming Earthlike.

# 6. Climate State

The preferred planet is on the **relatively dry stable branch**.

Qualitative climate:

## Deep day side
- warm/hot dry lowlands
- persistent convective/cloud regions where moisture reaches them
- excellent energy availability
- not a molten wasteland

## Broad transition belt
- most naturally moderate temperatures
- persistent wind systems
- local surface/subsurface liquid water possible
- prime initial settlement

## Deep night side
- cold, permanently dark
- major water-ice reservoirs
- atmosphere remains mostly gaseous because N2-dominated bulk atmosphere transports heat
- CO2/H2O condensation occurs regionally without total atmospheric collapse

# 8. The Important Long-Term Climate Risk

Because Ross 128 b receives ~1.38 Earth irradiation and lies near a difficult warm inner-habitable-zone regime, settlers should not assume:

> more atmospheric water is always better.

Large-scale release/redistribution of night-side ice can potentially:
- raise atmospheric water vapor
- strengthen near-IR absorption under an M-dwarf spectrum
- alter cloud circulation
- push the planet toward a substantially wetter/hotter climate state
- increase long-term upper-atmosphere water loss

The exact tipping threshold is unknown even to the settlers.

# 9. Terraforming Decision

## Preferred civilizational policy at series end

**No rapid global terraforming.**

Instead:

- build regional sealed/semisealed settlements
- use local controlled watersheds
- monitor atmosphere over decades
- move water conservatively
- establish planetary climate experiments before irreversible release

This is not fear-based stagnation.

It is the same mature series principle:

> irreversible changes require truthful constraints and consent across the people who inherit the consequence.

# 11. H2 Biosphere Relevance

H2 becomes the primary ecological reference/backup world.

It supplies:
- crop lineages
- microbial communities
- soil ecosystems
- controlled ecological testbeds

But H2 does not simply export Earth's ecosystem wholesale.

- climate may respond to water/biological cycles
- no extant native biosphere is confirmed, but scientific uncertainty remains

# 12. Settlement Architecture Consequence

Because external pressure is ~0.78 bar, settlements can choose internal atmospheres with modest pressure differential.

Working human habitat atmosphere may be lower total pressure than Earth while preserving safe O2 partial pressure.

Potential benefits:
- lighter pressure structures
- less catastrophic decompression energy

New risks:
- toxic external-gas infiltration
- CO2 accumulation
- oxygen/fire management

Exact internal gas mix remains engineering-open.

# 13. Agriculture

Outdoor unprotected agriculture is not the default.

Early agriculture uses:
- pressurized greenhouses
- controlled spectrum lighting blended with filtered stellar light
- sealed soil/hydroponic systems
- carefully contained microbial ecology

Later semi-open agriculture is possible only after decades of atmospheric/ecological validation.

This keeps H2/orbital food systems important after arrival.

# 15. Visual Identity

The planet's visual identity is not only “red sky.”

Key contrasts:

### Day region
fixed red-dwarf sun / persistent lighting direction / warm dry landscapes / structured cloud systems.

### Transition region
long angled illumination / cloud and wind corridors / ice-fed terrain / enclosed city lights mixed with natural dim-red daylight.

### Night region
true permanent dark / stars / industrial and settlement lights / enormous ice fields.

This supports visually distinct surface cultures later without separate planets.
