# 0. Global Character Rule

No major character exists primarily to:

- explain the theme to Haren,
- prove Haren wrong,
- deliver hidden-history exposition,
- provide one ideological position,
- wait for Haren to complete his arc.

Every core character must possess:

1. an independent present-tense goal,
2. a competence domain,
3. a consequential mistake or blind spot,
4. a cost that exists even when Haren is absent,
5. at least one autonomous decision that changes later events,
6. a final choice that is not simply agreement with Haren.

# 1. Haren Savin — Habitat Transition Officer

## Initial goal
Become the officer trusted with the hardest habitat transitions because he believes accurate dependency mapping and humane procedure can prevent avoidable loss.

## Competence
Maps hidden social/material dependencies and redesigns sequencing under constraint.

## False belief
A sufficiently accurate humane model can justify making difficult choices for people when he believes the alternatives are worse.

## Past violation
Eleven years before the series, Haren extended an expired priority-placement classification so his fourteen-year-old sister Yuna could remain near the treatment/school/care network she needed.

The action helped Yuna and harmed another household by pushing them into a worse temporary placement.

## Main arc

### Act I
Competence is rewarded. Haren saves a neighborhood transition by seeing dependencies the official model misses.

### Act II
He begins treating his own family history as data and damages trust even while seeking legitimate answers.

### Act III
He learns that institutional categories can be historically humane, politically dangerous, and technically necessary at the same time.

### Act IV
His old falsification is exposed. He loses unilateral signing/record-amendment authority and must watch another officer make a defensible decision he would not have chosen.

### Act V
He operates through delegated/shared authority and learns that coordination legitimacy must be earned from participants rather than inferred from expertise.

### Act VI
He helps H8 choose what to carry forward but cannot preserve the original neighborhood. The material loss is real.

### Act VII
He coordinates only one part of the final transition. Other operations succeed without him. His maturity is demonstrated by enabling distributed agency, not becoming supreme planner.

## Final state
Haren remains an expert planner, but no longer confuses care with authority over another person's choice.

# 2. Mara Savin — H8 Repair/Reuse Craftsperson and Mother

## Independent goal
Preserve H8's practical repair culture, neighbor obligations, and family continuity through the arrival transition.

## Competence
Knows how objects, workshops, informal borrowing, repair skills, and neighbor trust actually keep old H8 functioning.

## Blind spot
She sees local loss vividly and distant externalized costs abstractly.

## Autonomous action
Mara organizes a resident inventory of practices/tools/relationships worth carrying forward rather than simply joining either full-preservation or full-demolition politics.

## Loss
Her original workshop and the spatial neighborhood pattern around it do not survive.

## Final state
She accepts a rebuilt practice network while continuing to believe that some physical loss was unnecessary. The relationship with Haren ends in mutual respect, not ideological convergence.

# 3. Yuna Savin — Settlement Ecology Specialist

## Independent goal
Earn responsibility for closed ecological systems that will support the first permanent surface settlements.

She does not want her career interpreted as an extension of Haren's protection or guilt.

## Competence
Closed ecology, microbial balance, nutrient cycling, water budgeting, and later Ross 128 b settlement ecology.

## Independent mistake — required canon candidate
During Act II, Yuna advocates expanding an H2 closed-ecology/water-cycle pilot after the early stability window looks good.

Her reasoning is technically defensible:

- short-term nutrient balance is stable,
- major species metrics remain within tolerance,
- the next scaling step is already part of the approved research path.

She nevertheless underweights a slow microbial/chemical feedback that the conservative team wanted more time to observe.

### Result
- contained testbed instability,
- significant crop/microbial-stock loss,
- months of reset work,
- no deaths,
- no civilization-scale crisis.

### Mandatory response
Yuna reports the failure herself before it can be politically softened.

She accepts:
- project delay,
- temporary loss of expansion authority,
- review of her model assumptions,
- responsibility to help rebuild the testbed.

Haren does not rescue her from the consequence.

## Character meaning
Her autonomy arc is therefore not:

> stop Haren from controlling me.

It becomes:

> I have the right to choose, and that right includes owning the consequences of my choices.

## Later effect
In Act V–VII, Yuna becomes one of the strongest opponents of reckless planet-scale water release/terraforming precisely because she has personally experienced delayed ecological feedback.

She argues for regional closed systems and staged water transfer, not because Haren taught her caution.

## Haren relationship

### Early
Affection mixed with irritation at being treated as the person he once saved.

### Middle
The falsification discovery hurts because Haren changed her legal history without allowing future-adult Yuna any way to own the story.

### Required decision
Yuna controls correction of her own record and what medical details become public.

### Final
She accepts a surface-assignment risk Haren would not choose for her.

Haren may advise; he may not veto.

# 4. Tessa Nor — Material / Systems Integration Counterpart

## Independent ambition
Tessa wants to become lead architect of the **multi-world insertion and material-flow network** that turns one generation ship into several viable settlements.

Her goal is not promotion for prestige.

Her professional thesis is:

> A civilization's promises are only real if mass, heat, labor, energy, reserve, and timing close at the same time.

## Competence
- mass-flow accounting,
- fabrication scheduling,
- transit capacity,
- heat/rejection margins,
- coupled system dependencies.

## Relationships independent of Haren

### Sara Kor
Senior material auditor / mentor-rival.

Sara repeatedly challenges Tessa's tendency to treat a fully balanced model as evidence that implementation is ready.

### Jona Mer
Fabrication labor organizer.

Jona forces Tessa to confront the fact that labor capacity is not interchangeable with nominal labor-hours. Skill, fatigue, trust, shift culture, and family obligations change what a schedule can actually deliver.

At least two important Tessa decisions must occur with Haren absent and involve Sara and/or Jona.

## Independent mistake
ARC 35's physically valid cross-habitat transition model is partly Tessa's.

The model closes on:
- material mass,
- transport throughput,
- heat margins,
- fabrication windows.

It fails in execution because informal care/labor dependencies were incompletely represented.

### Required response
Tessa publicly owns the model failure.

She does not blame Haren for supplying bad social data.

She helps redesign the planning standard so models carry explicit uncertainty for human dependency networks rather than pretending all dependencies can be converted into precise numbers.

## Independent success
In Act VII, one major conversion/insertion segment succeeds under Tessa and local teams while Haren is physically elsewhere coordinating another front.

This success must be narratively visible.

## Final state
Tessa remains more materially conservative than Haren. She does not adopt his worldview; their methods become interoperable.

# 5. Elin Vess — Neural Rehabilitation / Continuity Medicine Specialist

## Independent goal
Design an arrival-era continuity-medicine protocol that protects people moving between radically different living conditions without normalizing compulsory cognitive capture.

## Competence
- neurorehabilitation,
- continuity capture counseling,
- sensory/motor adaptation,
- altered-gravity transition medicine,
- restoration-consent practice.

## Independent professional problem
Arrival creates new risks:

- rotating-habitat to low/variable gravity transfer,
- surface gravity adaptation,
- high-risk external work,
- delayed access to restoration/capture infrastructure,
- families splitting across worlds.

The medical establishment wants stronger pre-transition capture recommendations.

Elin agrees that preparation matters but resists turning recommendation into de facto obligation.

## Blind spot / mistake
Elin overcorrects against coercion.

In a difficult case, she protects a patient's refusal so strongly that the team delays making the practical burden explicit to family/caregivers/work partners.

The patient's refusal right remains valid.

The problem is not that Elin should have overridden consent.

The failure is that she treated:

> respect for choice

as if it required

> avoiding hard discussion of who will carry the consequences.

## Growth
Elin develops a stronger consent practice:

- choice remains with the person,
- foreseeable burdens are made explicit,
- affected people may state limits,
- support cannot be silently assumed.

## Haren relationship endpoint
The old relationship is not restored by nostalgia or crisis.

Working final endpoint:

- Haren and Elin deliberately choose to begin a **new** adult relationship during/after the final transition,
- neither gives up their professional domain,
- they may spend long periods on different worlds,
- Elin states what she wants rather than waiting for Haren to infer it,
- Haren asks rather than planning a future on her behalf.

This is a chosen resolution, not a theorem that “love proves AI people are real.”

# 6. Toma Venn — Senior Transition Administrator / Mentor

## Independent goal
Keep shipwide transition institutions legitimate enough that weak communities are not abandoned by whichever local groups have the most political leverage.

## Competence
- procedural legitimacy,
- cross-habitat guarantees,
- institutional memory,
- emergency sequencing,
- common-floor policy.

## Blind spot
Toma believes careful sequencing of information is sometimes itself a form of protection.

That is often true, which makes his mistake dangerous rather than foolish.

## Autonomous information-control mistake
During Act III, Toma receives an authenticated historical crosswalk / mission-service key that would help explain the relation between modern `non-seeded developmental route` data and an old launch-era human-origin category.

At the same time, a live medical/service transition is underway and partial release could enable re-identification of rare patients.

Toma delays publication long enough to establish a safer release package.

### Why the decision is defensible
- privacy risk is real,
- live services could be disrupted,
- provenance rumors are already harming patients.

### Cost
During the delay:
- false candidate criteria spread,
- unofficial interpretations dominate public discussion,
- institutional trust falls when the delay is later discovered,
- critics can fairly say the administration again decided what citizens were ready to know.

## Consequence
Toma loses unilateral information-release control and accepts external/public-interest oversight.

He remains in service; he is neither villainized nor absolved.

## Final agency
Toma later supports a minimum common personhood/safety floor even though doing so permanently reduces H1 central institutional control.

## Final state
He remains convinced that civilization needs institutions and universal guarantees. The story does not prove him obsolete.

# 7. Kian Rel — Habitat Autonomy Organizer / Political Rival

## Independent goal
Force transition planning to treat local communities as decision-makers rather than datasets whose consent can be optimized after the fact.

## Competence
- coalition building,
- local representation,
- public process,
- identifying hidden power asymmetries in technically neutral plans.

## Blind spot
Kian can make externalized costs politically invisible when defending the legitimacy of one community's refusal.

## Self-caused cost
During ARC 29, Kian helps a habitat coalition win a legitimate refusal/delay against an arrival-conversion process whose consultation was not genuine consent.

### The victory is real
It prevents a coercive precedent and forces redesign.

### The cost is also real
The delay consumes:
- a scarce fabrication window,
- thermal/transport margin,
- scheduling flexibility.

As a result, another habitat loses a preferred conversion option and must accept a more disruptive later transition.

No deaths occur because Kian won.

But the opportunity cost is visible and politically owned.

## Required response
Kian publicly acknowledges that local self-determination cannot mean exporting cost to unseen communities without representation.

He does **not** retract his refusal victory.

Instead he develops a stronger doctrine:

> local veto or consent must include a transparent account of systemwide externalities and a process for the people who will bear them.

## Final position
Kian remains a strong autonomy advocate and constitutional opponent of excessive central authority.

He never becomes Haren's lieutenant.

# 10. Act VII Distributed-Agency Requirement

The finale must show at least four important operations succeeding without Haren personally controlling them.

Minimum required examples:

1. **Tessa + local operations teams** complete a material/transport conversion segment.
2. **Yuna + ecological team** authorize or halt a surface ecological step based on live evidence.
3. **Elin + medical transition network** manage a continuity/adaptation decision without Haren's intervention.
4. **Kian / local representatives / shared authority** resolve one consent/externality conflict through the new process.

Haren remains important because he helped create coordination architecture, not because everything stops when he leaves the room.

# 11. Character Anti-Functionality Check

A core character fails later QA if any rolling ~40-episode span reduces them primarily to one of these functions:

- Haren's conscience,
- exposition source,
- ideological debater,
- romantic reward,
- competence sidekick,
- person to rescue,
- secret-holder.

Each return should advance at least one of:

- their independent work,
- their own relationship network,
- their own mistake/consequence,
- their political/community obligation,
- their future choice.
