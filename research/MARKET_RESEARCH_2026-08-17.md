# Current SF / Web-Novel Market Research

Date: 2026-08-17
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》
Status: RESEARCH INPUT — NOT CANON

## 1. Executive Finding

The Korean web-novel market does **not** imply that a philosophical generation-ship mystery is commercially impossible. It does imply that presenting the project as “pure philosophical SF” without an immediately repeatable story engine would be high risk.

The strongest practical conclusion is:

> Keep the one-line SF hook, but serialize through concrete competence, cases, relationships, social consequences, exploration, and recurring local payoffs rather than through philosophy lectures or prolonged clue withholding.

## 2. 2026 Munpia SF Snapshot

Munpia’s SF-category paid listings in July 2026 show that visible commercial SF is heavily hybridized with familiar serial-fiction engines.

Examples visible in the current SF paid list include:

- `반지성주의 마왕의 세계침략` — Fantasy + SF, 600+ episodes
- `사이버펑크 협객전` — Martial arts + SF, 300+ episodes
- `21세기 로봇공학자가 출신을 숨김` — Fantasy + SF, profession/competence hook
- `천재 기갑파일럿으로 살아남는 법` — Modern fantasy + SF, skill/survival hook
- `나를 위한 전쟁은 어디에 있나` — SF + military
- `초격차 문명 관리자가 되었다` — Modern fantasy + SF, civilization-management engine
- `우주전함으로 지구를 내 손 안에` — Modern fantasy + SF, ownership/power engine
- `고철장의 천재공학자, 우주 영주가 됨` — engineering + territory-growth engine

This is not proof that other forms cannot work. It is a strong signal that **SF on Munpia gains traction when the reader can instantly understand the protagonist’s repeatable activity and reward loop**.

The free SF list shows a similar pattern. `미래수선전` is a strong hybrid outlier, while many more purely labeled SF works have much smaller current view/favorite counts.

### Design implication

Our project should **not** bolt on levels, mecha progression, or a war-power fantasy just to imitate the ranking. Instead it must develop an equally legible non-combat engine, such as:

- professional competence
- socially consequential cases
- district/world exploration
- identity/death/family disputes
- institutional problem solving
- relationship decisions
- ship-system crises

The hook attracts the click; the engine earns episode 30+.

## 3. Current Web-Novel Competition / IP Environment

Naver Webtoon and Munpia reported that the 2026 `지상최대 웹소설 공모전` received more than 10,000 works, roughly 35% more than the previous year, with about 7,400 participating writers and roughly 6,600 writers without prior paid-serialization experience.

The same program explicitly connects winning novels to Naver Series distribution, promotion, webtoon opportunities for top winners, and OSMU review including video adaptation.

### Design implication

The project should be designed not only as text mystery but as an **IP-readable world**:

- visually distinct ship regions
- memorable professions and institutions
- character silhouettes and social identities
- repeatable dramatic units
- adaptation-friendly physical stakes
- mysteries whose clues can be visually represented

## 5. Commercial Strengths of This Project

### 5.1 Extremely clear one-line hook

`30만 명이 사는 세대우주선. 생물학적 인간은 단 한 명뿐이다.`

This is immediately understandable without lore.

### 5.2 Built-in uncertainty

The protagonist cannot prove they are the one human. Therefore the premise is not exhausted merely by revealing “there is one human.”

### 5.3 Strong relationship potential

The premise naturally attacks family, love, parenthood, death, inheritance, religion, and social recognition.

### 5.4 Strong IP geography potential

A ship of 300,000 people can function as a continent-scale closed world with visually and culturally distinct regions.

### 5.5 High recontextualization potential

Ordinary childhood, medicine, funerals, pregnancy, family photos, school records, and inheritance can all change meaning after later reveals.

## 6. Commercial Weaknesses / Risks

### 6.1 No obvious power-growth loop

Unlike current ranking-friendly works, the premise alone does not state what the protagonist does every episode.

**Required fix:** protagonist profession + competence engine must be selected before macro plotting.

### 6.2 Mystery-stall risk

If the series becomes “investigate who is human” for 100 episodes, reader trust will collapse.

**Required fix:** local questions must regularly receive real answers, and answers must create new social/relationship problems.

### 6.3 Emotional-distance risk

If 299,999 citizens are framed as “fake,” readers may detach from them.

**Required fix:** establish valuable relationships **before** ontological labels become dominant; AI status must not erase accumulated emotional reality.

### 6.4 Philosophy-dialogue risk

The premise invites abstract debate.

**Required fix:** every major philosophical question must first appear as a decision with a cost: restore/not restore, disclose/conceal, recognize/reject, inherit/disinherit, parent/not-parent.

### 6.5 SF-exposition risk

Birth, embodiment, compute, and medical detection can create explanation dumps.

**Required fix:** rules are learned through work, conflict, medicine, law, family, and emergencies.

### 6.6 100+ episode ceiling risk

The “one human” revelation itself is only a premise, not a 250-episode plot.

**Required fix:** the long series must have at least five independent engines and multiple mystery layers whose resolutions alter society rather than merely add lore.

## 7. Positioning Recommendation

Do **not** market internally as:

`철학 SF + 인간이란 무엇인가`

Design toward:

`폐쇄세계 사회 미스터리 + 직업/사건 해결 + 관계 드라마 + 세대우주선 탐험`

with the philosophical questions emerging from consequences.

A working commercial promise might be described internally as:

> Every district believes it knows what a human life is worth. The protagonist’s work keeps forcing those definitions to break.

This is not a final logline; it is a design criterion.

## 8. Market-Derived Requirements for Four-Direction Designs

Every candidate direction must answer:

1. What does the protagonist **do** repeatedly?
2. What can be completed/rewarded within 1–3 episodes?
3. What completes within 5–15 episodes?
4. What changes at Sub-Act scale?
5. Why can the same engine generate different stories after episode 50?
6. How does the engine expose ship society without lore dumps?
7. How does it produce relationship consequences?
8. How does it remain interesting even if the reader already suspects the main premise?
9. What is the non-combat competence pleasure?
10. What makes the series visually distinctive enough for adaptation?
