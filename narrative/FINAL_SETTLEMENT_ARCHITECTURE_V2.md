# 1. Central Revision

V1 proposed a 4/4 split between planet-associated and star-centric large habitats.

Orbital QA improves this to:

## Planet-associated large habitat worlds
- **H2 Biosphere Commons**
- **H4 Kinship Belt**
- **H5 Exchange Ring**

## Ross 128 star-centric / co-orbital large habitat worlds
- **H1 Civic Core**
- **H3 Fabrication Concord**
- **H6 Memory Commons**
- **H7 Low-Link Communities**
- **H8 Legacy Habitat**

This is now the preferred final physical map.

---

# 2. Why H1 Moves Outward

H1 does not need to sit physically closest to Ross 128 b merely because it began as the ship's administrative center.

Moving H1 into the wider star-centric network improves:

- orbital congestion
- political decentralization
- symbolic balance
- physical separation among new worlds

H1 still hosts important institutions, but its authority becomes networked.

Surface and planetary-orbit citizens do not literally live beneath the old capital.

# 7. Political Geometry

The final geography has **no single center**.

Three scales coexist:

1. planetary surface settlements
2. close planet-orbit habitat worlds
3. wider star-centric habitat worlds

Shared compact functions cross all three.

This physically supports the ending principle:

> difference and distance may remain without becoming hierarchy.

---

# 8. Travel Hierarchy

## Surface ↔ H5/H2/H4
Scheduled high-energy trips, generally hours-scale flight/transfer plus processing.

## Planet-bound ↔ wider star-centric worlds
Longer scheduled inter-world trips, potentially hours to days depending orbital phase and vehicle.

## Data/communications
Seconds-to-tens-of-seconds scale at most for likely settlement separations.

Thus:
- information civilization remains integrated
- bodies, goods, families, and institutions experience real geographic distance

---

# 9. No Geostationary Capital

Because the planet-synchronous orbital radius lies beyond the useful Hill sphere, no large passive station remains fixed over SR-A.

Communications use constellations.

Large habitats move through the sky.

This creates a recurring late-series visual:

> the worlds that carried civilization between stars now visibly cross above the new settlements, but none owns the sky permanently.
