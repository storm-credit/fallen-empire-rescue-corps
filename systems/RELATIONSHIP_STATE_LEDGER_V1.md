# Relationship State Ledger v1

Status: PROVISIONAL MACRO QA
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. State Axes

Each major relationship is tracked across:

- **Trust** — can they rely on each other?
- **Knowledge** — what does each know about the other/world?
- **Power** — who can materially affect whom?
- **Obligation** — what do they feel they owe?
- **Intimacy/Distance** — emotional accessibility, not romance only
- **Public Alignment** — are they seen as allies/opponents?

A relationship may revisit an issue only if at least one axis has changed.

# R1 — Parent / Home Anchor

## ACT I
State:
- trust high but friction familiar
- protagonist has professional authority over a transition affecting people like the parent
- parent sees protagonist as someone who mistakes functional equivalence for continuity

Change:
Parent refuses an efficient solution and is partly proven right.

## ACT II
State:
- protagonist begins treating origin as destabilizing
- parent refuses to let shared history become “evidence to verify”

Change:
Distance rises because protagonist intellectualizes fear.

## ACT III
State:
- public artificial-origin truth reframes family history
- parent has no secret answer

Choice payoff:
Parent refuses both “nothing matters” and “our life was fake.”

## ACT IV
State:
- protagonist confirmed seeded-origin
- parent becomes emotionally steadier than protagonist expected

Power reversal:
Protagonist needs ordinary familial recognition more than parent needs technical explanation.

## ACT V
State:
- parent/community faces mission-linked transition pressure
- disagreement shifts from ontology to future/home

## ACT VI
Major payoff:
A beloved place cannot survive intact.

Both change:
- protagonist admits functional replica is insufficient
- parent admits preserving everything can externalize costs to others

Relationship outcome:
more honest, less idealized; continuity chosen together.

## ACT VII
Final echo only.
No new parent conflict.
Parent's chosen future may differ from protagonist's preferred future.

# R2 — Younger Family / Former Protected Person

## ACT I
State:
- protagonist still unconsciously treats them as someone whose safety must be managed
- protected person resents this role

## ACT II
Change:
They make a small independent choice protagonist dislikes.

## ACT III
Knowledge pressure:
Public provenance debates make records/identity personally salient.
They begin questioning old administrative history around them.

## ACT IV-A/B
Power shift:
They gain evidence/access that makes protagonist's old falsification vulnerable.

## ACT IV-D
Major rupture/payoff:
Falsification exposed.

The protected person defines the meaning—not protagonist.
Possible mixed response:
- acknowledges it helped
- rejects the right protagonist assumed
- demands control over correction/disclosure

## ACT V
Independent path:
They choose an education/job/habitat/settlement position not organized around protagonist.

## ACT VI
Relationship can partially repair only through protagonist accepting non-control.

## ACT VII
Final payoff:
Their settlement choice is genuinely theirs, even if protagonist believes another option is safer.

# R3 — Closest Colleague / Material Counterpart

## ACT I
State:
Competence chemistry; light friction.

## ACT II
Trust grows through practical work rather than confession.
Colleague treats ontology panic with grounded concern, not mockery.

## ACT III
Public truth creates work overload/material side effects.
Colleague becomes more politically aware.

## ACT IV
Baseline medical/resource exceptions force first serious equal-dignity/unequal-needs debate.

## ACT V-B
Major reversal:
Protagonist's socially elegant plan fails because material budget/heat/mass assumptions are wrong.
Colleague was right.

Trust survives because colleague helps fix rather than gloat.

## ACT VI
Inverse reversal:
Colleague's optimized model fails because it breaks hidden care/community dependencies.
Protagonist was right.

## ACT VII
Synthesis:
They jointly execute transition using coupled social/material models.
No need for ideological merger; division of expertise remains.

# R4 — Intimate / Love-Level Relationship

Working shape: unresolved past or interrupted relationship, not instant destiny.

## ACT I
Establish:
- genuine chemistry/history
- current incompatibility around protagonist using duty to avoid commitment
- relationship matters even if central mystery vanished

## ACT II
Origin anxiety creates temptation for protagonist to seek reassurance.
Other person refuses to become an identity certificate.

## ACT III
Public truth creates professional/political strain distinct from romantic jealousy.

## ACT IV
Protagonist seeded-origin confirmation creates recontextualization but not automatic breakup/union.

Required choice:
One person must choose the other at a cost unrelated to “proving AI can love.”

## ACT V
Arrival future creates concrete compatibility question:
- habitat vs planetary future
- career duty
- restoration/recording preference
- family/reproduction plans

## ACT VI
Mortality/restoration issue forces explicit continuity expectations.

Relationship reaches chosen status:
- committed continuation,
- chosen separation,
- or another explicit durable form.

No unresolved romantic tease into final pages.

## ACT VII
Final echo through action/presence only.

# R5 — Mentor / Superior

## ACT I
State:
High professional respect.
Mentor represents competent, legitimate procedure.

## ACT II
Mentor protects protagonist from premature institutional overreaction while insisting rules matter.

## ACT III-B
First major fracture:
Mentor delays/restricts a truth release for defensible operational reasons.
Protagonist sees paternalism; mentor sees cascading harm.

## ACT IV-D
Protagonist falsification forces mentor to apply procedure personally.
Mentor cannot simply excuse protagonist.

This is important: institutional legitimacy must cost the protagonist too.

## ACT V
Mission/charter debates widen disagreement.
Mentor fears fragmented local veto will abandon weak communities.

## ACT VI
Open opposition on transition authority design.
Then partial synthesis:
- transparent constraints
- representation
- enforceable minimum guarantees
- real local choice within physical bounds

## ACT VII
Mentor's strongest institutional principle survives in redesigned system.
Mentor need not remain protagonist's superior.

# R6 — Rival / Local Self-Determination Advocate

## ACT I
Introduction as credible obstacle to transition plan.

## ACT II
Mutual respect grows after rival exposes a dependency protagonist missed or protagonist prevents harm rival underestimated.

## ACT III
Truth-disclosure politics split them again.
Rival prioritizes affected people's agency over administrative timing.

## ACT IV
Potential temporary alliance around BH-1 privacy.
The alliance must not erase broader disagreement.

## ACT V-D
Rival wins a major legitimate conflict:
A protagonist-designed process is rejected because participation was not meaningful enough.

Protagonist changes.

## ACT VI
Rival confronts limits of local autonomy when one community's refusal transfers lethal/material burden to another.

Rival changes too.

## ACT VII
They cooperate on plural transition architecture while still disagreeing over some boundary decisions.
No conversion into sidekick.

# R7 — Older Civilian / Community Memory Anchor

## ACT I
Introduced through an ordinary local institution threatened by transition.

## ACT II
Provides lived context that contradicts overly neat official social models.
No central-secret exposition.

## ACT III
Old memories acquire new meaning after mixed-family history emerges.
Some remembered details are proven wrong.
This protects against “wise elder knows everything.”

## ACT IV
Their community reacts to provenance truth in internally divided ways.

## ACT V
Arrival planning threatens or transforms the institution they care about.

## ACT VI
They participate in deciding what element of community continuity is worth carrying forward.
Could die, remain, move, or refuse—do not choose merely for tragedy.

## ACT VII
Their memory/institution can persist in transformed form without turning into museum nostalgia.

# 2. Cross-Relationship Collision Requirements

Relationships must interact with each other rather than exist as seven separate lanes.

Required collisions:

1. **Parent ↔ Younger family**
   - may disagree about whether protagonist's old protection was justified.

2. **Colleague ↔ Rival**
   - local autonomy collides with material accounting.

3. **Mentor ↔ Rival**
   - strongest institutional vs participatory arguments confront directly.

4. **Intimate relation ↔ Protagonist family**
   - at least one non-mystery history/commitment tension.

5. **BH-1 ↔ multiple relationships**
   - BH-1 cannot exist only in protagonist lane; their choices affect medical, family, political, and professional networks.

# 3. Anti-Repetition Flags

RED FLAG if any relationship spends >2 major beats in the same state pattern.

Examples:

- Parent: `refuses relocation` cannot repeat without changed stakes/agency.
- Younger family: `angry about protection` cannot be sole emotional mode.
- Intimate: `do you trust/love me despite origin?` may occur once at most as explicit issue.
- Mentor: cannot repeatedly “hide one more file.”
- Rival: cannot repeatedly block projects then be proven wrong.
- Colleague: cannot function only as numbers/exposition.
