# VI-A — THE PRICE OF KEEPING HOME
## EP191–202

### ARC31 — The Price of Keeping Home
EP191–196

Primary: E5 Material Constraint
Secondary: E4 Relationship / E3 H8 / E8 Arrival

Question:
Can H8 Old Transfer District remain physically intact inside the new broader-system H8 role?

Required result:
Full preservation is technically possible only by transferring substantial maintenance/mass/thermal/crew cost from other current/future communities.

No villain wants to bulldoze H8.
No magic preservation solution appears.

### ARC32 — What the Neighborhood Chooses to Carry
EP197–202

Primary: E1 Community Transition
Secondary: E3/E4/E5

H8 itself decides what must be carried:
- people/care networks
- repair/apprenticeship practices

- original route geometry

By EP202 the original Old Transfer District has crossed a physical point of no return.

This is the required beloved-place loss.

# VI-B — THE RIGHT TO REFUSE A FUTURE SELF
## EP203–213

### ARC33 — The Right to Refuse a Future Self
EP203–208

Primary: E2 Continuity/Consent
Secondary: E7 Labor / E5 Arrival Risk / E4 Elin-Daria

Preferred recurring case:
**Daria Tov** applies for a high-risk arrival-construction/commissioning role.

The program proposes mandatory fresh continuity capture for high-risk personnel because loss of scarce specialists would threaten mission operations.

Daria, herself previously restored, refuses compulsory capture as a condition of service.

Important:
She is not anti-restoration.
She defends access to restoration while insisting creation/use of a future claimant must remain consensual.

The conflict is not solved by saying `rights beat safety` or `safety beats rights`.

### ARC34 — Fairness Has a Capacity Limit
EP209–213

The alternative to compulsory capture requires real capacity:
- more trained redundancy
- rescue/evacuation margin
- medical support
- replacement staffing
- schedule reserve

Only a limited number of such accommodations can be supported in the first high-risk cohorts.

Required result:
A common right to refuse capture survives, but it does not guarantee access to every scarce role regardless of operational capacity.

Eligibility must use current task risk and transparent accommodation limits—not provenance, wealth, prestige, or ideology.

This creates a fair but unsatisfying capacity rule.

# VI-C — THE MOVE THAT BREAKS THE MODEL
## EP214–219

### ARC35 — The Move That Breaks the Model

Primary: E1 Cross-Habitat Transition Execution
Secondary: E5 Material / E4 Haren-Tessa

This is distinct from Act V standardization failure.

The plan is physically valid and already respects different habitat architectures.

Failure source:
The synchronized staging move models formal care/labor/service dependencies well, including Haren's methods, but still misses:
- informal shift substitution
- unregistered caregiving
- apprenticeship know-how
- social trust links
- people who serve as cross-system bridges without formal titles

The failure occurs in live execution.

No mass casualty needed.

Required character consequences:
- Tessa publicly owns the execution model she helped build.
- Haren owns that `social dependency modeling` also became overconfident.
- neither blames local people for failing to fit the model.

New method:
- local validation
- uncertainty ranges
- buffers
- explicit `unknown dependency` reserve
- authority to stop/resequence locally during execution

# VI-D — WHO COORDINATES THE IRREVERSIBLE
## EP220–225

### ARC36 — Who Coordinates the Irreversible

Primary: E7 Shared Governance
Secondary: E8/E1/E5

Problem:
Final conversion now requires simultaneous decisions across:
- rights/consent
- local autonomy
- externalized cost
- material feasibility
- common safety floor
- rapid execution

No existing single office should own all of this.

Required solution:
Create the **Multi-World Transition Coordination** architecture (final name flexible):

- political/rights bodies decide legitimate option boundaries
- habitats/local representatives decide within real local scope
- material/technical teams publish constraints and execute
- external cost-bearers receive representation
- shared emergency rules preserve common safety
- one coordination function sequences decisions without becoming sovereign

Haren may serve as a high-level coordinator because his competence is useful, but:
- he is not sole final authority
- no unilateral signature returns
- local teams can stop/resequence within defined scope
- critical operations can and will succeed without him in Act VII

Act exit:
The civilization has a method capable of executing the final transition without pretending one person or one model can see everything.
