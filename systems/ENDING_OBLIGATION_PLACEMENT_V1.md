# 4. Character Obligations That Must Not Wait for Finale

Main emotional closure should begin before EP226:

### By ACT IV
- protagonist stops hinging self-worth on being BH
- younger-family falsification is exposed
- BH-1 becomes a person with agency, not just mystery target

### By ACT V
- rival has defeated protagonist on legitimate process grounds
- colleague has proven a material blind spot
- mentor/protagonist disagreement is explicit

### By ACT VI
- parent/home conflict receives its major payoff
- mortality/restoration consent receives durable answer
- intimate relationship reaches a chosen post-truth state or a decisive near-final state
- mentor/rival/colleague synthesis is operational, not merely discussed

ACT VII may echo these; it should not introduce their actual emotional problem for the first time.

# 6. Risk Flags

## RISK A — Too many final-law payoffs

Many obligations naturally touch law.

Countermeasure:
Do not resolve them all in one constitutional speech/document.

Show resolution through different mechanisms:
- medical consent rule
- court precedent
- professional procedure
- habitat compact
- resource budget
- family choice
- archival access policy
- settlement allocation

## RISK B — ACT VI overloaded emotionally

ACT VI carries parent/home, restoration, mentor/rival/colleague synthesis.

Countermeasure:
These must be distributed across three separate Sub-Acts with different primary engines and temperatures.

## RISK C — BH-1 becomes passive symbol

Countermeasure:
BH-1 must make at least three decisions that frustrate major factions and at least one that frustrates protagonist.

## RISK D — protagonist secret feels tiny after cosmic reveals

Countermeasure:
The classification falsification must materially affect:
- career legitimacy
- younger family autonomy
- faction criticism
- final transition ethics

Its thematic weight, not world-scale secrecy, makes it important.
