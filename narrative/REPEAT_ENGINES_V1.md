# Durable Repeat Engines v1

Status: PROVISIONAL — LONG-FORM STRESS TEST
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Core Rule

The premise is **not** the repeat engine.

`Only one biological human exists` is a destabilizing fact.

A 150–250 episode series requires systems that produce satisfying stories:

- before that fact is known
- while it is being discovered
- after it becomes public
- after the sole person’s identity is known

Every engine below is tested against those phases.

---

# E1 — HABITAT TRANSITION PROJECT
## Anchor professional engine

### Core loop

`A district/system must change → map real dependencies → negotiate competing needs → redesign transition → execute → something survives / something is lost → consequences persist`

### What changes each time

Transition type can vary:

- structural overhaul
- temporary evacuation
- district merger
- school/clinic consolidation
- transport rerouting
- ecological quarantine
- industrial conversion
- population redistribution
- arrival preparation
- post-disaster rebuilding

### 1–3 episode reward

A clever social/logistical solution works.

### 5–15 episode payoff

A community transition completes and permanently changes the map.

### Long-term consequence

Previously moved populations reappear elsewhere, affecting elections, schools, labor, family networks, and later crises.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Excellent**
- Truth emerging: **Excellent** — identity categories break relocation rules
- Truth public: **Excellent** — communities disagree about classification/protection
- Sole identity known: **Excellent** — political pressure changes but transition work continues

### Primary risk

Could become repetitive “save neighborhood” stories.

### Rotation rule

Never use the same combination of transition type + dominant conflict + habitat type twice consecutively.

---

# E2 — PERSONHOOD / CONTINUITY CLAIM
## Decision engine

### Core loop

`A real-life status dispute appears → existing categories produce incompatible claims → protagonist must make or influence a binding practical decision → someone gains continuity → someone pays the cost`

### Case families

Not limited to restoration:

- household membership
- guardianship
- marriage/partnership continuity
- inheritance
- work certification
- relocation rights
- medical consent
- criminal/civil responsibility
- identity after memory loss
- identity after body reconstruction
- citizenship across habitat jurisdictions
- parentage
- right to refuse recording/restoration

### 1–3 episode reward

A concrete claim is resolved or narrowed.

### 5–15 episode payoff

Decision creates precedent or institutional backlash.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Excellent**
- Truth emerging: **Excellent**
- Truth public: **Excellent** — entire law becomes contested but daily cases still need decisions
- Sole identity known: **Excellent** — now law has to function under maximum symbolic pressure

### Primary risk

Legal/procedural talkiness.

### Safeguard

Every claim must be attached to a physical transition, family, job, home, body, or scarce resource. No abstract courtroom-only arcs.

---

# E3 — HABITAT SOCIETY / CULTURE COLLISION
## World-exploration engine

### Core loop

`Protagonist enters/returns to a habitat → local custom/institution collides with shipwide plan → both sides reveal valid needs → protagonist learns how this society actually functions → local resolution changes cross-habitat politics`

### Sources of variation

- death ritual
- recording/privacy
- family structure
- labor credentialing
- food/resource stewardship
- civic identity
- restoration acceptance
- education
- local autonomy
- historical memory

### Reward

The reader understands a new way of living inside the ship and sees it affect plot.

### Long-term rule

No habitat is discarded after introduction.

Later arcs must revisit prior habitats under changed circumstances.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Excellent**
- Truth emerging: **Excellent** — cultures interpret clues differently
- Truth public: **Excellent** — philosophies become political responses
- Sole identity known: **Excellent** — local responses keep diverging

### Primary risk

Travelogue.

### Safeguard

Every culture visit must force a concrete decision and leave a shipwide consequence.

---

# E4 — RELATIONSHIP OBLIGATION
## Emotional engine

### Core loop

`A person needs something the protagonist cannot solve professionally → protagonist must choose between role, honesty, loyalty, autonomy, or care → relationship state changes permanently`

### Relationship families

- parent/home
- younger family/autonomy
- colleague/resource ethics
- intimate relationship/commitment
- mentor/institutional loyalty
- rival/self-determination
- community elder/lived memory

### Reward

Emotional movement: trust earned, boundary set, care demonstrated, rupture made honest, old wound reinterpreted.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Excellent**
- Truth emerging: **Excellent**
- Truth public: **Essential**
- Sole identity known: **Essential**

### Primary risk

Relationship scenes become cooldown filler.

### Safeguard

Major relationship beats must change a future decision, not merely express feelings.

---

# E5 — MATERIAL / SYSTEM CONSTRAINT
## SF competence pressure engine

### Core loop

`Physical limit appears → multiple communities depend on incompatible outcomes → technical/logistical diagnosis → sacrifice/priority choice → system stabilized → social cost remains`

### Constraint families

- heat rejection
- water/nutrient loss
- structural fatigue
- transit chokepoint
- power outage
- agricultural contamination
- material shortage
- data-network failure
- low-gravity transfer accident
- exterior maintenance window

### Reward

Visible competence, rescue, repair, clever allocation, physical SF spectacle.

### Phase durability

Independent of ontology reveal: **Excellent at all phases**.

### Primary risk

Disaster-of-the-week / bigger-explosion escalation.

### Safeguard

Use sparingly. Escalate meaning, dependency, or moral cost—not only physical scale.

---

# E6 — HISTORICAL LAYER / INFRASTRUCTURE ARCHAEOLOGY
## Past-to-present engine

### Core loop

`Current project encounters obsolete physical/data/social layer → fragment conflicts with official assumptions → practical problem must be solved first → historical implication remains → later evidence recontextualizes it`

### Evidence types

- old architecture
- obsolete medical fixtures
- legal category remnants
- neighborhood ritual
- repurposed equipment
- erased terminology
- genealogical irregularity
- maintenance standard mismatch
- sealed registry fragments
- transition-era civic design

### Reward

A real historical question gets a partial or full answer—not merely another clue.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Strong**
- Truth emerging: **Excellent**
- Truth public: **Strong** — focus shifts from “what happened?” to “who did what, why, and what obligations remain?”
- Sole identity known: **Strong**

### Primary risk

Every old wall contains a secret.

### Safeguard

Most old infrastructure is ordinary. Historical discoveries emerge when they are relevant to present work.

---

# E7 — FACTION AUTONOMOUS MOVE
## Political engine

### Core loop

`A faction pursues its own goal → move alters law/resources/public opinion → protagonist’s current project becomes harder/easier → counter-coalitions form → political map changes`

### Examples of move types

- court challenge
- policy campaign
- strike
- archive release
- restoration-rights initiative
- privacy refusal
- resource-budget veto
- habitat autonomy referendum
- arrival charter amendment
- professional boycott

### Reward

The world visibly moves without waiting for protagonist discovery.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Excellent**
- Truth emerging: **Excellent**
- Truth public: **Critical**
- Sole identity known: **Critical**

### Primary risk

Characters become ideology mouthpieces.

### Safeguard

Faction moves must have material interests, internal dissent, and named people whose lives are affected.

---

# E8 — ARRIVAL / VOYAGE DECISION
## Long-horizon pressure engine

### Core loop

`New destination data or arrival requirement becomes actionable → current society must spend resources or change institutions now → factions disagree over who owes what to future settlement → partial decision commits the ship further`

### Possible issue families

- habitat conversion for settlement equipment
- reserve allocation
- population skills/certification
- ecological compatibility
- landing/settlement cohort rules
- charter revision
- whether to alter route/timetable
- ship-preservation vs settlement investment

### Reward

The destination becomes an active present-tense force rather than final-act lore.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Moderate**
- Truth emerging: **Strong**
- Truth public: **Excellent**
- Sole identity known: **Excellent**

### Primary risk

Prematurely making destination bureaucracy dominate the story.

### Safeguard

Use at low frequency early, increasing only when destination decisions become irreversible.

---

# E9 — PUBLIC NARRATIVE / INFORMATION CASCADE
## Information-social engine

### Core loop

`A fragment of information becomes public → different groups interpret it through existing beliefs → rumors/media/archives reshape behavior → protagonist must act in altered social reality → correction cannot simply restore old state`

### Why separate from mystery

Mystery tracks what is true.

This engine tracks **what people do with what they think is true**.

### Event families

- leaked relocation model
- falsified identity rumor
- restoration controversy
- archival release
- manipulated family story
- biological-origin rumor
- public misreading of medical data

### Reward

Social consequence occurs now, even before factual certainty.

### Phase durability

- Pre-truth: **Strong**
- Truth emerging: **Critical**
- Truth public: **Critical**
- Sole identity known: **Critical**

### Primary risk

Endless social-media noise.

### Safeguard

Every information cascade must alter behavior, law, resource use, safety, or relationships.

---

# 2. Engine Combination Rule

A typical Arc should have:

- **1 Primary Engine**
- **1–2 Secondary Engines**
- other systems only in background

Do not run all nine at equal intensity.

Example signatures:

`E1 primary + E4 + E5`

`E2 primary + E3 + E7`

`E6 primary + E1 + E4`

`E7 primary + E9 + E2`

`E8 primary + E5 + E7`

# 3. 20-Episode Pre-Reveal Stress Test

Purpose: prove the series can function before using the headline twist as fuel.

This is **not** a final episode outline. It is an engine test only.

## Test Arc A — A district relocation that should be easy

Primary: E1
Secondary: E4, E3

Question:
Why do apparently equivalent households refuse identical relocation offers?

Payoff:
Protagonist discovers an invisible caregiving/community dependency and redesigns the move.

No AI/human clue required.

---

## Test Arc B — Identity record conflict created by the move

Primary: E2
Secondary: E4

Question:
Two legally valid records imply incompatible household/guardianship rights.

Payoff:
A practical ruling is made; protagonist’s old falsification secret becomes emotionally relevant without being exposed.

No central mystery required.

---

## Test Arc C — Material shortage changes the approved plan

Primary: E5
Secondary: E1, E7

Question:
Which part of the project loses scarce fabrication capacity?

Payoff:
Technical/logistical solution saves the transition but creates a political loser.

No central mystery required.

---

## Test Arc D — Return consequence

Primary: E4
Secondary: E7, E3

Question:
The protagonist’s own family/community is placed under a later transition review.

Payoff:
Professional principle collides with home; parent refuses to be “optimized.”

No central mystery required.

---

## Test Arc E — First historical seam

Primary: E6
Secondary: E1

Question:
An obsolete classification prevents a necessary infrastructure handoff.

Payoff:
The practical problem is solved and the obsolete category receives a plausible local explanation.

Only a **small anomaly** points toward the larger history.

---

## Stress-Test Verdict

The core engine can plausibly support the first ~20 episodes **without revealing the one-human premise immediately as a fully explained fact**, provided the first episode still contains a strong anomaly/promise that tells readers the series is not only a relocation procedural.

This supports a structure where:

- the headline mystery hooks early
- local professional/family stories provide immediate satisfaction
- the full ontological truth is earned rather than delayed artificially

---

# 4. Post-Reveal Stress Test

Assume society learns that almost everyone is artificial-origin.

Do engines die?

- E1 Transition: no; classification and protection rules become harder.
- E2 Claims: grows dramatically.
- E3 Habitat Society: grows because interpretations diverge.
- E4 Relationship: grows.
- E5 System Constraint: unchanged physically, changed politically.
- E6 History: question changes from existence to causation/responsibility.
- E7 Faction Moves: grows.
- E8 Arrival: becomes central.
- E9 Information Cascade: grows.

Result: **main premise disclosure does not destroy the series engine.**

---

# 5. Sole-Identity-Reveal Stress Test

Assume everyone learns exactly who the sole baseline human is.

The series still retains:

- relocation/resource conflicts
- family/personhood claims
- habitat autonomy
- restoration/death disputes
- public ownership/privacy problem around the individual
- charter/arrival interpretation
- historical accountability
- future reproduction policy
- whether baseline biology should be preserved at all

Result: **identity reveal can occur before the finale if desired.**

The story therefore does not need to keep “who is the human?” hidden until episode 240.

---

# 6. Anti-Repetition Dimensions

Each Arc later receives a signature across:

- Primary engine
- Secondary engine
- Habitat/location
- Conflict family
- Tension type
- Social scale
- Emotional relationship
- Payoff type
- Mystery intensity
- Physical-risk level
- Public/private scope

Two neighboring Arcs sharing too many dimensions trigger redesign.

---

# 7. Long-Form Pass Condition

Before macro plotting, demonstrate at least:

- 8 substantially different E1 transition premises
- 8 substantially different E2 claim types
- 6 E5 physical-system pressures without repeating disaster structure
- 8 faction moves independent of protagonist
- 6 relationship turning points not driven by secrets
- 6 history questions that receive actual answers
- 5 arrival decisions that alter present resource allocation

If this cannot be done without repetition, the project should target a shorter length rather than pad toward 250–300 episodes.
