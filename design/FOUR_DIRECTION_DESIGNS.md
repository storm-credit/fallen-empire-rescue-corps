# Four Direction Designs

Status: CANDIDATE COMPARISON — NOTHING HERE IS CANON
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## Evaluation Rule

The four designs must differ in **repeat engine**, not just mood.

Scores are provisional, 10-point scale:
- Popularity / accessibility
- Originality
- Web-novel durability
- Literary/thematic strength
- Character strength
- Adaptation potential

A high score does not automatically mean selection. Failure mode matters.

# Direction A — CONTINUITY CASE PROCEDURAL
## “When does a person legally die?”

### Core engine

The protagonist works in a civic profession responsible for difficult cases involving **death, identity continuity, restoration, inheritance, kinship, and legal personhood**.

Exact job title/mechanics are deliberately unfrozen.

Possible functional equivalents:
- death/continuity certifier
- identity continuity adjudicator
- post-accident civil examiner
- restoration-rights officer

The key is not “detective.” The protagonist’s job requires them to make **binding decisions that change people’s lives**.

### Repeat loop

`Incident → competing claims → rule collision → protagonist decision → cost → social/relationship consequence`

### 1–3 episode reward

A concrete case receives a decision or unexpected factual answer.

Examples of case *types* only:
- disputed death
- restored spouse
- contradictory inheritance claim
- parent/child identity dispute
- person legally alive but socially rejected
- memory gap and culpability

### 5–15 episode arc

A local case expands into:
- family fracture
- institutional precedent
- religious response
- labor/property dispute
- faction exploitation

### 50+ episode durability

Very high because the same ontology can generate different legal/emotional questions without repeating the same mystery.

### Main-mystery integration

The protagonist gradually discovers that the rules used to determine “same person,” “dead,” and “human” contain exceptions, inaccessible categories, or historical seams.

### Emotional engine

Strong: every abstract question has a family attached to it.

### Strengths

- theme becomes event, not lecture
- unique non-combat competence
- strong local payoffs
- naturally creates law/religion/family/politics
- excellent clue and recontextualization opportunities
- easy to make choices costly

### Main dangers

- courtroom/procedural talkiness
- case-of-the-week reset
- too much dependence on backup/restoration technology
- protagonist could become passive bureaucrat
- if every case is “same person or copy?” repetition becomes obvious

### Required safeguards

- many case families beyond restoration
- protagonist decisions must cause long-term consequences
- physical/social fieldwork, not desk-only procedure
- at least one other major engine must regularly interrupt the procedural rhythm

# Direction B — SHIP SYSTEMS CRISIS
## “Keeping 300,000 people alive reveals who the system thinks is alive.”

### Core engine

The protagonist is a high-competence field professional dealing with habitat safety, infrastructure, life support, emergency routing, or cross-district incident response.

Exact job title is unfrozen.

### Repeat loop

`Failure/anomaly → triage → physical investigation → resource choice → rescue/repair → hidden systemic inconsistency → consequence`

### 1–3 episode reward

- rescue
- repair
- diagnosis
- clever workaround
- successful evacuation
- prevention of cascading failure

### 5–15 episode arc

A local infrastructure problem reveals:
- class inequality
- hidden district practices
- undocumented populations
- conflicting resource rights
- strange system priorities
- historical modifications

### 50+ episode durability

High if crisis types vary:
- ecology
- structural integrity
- power
- transit
- data/network
- medical logistics
- population/resource mismatch
- external hull work

### Main-mystery integration

The ship’s infrastructure may encode distinctions that society no longer understands. The protagonist repeatedly encounters places where system behavior conflicts with public assumptions.

This does **not** mean a magical computer directly labels “human / AI.” Any such mechanic would need deep canon justification.

### Emotional engine

Moderate by default, strong only if every crisis is attached to recurring people and communities.

### Strengths

- strongest immediate web-novel momentum
- visible competence pleasure
- visually cinematic
- natural access to many districts/classes
- SF engineering appears through action
- can support exploration without a detective protagonist

### Main dangers

- “disaster of the week” repetition
- drifts into generic engineering/rescue SF
- physical crises could crowd out family/identity themes
- escalation may turn into bigger explosions rather than deeper meaning

### Required safeguards

- every major crisis changes a relationship/institution
- crisis types rotate
- social causes matter as much as technical causes
- no endless sabotage/conspiracy explanation

# Direction C — KINSHIP / FAMILY CIVILIZATION DRAMA
## “If everyone in my family is artificial, what exactly stops being family?”

### Core engine

The protagonist works close to family formation and social continuity rather than crime or infrastructure.

Possible functional domains, not fixed jobs:
- family/kinship services
- child welfare
- birth registration
- adoption/guardianship
- developmental care
- family mediation

The protagonist repeatedly enters the most intimate place where the society defines humanity: **who counts as someone’s child, parent, spouse, or heir**.

### Repeat loop

`Family need → hidden contradiction → conflicting love/rights → protagonist intervention → choice → relationship transformation`

### 1–3 episode reward

- child reunited/protected
- family truth clarified
- guardianship decided
- relationship repaired or honestly broken
- concrete emotional decision

### 5–15 episode arc

A family problem expands into:
- birth-policy controversy
- inheritance law
- educational classification
- medical ethics
- religion
- social stigma

### 50+ episode durability

Potentially high because family stages continuously change:
- birth
- childhood
- coming of age
- partnership
- parenthood
- illness
- death
- remembrance

### Main-mystery integration

The protagonist sees anomalies in genealogies, childhood memories, birth records, parentage patterns, and family rituals long before understanding the ontological truth.

### Emotional engine

Extremely high.

This direction most directly protects the project’s central emotional thesis:

> A later label cannot retroactively make genuine love unreal.

### Strengths

- strongest character attachment
- strongest emotional recontextualization
- philosophy emerges naturally from love and grief
- gives birth/childhood system narrative importance
- unusual SF focus
- potentially powerful prestige/IP identity

### Main dangers

- slower external momentum
- can feel like connected domestic cases rather than epic SF
- weak exploration unless deliberately built in
- difficult to attract readers who expect immediate action/competence
- mystery can become background too long

### Required safeguards

- clear professional competence
- periodic high-pressure public events
- family cases must change laws/factions/world state
- ship geography must be integrated, not ignored

# Direction D — ARRIVAL CENSUS / SHIP-WORLD EXPEDITION
## “Before arrival, someone has to count who we are.”

### Core engine

The protagonist is part of a shipwide field mission that must **map, verify, classify, negotiate with, or prepare the population and districts for a future civilizational transition**.

“Arrival census” is a design shorthand, not yet canon. The triggering institution might later be a census, settlement certification, demographic audit, infrastructure survey, cultural registry, or other shipwide mandate.

### Repeat loop

`Enter district → discover local social order → perform concrete mission → encounter contradiction → negotiate/solve → district state changes → carry consequence forward`

### 1–3 episode reward

- access gained
- local problem solved
- useful map/record/relationship acquired
- strange custom understood
- specific contradiction answered

### 5–15 episode arc

One district/community becomes a complete local story with:
- distinctive economy
- distinctive family/death culture
- local political conflict
- local interpretation of humanity
- hidden relation to ship history

### 50+ episode durability

Very high because the ship itself becomes a continent.

A sufficiently varied world can support:
- industrial regions
- ecological habitats
- legacy residential sectors
- data-intensive regions
- low-connectivity communities
- prestige administrative centers
- neglected/closed spaces
- transport corridors
- religious/cultural enclaves

None of these examples are canon yet.

### Main-mystery integration

The protagonist discovers that population, medical, historical, and settlement records do not describe one coherent humanity. Each region supplies a different piece of the contradiction.

### Emotional engine

Moderate unless anchored by a strong recurring family/relationship cast.

### Strengths

- best realization of “ship as continent”
- strongest visual/IP geography
- natural social-science SF
- excellent anti-repetition potential
- supports multiple cultures/factions
- easy to vary tone by region
- destination becomes structurally relevant

### Main dangers

- travelogue / tour-of-the-week structure
- protagonist becomes observer rather than driver
- local casts discarded after each district
- census/mission premise may feel bureaucratically contrived
- main mystery could become a scavenger hunt

### Required safeguards

- every district permanently alters shipwide politics or protagonist relationships
- recurring cross-district characters/factions
- protagonist has binding decisions, not just observation duties
- destination pressure must be real
- local arcs must interact after introduction

# Current Recommendation

## Recommended architecture: **D as the macro engine + A as the recurring decision engine + C as the emotional core; B used as periodic pressure, not the default engine.**

This is **not yet a canon selection**. It is the strongest direction to carry into the next comparative stress test.

### Why not choose one pure direction?

- Pure A risks procedural sameness.
- Pure B risks becoming generic disaster/engineering SF.
- Pure C risks insufficient serialization momentum.
- Pure D risks travelogue distance.

The combination creates distinct layers rather than mixing everything every episode:

### Macro layer — D
The ship gradually opens as a continent-scale world.

### Arc decision layer — A
Each major region produces a concrete personhood/continuity/civic problem that must actually be decided.

### Emotional layer — C
The protagonist’s own family and closest relationships turn the abstract consequences personal.

### Event-pressure layer — B
Infrastructure emergencies and physical risks punctuate the series when needed, producing SF competence and visual stakes without turning the story into combat/disaster fiction.

## Critical constraint

Do **not** give the protagonist four jobs.

The eventual protagonist needs **one coherent profession** whose duties naturally touch several systems. The next protagonist-design phase must find that single role.

# Next Stress-Test Questions

Before this recommendation can be promoted from `CANDIDATE` to `PROVISIONAL`, answer:

1. What single profession can plausibly access multiple districts **and** make consequential decisions?
2. Why would this person deal with identity/family issues without becoming an all-purpose plot bureaucrat?
3. What is the 1–3 episode reward loop?
4. What makes the protagonist personally vulnerable to the same rules they enforce?
5. What recurring cast follows them across regions?
6. How do local communities continue affecting the story after their introduction?
7. What destination-related pressure justifies shipwide movement?
8. Can the core engine still work before the “one human” fact becomes public?
9. Can it still work after the fact becomes public?
10. Can it still work after the protagonist’s own identity is known?

If the answer to #8, #9, or #10 is no, the direction is not durable enough for 150–250 episodes.
