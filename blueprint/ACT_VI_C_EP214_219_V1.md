# EP216 — The People Who Were Never a Dependency Field

**Immediate Goal**
Prevent multiple local service failures from cascading after the second wave removes several informal bridge people at once.

**Primary Engine**
E1 Live Execution Crisis.

**Secondary**
E4 Social Dependency / E5.

**External Event**
Several small absences align:

- in H4, two formally separate households lose the same unofficial elder/childcare bridge during overlapping shift transfers,
- an H3 fabrication team loses the technician everyone informally uses to translate old machine behavior into new procedures even though that person is not the official trainer,
- an H5 cargo handoff loses the worker trusted by two teams to resolve schedule ambiguities without escalation,
- local staff who usually absorb short gaps are themselves in transit.

Individually none was marked `mission critical`.
Together they create:
- missed shifts,
- care coverage gaps,
- delayed fabrication handoffs,
- temperature/time-sensitive cargo risk,
- growing transport backlog.

No mass casualty occurs, but continued execution would create real safety risk.

**Decision / Action**
A local H4 coordinator uses discretionary stop authority ambiguously/partially outside the planned central sequence and pauses one branch.

Haren/Tessa then halt the next synchronized wave rather than forcing schedule recovery.

**Result**
The cascade is contained.

**Cost**
The model's schedule/thermal/transport reserve begins burning rapidly; several already-moved groups face extended temporary arrangements.

**Character**
C1/C4 failure lands.
Arun Vey/F7 or equivalent local care-network voice becomes operationally useful, not merely political.

**Theme**
TH-001 hidden care network returns at a more difficult scale; TH-015.

**Reward / Hook**

# EP217 — The Numbers Were Right

**Immediate Goal**
Diagnose why a physically valid, formally dependency-aware plan failed before deciding whether to restart.

**Primary Engine**
E5/E1 Failure Analysis.

**Secondary**
E4 Character.

**External Event**
The review confirms:
- mass was correct,
- heat was correct,
- transport capacity was correct,
- registered care relationships were included,
- medical/accommodation rules were followed.

Failure came from **completeness assumptions**:
- local substitutes made earlier frictions invisible,
- informal roles were not recognized by the people themselves as formal dependencies,
- one person's role changed by context/day,
- trust relationships cannot be inferred solely from titles/households,
- model buffers assumed local interchangeability that did not exist simultaneously across several sites.

**Decision**
Haren rejects the tempting response `we just need more data on everyone`.

Tessa rejects `add bigger generic buffer and keep the same model` as sufficient.

**Result**
The failure is defined as epistemic/organizational, not missing one magical dataset.

**Cost**
The current model cannot simply be patched and restarted immediately.

**Character / Relationship**
R3 deepens through shared ownership rather than blame.

**Theme**
TH-015 master dilemma.

**Continuity**
Anti-omniscient-model theme pays from Act I onward.

**Reward / Hook**
RWD-FACT + RWD-EMO/ACCOUNTABILITY.
HK-DECISION.

**4-point** 4/4.

# EP219 — Leave Room for What the Model Does Not Know

**Immediate Goal**
Restart the staging move under a method that can survive unknown dependencies rather than pretending to catalog them all.

**Primary Engine**
E1 Transition Execution.

**Secondary**
E7/E5.

**External Event**
The revised method adds:
- local pre-wave validation assemblies,
- explicit `unknown dependency` reserve in staffing/transport/time,
- smaller asynchronous wave boundaries,
- local authority to pause/resequence within defined scope,
- live feedback from service/care/work nodes,
- uncertainty ranges instead of false point precision,
- no requirement to expose all private relationships to central planners.

**Decision / Action**
The halted branch restarts under the new rules.

A local team independently pauses/reorders one sequence based on a newly surfaced informal dependency; the system absorbs the change rather than treating it as failure.

**Result**
The move resumes successfully enough to prove the method.

**Cost**
It is slower and less throughput-efficient than the original synchronized optimum.

**Character / Relationship**
Haren/Tessa remain central experts but accept less predictability/control.

**Theme**
TH-015 + TH-008 privacy secondary.

**Continuity**
Direct handoff to ARC36: if local teams can stop/resequence, who holds final authority over irreversible civilization-scale coordination?

**Reward / Hook**
RWD-COMP + RWD-CONSEQ + RWD-CLOSURE.
HK-CLOSURE+NEXT.

# VI-C Rollup

## Failure distinction
- physical model correct
- formal social/medical rules included
- failure = overconfidence in completeness and local interchangeability

## No total-surveillance solution
The answer is not `collect every relationship and monitor everyone more`.

## New execution method
- local validation
- uncertainty reserve
- smaller/asynchronous waves
- local pause/resequence authority
- live feedback
- purpose-limited data

## Character payoff
- Tessa owns model failure
- Haren owns social-model overconfidence
- neither loses all competence
- neither becomes sole savior of the correction

## Handoff
The redesign proves distributed execution works, but it creates a governance question:

> when local teams can stop/resequence and systemwide externalities remain real, who coordinates the irreversible whole?
