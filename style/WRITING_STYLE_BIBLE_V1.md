# Writing & Style Bible v1

Status: PROVISIONAL PREWRITING BIBLE — NO MANUSCRIPT AUTHORIZATION
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

---

# 1. Purpose

This Bible defines **how the eventual novel should deliver the design**.

It does not authorize scene drafting.

Style must protect:
- mystery fairness
- web-novel readability
- character intimacy
- large-scale society
- SF credibility
- non-lecture philosophy

---

# 2. POV Decision

## Preferred working POV

**Close third-person limited, protagonist-dominant.**

Target distribution:
- protagonist POV: roughly 85–90%
- rare secondary POV/interlude: 10–15% maximum

### Why not protagonist-only first person
First person gives strong identity intimacy but makes it harder to show:
- autonomous faction moves
- BH-1 as an independent person
- consequences in habitats protagonist is not currently visiting

It also risks making every world issue feel like protagonist investigation.

### Why not omniscient
Omniscient narration weakens mystery fairness and emotional uncertainty.

### Secondary POV rule
Secondary POV is allowed only when it provides **dramatic consequence**, not hidden-answer exposition.

Best uses:
- BH-1 after reader knows identity or when scene does not unfairly reveal it
- parent/younger sister during a consequence protagonist cannot witness
- faction/local character during public cascade

Do not use POV switches merely to manufacture cliffhangers.

---

# 3. Mystery Fairness POV Rule

The narration may withhold what the POV character does not know.

The narration may **not** deceptively suppress a fact the POV character is consciously thinking about simply to fool the reader.

Forbidden:
- POV character sees diagnostic result, narration says only “the result surprised him” for ten episodes
- mentor POV deliberately thinks around a secret they fully know
- BH-1 POV before reveal internally avoids obvious knowledge they would naturally think

Allowed:
- character possesses an old memory whose significance they do not understand
- professional jargon appears before its historical meaning is known
- character chooses not to tell protagonist, while reader knows only through observable behavior unless that character is POV

---

# 4. Narrative Distance

Default:
**close enough to feel protagonist's reasoning, not so close that every paragraph becomes interior monologue.**

Use three distances:

### Near
- moral choice
- family conflict
- identity shock
- intimate relationship

### Medium
- professional analysis
- negotiation
- social observation

### Far/brief
- large habitat movement
- ship-scale infrastructure
- public consequence montage-like transitions

Do not remain at the same distance for entire episodes.

---

# 5. Core Prose Principle

## Event before explanation

Default sequence:

`problem appears`
→ `character attempts action`
→ `system behaves unexpectedly`
→ `consequence`
→ `only then explain the minimum rule needed`

Avoid:

`five paragraphs explaining system`
→ `event finally starts`

---

# 6. SF Exposition Rule

Every technical explanation must answer at least one current dramatic need:

- what can the character do?
- what can't they do?
- what will it cost?
- why did the plan fail?
- why does this person need different treatment?
- what choice becomes possible/impossible?

If information changes no action, relationship, expectation, or mystery interpretation, move it to design notes, not prose.

---

# 7. Terminology Budget

Per ordinary scene:
- preferably 0–2 unfamiliar technical terms
- rarely 3 if immediately grounded by action

Do not stack:
`technical term + institution name + historical era + faction acronym`
in one sentence unless already familiar.

Design-document IDs such as H4, E7, BH-1 never appear as normal reader-facing prose labels unless there is an in-world reason.

---

# 8. Philosophy Rule

Characters may hold philosophies.

The novel must not become philosophy lecture.

Preferred form:

`two legitimate needs become incompatible`
→ `someone chooses`
→ `someone pays`
→ `reader understands the philosophical question through consequence`

Direct abstract debate is allowed when:
- characters have something concrete at stake
- the decision deadline is real
- dialogue changes the decision

Avoid multi-page conversations whose only function is to explain the theme.

---

# 9. Dialogue Rule

Dialogue should primarily do one or more of:
- negotiate
- evade
- reveal relationship status
- test authority
- request/refuse
- change a plan
- expose conflicting assumptions

Avoid characters asking questions solely so another character can explain the world.

### Voice differentiation
Major cast later receives:
- sentence-length tendency
- directness/evasion style
- preferred metaphor domain
- taboo topic
- humor style
- professional vocabulary limit

No gimmick catchphrases as substitute for voice.

---

# 10. Protagonist Competence Prose

Professional intelligence should be shown through **selection**.

The protagonist notices:
- the one dependency others ignored
- the sequence order that matters
- who actually carries care labor
- which “equivalent” replacement is not equivalent
- which concession creates a second-order problem

Avoid genius narration where everyone else becomes stupid.

Other experts must regularly see things protagonist misses.

---

# 11. Action Without Combat Rule

Action scenes may be:
- live relocation
- gravity transition emergency
- evacuation
- infrastructure handoff
- system repair coordination
- crowd movement
- ecological containment
- physical conversion
- exterior/axial operations
- medical deadline

Write them with:
- clear spatial goal
- clock/constraint
- choice
- irreversible consequence

Do not imitate battle prose unless actual combat occurs.

---

# 12. Violence Rule

Violence may occur, but it is not the default escalation mechanism.

When violence occurs:
- it should reveal social pressure or personal choice
- aftermath matters
- injury/death follow world rules
- no sudden action-hero transformation for protagonist

No routine gunfight inserted because an arc “needs excitement.”

---

# 13. Emotional Writing Rule

Prefer:
- changed behavior
- interrupted routine
- physical distance/proximity
- choice under cost
- what someone stops doing
- what someone does without being asked

over explicit labeling of every emotion.

But do not become so restrained that web-novel readers cannot read the emotional state.

Clarity beats literary obscurity.

---

# 14. Relationship Scene Rule

A meaningful relationship scene must change at least one:
- trust
- knowledge
- obligation
- intimacy
- power
- future decision

A scene that only repeats feelings is cut or merged.

---

# 15. Scene Entry / Exit

## Enter late
Begin near the pressure point rather than with full travel/setup.

## Exit after change
Once the scene's state has changed, leave before explaining the meaning twice.

Travel is shown when:
- geography matters
- gravity/environment changes
- relationship interaction occurs
- transition reveals class/culture

Otherwise compress.

---

# 16. Episode Structure

Each episode should contain at least 3 of 4:

1. **Immediate Goal**
2. **Meaningful Change**
3. **Reward / Answer / Competence payoff**
4. **Forward Pull**

A chapter whose only function is “more questions” fails.

---

# 17. Hook Rotation

Rotate endings among:
- revelation
- decision
- consequence
- emotional shift
- threat
- promise of action
- reversal
- closure-with-new-direction

Avoid repeated:
> “Then the door opened.”
> “There was one more secret.”
> “He froze.”

Within any 10 episodes, pure mystery-question endings should be a minority.

---

# 18. Reward Cadence

### 1–3 episodes
Concrete result:
- solved logistics problem
- relationship movement
- factual answer
- physical change
- successful/failed choice with consequence

### 5–15 episodes
Arc payoff.

### Act
Large permanent change.

Never require 30–50 episodes of trust before paying any meaningful answer.

---

# 19. Description Rule

Ship environments should be described through:
- what residents use
- what workers maintain
- what limits movement
- what smells/sounds/feels different
- what people take for granted

Avoid generic:
- endless silver corridors
- blue holograms everywhere
- sterile white future

Each habitat's visual identity must also reflect labor and history.

---

# 20. Wonder Rule

Wonder is not a lore paragraph.

A wonder beat must be tied to:
- work
- risk
- ritual
- relationship
- discovery
- transition

Examples structurally:
- crossing from rotating habitat to axis during a live job
- engineered season being interrupted by ecological work
- seeing destination during a decision that will consume present resources

---

# 21. Humor / Breathing Rule

Humor should come from:
- professional friction
- family familiarity
- habitat cultural difference
- bureaucracy meeting lived reality
- people being people in impossible circumstances

No Marvel-style quip after every serious moment.

After heavy reveal arcs, include ordinary-life scenes that **still move relationships or consequences**.

---

# 22. Recontextualization Writing Rule

When a major truth changes earlier meaning:

Do not reproduce long flashback summaries.

Prefer:
- a repeated phrase now understood differently
- same location revisited
- familiar medical ritual given new context
- character remembering one short concrete detail

Reader should experience recognition, not be told “this changes everything” repeatedly.

---

# 23. Flashback Rule

Use sparingly.

Historical information usually appears through:
- physical infrastructure
- records used for present action
- testimony
- rituals
- contemporary consequences

Full historical POV flashbacks are exceptional because they risk making dead-era characters more dramatic than current cast.

---

# 24. Tone

Target blend:
- grounded
- curious
- humane
- occasionally unsettling
- visually expansive
- not nihilistic
- not cozy enough to erase danger

The story can be serious without being continuously solemn.

---

# 25. Sentence Rhythm — Working Korean Prose Target

Default:
- medium-short narrative sentences
- concrete subject/action clarity
- longer sentences reserved for layered professional reasoning or wonder
- short sentences used at decisions/impact, not constantly

Avoid:
- excessive noun-stacking
- untranslated English technical clusters
- repeated “~였다. ~였다. ~였다.” monotony
- overuse of ellipses for mystery

Exact style sample cannot be written until prewriting gate permits prose testing.

---

# 26. Episode Length

Do not freeze until publication platform is selected.

Working structural assumption:
A standard Korean web-novel episode should be compact enough to deliver one meaningful state change and reward.

The blueprint tracks beats, not word padding.

No arc may require inflated prose to hit a character-count quota.

---

# 27. Forbidden Style Drift

- status-window language
- game-system notifications
- constant faux-scientific percentages
- protagonist narrating like omniscient philosopher
- every AI issue described with “code/error/program” metaphors
- treating organic seeded citizens as robots in narration
- techno-babble used to hide causal gaps
- cinematic screenplay shorthand replacing prose
