# 1. Cast Design Rule

No major character exists only to:
- deliver clues
- state one philosophy
- love the protagonist
- oppose the protagonist
- explain a technology

Every core character needs:
- independent work/life
- independent desire
- a pressure source not created by protagonist
- at least one decision protagonist dislikes
- a relationship with someone other than protagonist

---

# P0 — PROTAGONIST

## Working demographics
- male
- age 36
- seeded-development citizen
- mid-level field transition specialist

## Job
Habitat transition field specialist.

One-sentence function:
> When part of the ship must be emptied, rebuilt, merged, or repurposed, he keeps the human network functioning while the physical world changes.

## Strength
Sees dependency networks others miss.

## Blind spot
Believes enough modeling can make a fair solution discoverable.

## Coping style
Turns emotional uncertainty into technical/procedural problems.

## Desire
Be trusted with the ship's hardest transitions.

## Fear
Destroying a life/community while being able to prove the decision was procedurally correct.

## Secret
Past unauthorized care/household classification alteration involving younger sister.

## Independent life requirement
Needs:
- one hobby/routine unrelated to work/mystery
- one friendship outside core plot team
- one personal habit inherited from H8 home culture

Do not define him solely through work.

# R1 — MOTHER / HOME ANCHOR

## Working demographics
- woman
- early-to-mid 60s
- seeded-development
- H8 Legacy Habitat resident

## Working occupation
Small neighborhood **garment repair / reuse workshop operator** or equivalent material-repair trade.

Why this works:
- ordinary livelihood
- deeply place- and customer-network dependent
- ship-specific material reuse culture
- not a historian, scientist, politician, or medical expert

## Desire
Keep her neighborhood life viable through transition rather than receive an objectively “better” replacement elsewhere.

## Strength
Knows local people, favors, routines, and informal mutual aid.

## Flaw
Can treat long familiarity as moral entitlement and expect other districts to absorb the cost of preservation.

## Independent relationships
- long-term customers/neighbors
- local friend/rival
- relationship with daughter independent of son

## Main thematic pressure
Place is part of continuity, but place cannot be frozen at unlimited cost.

# R2 — YOUNGER SISTER / FORMER PROTECTED PERSON

## Working demographics
- woman
- age 24–26
- seeded-development
- grew up H8/H4 network

## Working career
**Settlement ecology / environmental systems trainee** tied to H2 and forward destination preparation.

Why this works:
- personally links arrival to family before ACT V
- gives her independent technical competence
- makes her want to leave the home/protection protagonist values
- avoids making her another records/law character

## Desire
Qualify for an early planetary/ecological transition cohort or another high-autonomy future role.

## Flaw
May overcorrect against protection by underestimating real risk or refusing help reflexively.

## Past incident
During an earlier emergency transition, protagonist altered a care/household classification so she could remain inside a trusted caregiving/medical network rather than be reassigned under emergency rules.

Outcome was materially beneficial.

Problem:
He chose for her and altered a durable official history without her meaningful consent.

## Present conflict
She refuses to let “I saved you” become permanent authority over her choices.

## Independent relationships
- training cohort
- mentor outside protagonist
- mother
- possible partner/friend not routed through protagonist

# R4 — FORMER SERIOUS PARTNER / INTIMATE AXIS

## Working demographics
- woman
- mid-to-late 30s
- seeded-development

## Working profession
**Neural rehabilitation / continuity medicine physician**.

Not:
- secret cognition-seed expert
- omniscient AI researcher
- historical conspiracy holder

Current expertise:
- neural injury recovery
- adaptation after reconstruction/restoration
- consent/capture decisions
- body-specific treatment limits

## Why profession works
- embodied identity theme without being a philosopher
- intersects restoration and BH-1 medicine naturally
- can disagree with protagonist professionally
- does not duplicate rival or mentor

## Independent goal
Build/defend a care model that respects patient choice even when institutions need predictable outcomes.

## Relationship wound
Protagonist treated professional duty as a reason not to make a personal future choice.

Her life continued after the relationship ended.
She is not waiting.

## Flaw
Can overprotect patient autonomy in ways that underestimate collective/caregiver burden.

# R5 — MENTOR / SENIOR TRANSITION DIRECTOR

- woman or man, late 50s
- seeded-development

## Job
Senior cross-habitat transition administrator.

## Strength
Understands why procedures exist across unequal communities.

- seeded-development

Habitat-level elected representative / community transition advocate.

## Working demographics
- older man, 70s
- seeded-development
- H8 resident

## Working occupation/history
Retired **local transit dispatcher/operator** or another ordinary infrastructure-adjacent job.

Current role:
Active in a local club/institution under threat from transition.

# BH-1 — SOLE BASELINE HUMAN

## Preferred working demographics
- woman
- early 50s
- baseline-development
- unrelated to protagonist

## Working occupation
Neighborhood **fermentation / food-production cooperative manager**.

## Family
- seeded-development spouse/partner
- one adult or near-adult seeded child

## Independent goals
At least three:
1. keep cooperative viable through transition
2. hand responsibility to next generation on her own timetable
3. preserve medical privacy / avoid becoming institutional case

## Independent flaws
- withholds medical information when she fears institutional attention
- locally protectionist about cooperative/community resources
- may make one bad decision that increases risk for others

## Independent competence payoff
Must solve a real food-production/community problem through expertise before or after identity reveal.

## Decisions that must frustrate allies
At minimum:
- refuses a research request from someone defending her rights
- rejects a political role offered by a sympathetic faction
- chooses a settlement/family future protagonist would not select for her

## Relationship outside protagonist
At least:
- spouse/partner
- child
- coworker/community rival

## Core dramatic function
Tests whether society can recognize an important factual biological difference **without converting difference into ownership or rank**.
