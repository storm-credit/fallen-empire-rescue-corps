# Theme / Dilemma Ledger v1

Status: PROVISIONAL — MACRO PLACEMENT PENDING
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Core Rule

A theme is not a topic characters discuss. A thematic beat exists only when:

1. someone wants something concrete,
2. at least two defensible values conflict,
3. a decision changes someone’s life,
4. the choice has cost and aftermath.

Master question:

> What makes a person human—and who has authority to decide?

Final working answer:

> Provenance can be true without determining moral rank; personhood and civic dignity do not depend on one developmental substrate.

The series must earn this answer by showing situations in which provenance genuinely matters technically, emotionally, or historically.

# TH-001 — HOME VS FUNCTIONAL REPLACEMENT

Question:

If every material function of a home/community can be reproduced elsewhere, what is lost by moving it?

Values:
- system efficiency / survival
- place / memory / informal relationship networks

Primary engines:
- E1 Habitat Transition
- E4 Relationship

Protagonist pressure:
Begins too confident that dependency mapping can preserve continuity.

Final evolution:
A mature transition preserves what people themselves identify as essential while accepting some unavoidable loss.

---

# TH-002 — PROTECTION VS AGENCY

Question:

When is protecting someone another form of controlling them?

Values:
- immediate safety
- informed autonomy

Primary relationship:
R2 younger protected family member.

Protagonist secret:
Past classification falsification protected someone but bypassed their agency.

Final payoff:
Protagonist must not repeat this mistake with BH-1 or whole communities.

---

# TH-003 — RECORD VS PERSON

Question:

When official data contradicts lived relationships, which should govern real rights?

Values:
- predictable law / accountability
- lived care / exceptional reality

Primary engines:
- E2 Claim
- E1 Transition

Danger:
Do not conclude “records bad.” Records protect weak people too.

Final answer:
Institutions need categories, but categories must permit correction and appeal without pretending data equals personhood.

---

# TH-004 — TRUTH VS ANTI-CASTE SAFETY

Question:

Can suppressing accurate origin information be justified if that information repeatedly becomes a caste weapon?

Values:
- truth / historical accountability / medicine
- equality / privacy / anti-discrimination

Historical embodiment:
Continuity Settlement.

Final answer direction:
Neither total erasure nor total public provenance. Truthful, protected provenance without ranked citizenship.

---

# TH-005 — BIRTH ORIGIN VS PARENTHOOD

Question:

Does the mechanism that generated a child’s cognitive architecture change who their parents are?

Values:
- biological/developmental causation
- gestation/care/relationship/intention

Required event types:
- mixed-origin family history
- modern seeded child of BH-1
- guardianship/parentage claim

Final answer:
Origin can be factual without monopolizing parenthood.

---

# TH-006 — CONTINUITY VS DEATH

Question:

How much continuity is enough for society to recognize a restored person as carrying the previous person’s obligations?

Values:
- continuity / rescue from interruption
- finality / consent / uniqueness of embodied life

Rule:
Technology never supplies a metaphysical answer.

Final institutional answer:
RC is unquestionably a person; continuity rights are explicitly governed rather than scientifically declared absolute.

---

# TH-007 — EQUALITY VS UNEQUAL NEED

Question:

If one body requires more resources to receive equal safety, is unequal allocation fair?

Values:
- equal shares
- equal dignity / accommodation
- total survival under scarcity

Potential embodiment:
BH-1’s rarer medical needs; disability/access cases; habitat infrastructure.

Danger:
Do not reduce the debate to cold utilitarian villain vs compassionate hero.

---

# TH-008 — PRIVACY VS PUBLIC CONSEQUENCE

Question:

Can one person keep biological/provenance information private when society believes that information affects constitutional or mission questions?

Values:
- bodily/medical privacy
- public interest / institutional legitimacy

Central embodiment:
BH-1.

Working answer:
Public interest does not imply public ownership of a body. Aggregate/verified facts can be governed without forced personal exposure wherever possible.

---

# TH-009 — PRESERVATION VS INSTRUMENTALIZATION

Question:

Should society deliberately create more baseline humans to preserve biological lineage?

Values:
- biological diversity / historical continuity / redundancy
- reproductive autonomy / child not being a means to an end

Hard rule:
No current individual owes reproduction to civilization.

Working answer:
Preserve capability and voluntary choice, not demographic quotas based on provenance.

---

# TH-010 — FOUNDER DUTY VS LIVING CONSENT

Question:

How much can dead generations obligate people alive centuries later?

Values:
- intergenerational promise / mission continuity
- present autonomy / changed reality

Primary engine:
E8 Arrival.

Final answer:
The mission is inheritance, not commandment. Current society may rewrite means and goals while acknowledging sacrifices that enabled its existence.

---

# TH-011 — SHIP AS VEHICLE VS SHIP AS HOME

Question:

When does infrastructure built as transportation become a world with claims of its own?

Values:
- settlement mission / finite colony mass
- preservation of current cultures/places

Final manifestation:
Some ship structures become settlement; some remain as one founding world.

---

# TH-012 — BIOLOGICAL HUMANITY VS HUMAN DESCENT

Question:

If cultural/familial descendants no longer use baseline human cognitive development, did humanity survive?

Possible answers in-world:
- no, biological humanity mostly ended
- yes, humanity transformed
- a successor people inherited humanity
- question itself improperly ranks personhood

Authorial discipline:
The narrative need not force all characters onto one semantic answer.

Final legal answer:
Rights do not depend on winning this definition.

---

# TH-013 — DIFFERENCE VS HIERARCHY

Question:

Can a civilization openly name real differences without turning them into ranks?

This is the late-series master dilemma.

Historical false binary:
- classify provenance → caste
- erase provenance → equality

Final third path:
- protected truthful provenance
- universal personhood
- anti-discrimination
- consent and purpose limitation for sensitive data

---

# TH-014 — LOVE VS ONTOLOGICAL LABEL

Question:

If nothing about a relationship’s shared history changes except how one person’s mind originated, what exactly becomes false?

Values:
- categorical authenticity
- lived relational authenticity

Required proof:
The relationship must make a costly choice after reclassification. Dialogue alone cannot answer it.

# TH-015 — EXPERTISE VS SELF-DETERMINATION

Question:

Can experts legitimately decide how to preserve a community when residents reject the expert model?

Values:
- system-level knowledge
- local consent / lived knowledge

Primary relationship:
R6 rival.

Final answer:
Neither total technocracy nor total local veto; meaningful representation within physical constraints.

---

# TH-016 — HISTORY VS THE RIGHT TO FORGET

Question:

Do descendants owe preservation of every painful identity category their ancestors tried to abolish?

Values:
- accountability / learning
- privacy / social peace / freedom from inherited labels

Final answer:
Preserve historical evidence while limiting personal provenance exploitation.

# 2. Theme Distribution Rule

Each Sub-Act should carry:

- 1 primary dilemma
- at most 1 meaningful secondary dilemma

Do not make every Arc about “what is human?” in explicit terms.

The master theme should emerge from varied concrete subjects:

- housing
- parenting
- medical triage
- restoration
- privacy
- labor
- archives
- arrival
- reproduction

---

# 3. Theme Escalation Rule

Escalate a thematic question by changing **who pays and what institution must decide**, not by repeating the same debate with higher stakes.

Example:

`TH-003 record vs person`

1. one household relocation
2. one guardianship dispute
3. habitat policy
4. provenance reclassification
5. constitutional personhood architecture

Each stage must introduce a new consequence.

---

# 4. Anti-Lecture QA

For every major thematic scene/episode later, answer:

- concrete goal?
- decision deadline?
- two defensible values?
- visible cost?
- relationship/institution changed?
- could the theme be understood if all explicit philosophical dialogue were removed?

If the last answer is no, redesign.
