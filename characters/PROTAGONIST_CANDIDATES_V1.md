# Protagonist Candidates v1

Status: COMPARATIVE DESIGN — NOT FROZEN
Project: 《우주선에는 인간이 한 명뿐이다》

## 1. Protagonist Requirements

The protagonist must not be a generic truth-seeking detective.

The selected role must provide:

- repeatable competence pleasure
- legitimate movement across multiple habitats
- concrete authority/responsibility but not omnipotent access
- reasons to enter ordinary homes and institutions
- contact with family, law, records, infrastructure, and social conflict
- decisions that create consequences
- usefulness before and after the main truth is public
- vulnerability to the same rules they apply to others

The role should be understandable in one sentence.

# P1 — HABITAT TRANSITION OFFICER
## Working Korean concept: 생활권 전환관 / 구획 이주조정관

### One-sentence job

> When an aging part of the ship must be emptied, rebuilt, merged, or repurposed, this person figures out **how to move people without destroying the life that made them a community**.

### Why the job exists

A centuries-old ship cannot keep every residential/industrial section open continuously.

Major maintenance cycles require:

- evacuation
- temporary relocation
- household verification
- school/clinic continuity
- business compensation
- access-right changes
- cultural-site preservation
- resource reallocation

Approaching destination preparation could later increase this work, but the profession exists even without arrival pressure.

### Competence pleasure

The protagonist is good at seeing **dependency networks** other people miss.

Example skill types:

- move one clinic instead of 400 patients
- keep a school cohort intact to prevent family collapse
- identify that a “redundant” transit stop actually sustains elder care
- resolve household claims that databases misrepresent
- redesign a relocation sequence so industry, food, and childcare continue functioning

This creates a non-combat “smart professional” engine.

### Natural story access

- homes
- families
- schools
- clinics
- markets
- local councils
- old infrastructure
- archives
- maintenance teams
- legal disputes

### Mystery access

Population and household systems can contain contradictions without making the protagonist an investigator by profession.

They discover truth because **bad classifications make real relocation decisions fail**.

### Agency

High.

The protagonist must sign/approve/deny plans that have visible consequences.

### Weakness / false belief candidate

> If records are accurate and procedures are fair, conflicting lives can be reconciled.

The story gradually demonstrates that categories themselves can be wrong.

### Personal secret candidate

Years earlier, the protagonist falsified one household classification during an emergency to keep a vulnerable person with the only caregiver who could protect them.

Nobody was harmed, but the act would destroy their career if discovered.

This means the protagonist already knows the law can fail human relationships, while publicly defending the system.

### Biggest strength

Best integration of D macro exploration + A civic decisions + C family stakes + occasional B crisis.

### Biggest danger

The profession could sound bureaucratic or invented-for-plot unless the maintenance/relocation economy is made concrete and familiar.

### Score

- anti-detective: 10

# 2. Recommended Protagonist Role

## P1 — Habitat Transition Officer

Promote to **PROVISIONAL WORKING ROLE**, not FROZEN.

### Why

It best solves the project-specific problem rather than the generic SF problem.

The protagonist’s job is fundamentally about:

> **What has to remain connected for a human life to remain a life when the physical world around it is being rearranged?**

That question mirrors the main ontology without sounding philosophical.

When later told that almost everyone is artificial, the protagonist has already spent years making decisions based on a practical principle:

> A life is more than the body occupying one address.

This creates thematic irony and character growth.

# 3. Protagonist Starting Position — Working Model

## Social position

Mid-level professional, respected for competence but not elite.

High enough to make decisions.

Low enough to be overruled.

Known across departments but not part of ruling leadership.

## Strength

Can understand a community as a system of people, infrastructure, obligations, and informal care.

## Weakness

Uses categories to make complexity manageable and underestimates how categories can erase people.

## Desire

Become the person trusted with the hardest transition projects; prove that orderly systems can prevent needless loss.

## Need

Accept that legitimate human/personhood value sometimes exists **before** the system has language to recognize it.

## Fear

Making a decision that destroys a family/community while believing it was “correct.”

## Hidden hypocrisy

The protagonist already broke classification rules once to protect a real relationship.

## Why they matter

Not because they are the chosen human.

Because their profession places them exactly where the civilization’s definitions become material:

- who belongs to a household
- who has relocation rights
- whose identity continues
- which community counts as one unit
- what must be preserved when something is dismantled

# 4. Must-Pass Test Before Freeze

The role is rejected if:

- every plot requires expanding the protagonist’s legal authority
- they become a disguised police detective
- relocation is only an excuse to visit new locations
- there is no satisfying 1–3 episode competence reward
- the job becomes irrelevant after the main truth is public
- the job is too bureaucratic to dramatize visually

A future pilot-blueprint test should prove that the role can carry at least 20 episodes **without using the main human/AI reveal as fuel**.
